# Research Gaps & Emerging Methodologies (2024-2025 Update)

This document consolidates research gaps identified in the wiki's open questions, supplemented with emerging methodologies and discoveries from 2024-2025 archaeological literature.

---

## Emerging Methodologies (2024-2025)

### 1. Ancient Protein Analysis (Paleoproteomics)
**Key Advances:**
- **Shotgun proteomics** on stone tools and ceramics - identifying species-specific proteins from microscopic residues
- **Protein preservation** in ceramics and lithics - new extraction protocols optimized for different material types
- **Species identification** from fragmentary remains (e.g., Denisovan mandible from Baishya Karst Cave via collagen fingerprinting)
- **Disease detection** - combining ancient proteins with DNA to study immune response to mycobacterial diseases (tuberculosis, leprosy)
- **Food residue analysis** - metaproteomics on ceramics revealing ancient foodways (milk, meat, plant proteins)

**Impact on Wiki Topics:**
- Stone tool function analysis (protein residues on scrapers, points)
- Pottery use (dairy, meat, plant processing)
- Dental calculus analysis (diet, medicinal plants)
- Birch tar hafting adhesives (Neolithic tool hafting)

**Key References (2024-2025):**
- Science Advances review on ancient protein analysis (2024)
- ScienceDirect metaproteomics review (March 2025)
- Royal Society Proceedings B - ancient DNA + biomarkers from Neolithic birch tar artefacts (Oct 2025)

---

### 2. Ancient DNA + Multi-Proxy Approaches
**Key Advances:**
- **Combined aDNA + organic residue analysis** on artefacts (e.g., 30 birch tar artefacts from 9 Alpine Neolithic sites)
- **Sedimentary ancient DNA (sedaDNA)** from cave sediments - detecting human and animal presence without bones
- **Y-chromosome + mtDNA** refining Neolithic diffusion models (demic vs. cultural diffusion)
- **Pathogen DNA** from dental calculus and skeletal remains

**Impact on Wiki Topics:**
- Neolithic farmer migrations (Europe, Asia)
- Disease evolution and prehistoric medicine
- Birch tar production and hafting technology
- Population movements and technology diffusion

**Key References (2025):**
- Royal Society Proceedings B - aDNA + biomarkers from birch tar (Oct 2025)
- Molecular genetic investigation of Carpathian Basin Neolithic populations (2025)

---

### 3. Residue Analysis on Stone Tools & Ceramics
**Key Advances:**
- **DESI-MS (Desorption Electrospray Ionization Mass Spec)** - in situ protein detection on stone tools
- **Lipid biomarkers** - distinguishing dairy, meat, plant processing
- **Microwear + residue combined** - functional interpretation of tool use
- **Starch grain + phytolith analysis** - plant processing on grinding stones

**Impact on Wiki Topics:**
- Stone tool function verification (butchery, plant processing, hide working)
- Pottery function (dairy vs. meat vs. plant)
- Grinding stone use (wild vs. domesticated grains)
- Hafting adhesives identification

---

### 4. Microarchaeology & Micromorphology
**Key Advances:**
- **Thin-section micromorphology** of site sediments - identifying activity areas, hearths, flooring
- **FTIR spectroscopy** - detecting controlled fire use, heating temperatures
- **Phytolith + starch analysis** in sediment - plant use in absence of macrobotanicals

**Impact on Wiki Topics:**
- Fire control and pyrotechnology (Gesher Benot Ya'aqov ~790 ka)
- Household activity areas in Neolithic longhouses
- Cave occupation patterns

**Key References:**
- Goren-Inbar et al. 2004 (Gesher Benot Ya'aqov controlled fire ~790 ka)
- Micromorphology of Neolithic longhouses (ongoing)

---

### 5. Maritime & Coastal Archaeology
**Key Advances:**
- **Underwater archaeology** - submerged Pleistocene landscapes
- **Isotope analysis** of marine shells - sea level, seasonality, trade
- **Boat/ship reconstruction** from waterlogged remains

**Impact on Wiki Topics:**
- Polynesian navigation and voyaging
- Coastal resource exploitation
- Early seafaring in Southeast Asia (40,000+ years ago)
- Obsidian and shell trade networks

**Key Finding (2024):**
- Southeast Asia advanced seafaring 40,000+ years ago - earlier than previously thought
