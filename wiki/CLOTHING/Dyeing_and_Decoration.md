# Dyeing and Decoration

Textile coloration and decoration transformed functional cloth into expressions of identity, status, and artistry. From Paleolithic ochre to Bronze Age Tyrian purple, dye technologies reflect trade networks, chemical knowledge, and social complexity. [1]

## Overview

Dyeing is the process of imparting color to fibers, yarns, or fabrics using dyes that form chemical bonds with the substrate. Decoration includes resist techniques, printing, embroidery, and applied ornamentation.

## Mordants (Fixatives)

| Mordant | Chemical | Effect | Period |
|---------|----------|--------|--------|
| **Alum** | KAl(SO₄)₂·12H₂O | Brightens, most colors | Neolithic+ |
| **Iron** | FeSO₄ | Saddens, greys, blacks | Bronze Age+ |
| **Tin** | SnCl₂ | Brightens reds/yellows | Medieval+ |
| **Copper** | CuSO₄ | Greens, improves fastness | Bronze Age+ |
| **Tannin** | Plant galls | Pre-mordant for cellulose | Ancient |

## Dyeing Techniques

### Vat Dyeing (Indigo/Woad)
1. **Reduction**: Anaerobic fermentation removes oxygen [2]
2. **Leuco form**: Dye soluble, colorless/yellow
3. **Immersion**: Fiber absorbs leuco dye
4. **Oxidation**: Air exposure → blue precipitate

### Direct Dyeing
- Substantive dyes (no mordant): Turmeric, safflower, some barks [3]
- Hot water extraction, fiber simmered

### Mordant Dyeing
1. **Pre-mordant**: Fiber treated with mordant
2. **Dye bath**: Mordanted fiber + dye
3. **Post-mordant**: Modifier after dyeing

## Resist & Pattern Techniques

### Tie-Dye / Binding (Ancient)
- **Shibori** (Japan): Stitch, bind, fold, clamp
- **Bandhani** (India): Tiny tied dots
- **Tritik** (SE Asia): Stitch resist

### Batik / Wax Resist (Indonesia, Africa, China)
- **Hot wax**: Applied with tjanting/cap
- **Dye**: Wax prevents penetration
- **Remove**: Boiling water/ironing

### Ikat (Warp/Weft Resist)
- **Warp ikat**: Threads resist-dyed before weaving [4]
- **Weft ikat**: More difficult, precise
- **Double ikat**: Both warp and weft (Patola, Bali)

### Printing
- **Block printing**: Carved wood stamps (India, China, Egypt) [5]
- **Screen printing**: Silk mesh (modern)
- **Stencil**: Cut patterns (Japan katazome)

## Embroidery & Applied Decoration

### Stitches
- **Chain stitch**: Oldest (China, Egypt)
- **Stem/outline**: Linear
- **Satin**: Fill areas
- **Couching**: Laid threads secured
- **Goldwork**: Metal threads (Gold, silver)

### Appliqué & Patchwork
- **Appliqué**: Cut shapes sewn on ground
- **Reverse appliqué**: Mola (Kuna, Panama)
- **Patchwork**: Geometric pieces joined

### Beadwork & Sequins
- **Beads**: Shell, stone, glass, metal
- **Sequins**: Metal, plastic (modern)
- **Quillwork**: Porcupine quills (Native American)

## Archaeological Evidence

### Preserved Dyed Textiles
- **Hallstatt** (Austria): Iron Age, madder, woad, weld
- **Pazyryk** (Altai): 4th c. BCE, madder, indigo, cochineal(?)
- **Egyptian tombs**: Linen, madder, indigo, safflower
- **Tarim mummies** (China): 2000 BCE, plaid twill, madder/indigo
- **Nubia**: Medieval, indigo, madder, woad

### Dye Analysis
- **HPLC-MS**: Identifies dye compounds
- **Raman spectroscopy**: Non-destructive
- **FTIR**: Functional groups

### Dye Workshops
- **Pompeii**: Fulling/dyeing vats
- **Tyrian purple**: Murex shell middens (Phoenician sites)
- **Indigo vats**: Archaeological features
