# Weaving

Weaving is the interlacing of two sets of threads (warp and weft) at right angles to create fabric. It represents one of humanity's most transformative technologies, enabling the production of textiles for clothing, shelter, storage, and trade. [1]

## Overview

The invention of weaving transformed human societies by enabling the production of flexible, durable cloth from plant and animal fibers. From the simple ground loom to the complex drawloom, weaving technology evolved in parallel with social complexity.

## Loom Types

### Ground Loom (Earliest)
- **Structure**: Warp threads staked horizontally to ground [2]
- **Region**: Near East, Egypt, North Africa
- **Period**: Neolithic (~7,000 BCE)
- **Limitations**: Fixed tension, limited width

### Warp-Weighted Loom (Neolithic Europe/Near East)
- **Structure**: Vertical frame, warp weighted by clay/stone weights [3]
- **Advantages**: Portable, adjustable tension, unlimited length
- **Archaeology**: Loom weights = primary evidence
- **Period**: ~6,000 BCE – Medieval

### Vertical Two-Beam Loom (Near East/Egypt)
- **Structure**: Two horizontal beams, tensioned warp [4]
- **Advantages**: Faster weaving, consistent tension
- **Period**: Bronze Age onward

### Backstrap Loom (Americas/SE Asia/China)
- **Structure**: Warp tensioned between fixed point and weaver's body
- **Advantages**: Extremely portable, minimal materials
- **Period**: Ancient to present

### Horizontal Treadle Loom (Medieval Europe/China)
- **Structure**: Foot pedals (treadles) operate heddles
- **Advantages**: Hands free for shuttle, faster production
- **Period**: Medieval (Europe), Han Dynasty (China)

## Weave Structures

| Structure | Description | Period/Region |
|-----------|-------------|---------------|
| **Tabby/Plain** | Over-one-under-one | Universal, earliest |
| **Basket** | Groups of threads together | Neolithic |
| **Twill** | Diagonal ribs (2/1, 2/2, 3/1) | Bronze Age Europe/China |
| **Satin** | Long floats, smooth surface | Medieval/China |
| **Gauze/Leno** | Twisted warp pairs | Bronze Age |
| **Pile** | Loops/cut loops for texture | Ancient Peru/Egypt |
| **Tapestry** | Weft-faced, discontinuous weft | Bronze Age Near East |

## Archaeological Evidence

- **Loom weights**: Clay/stone disks, pyramids (Neolithic Europe)
- **Spindle whorls**: Clay, stone, bone (universal)
- **Shuttles**: Wood, bone (rarely preserved)
- **Beaters/swords**: Wood, bone
- **Textile impressions**: On pottery, clay
- **Pseudomorphs**: Textile corrosion on metal
- **Preserved textiles**: Bog bodies, salt mines, arid caves

## Regional Traditions

### Near East
- Linen (flax) dominant, wool increasing in Bronze Age
- Palace workshops (Ur, Mari, Knossos)
- Standardized thread counts, quality grades

### Europe
- Warp-weighted loom standard until Medieval
- Wool dominant in North, linen in South
- Hallstatt salt mines: exceptional preservation

### China
- Silk (sericulture) from Neolithic
- Hemp, ramie for commoners
- Drawloom for complex patterns (Han+)

### Americas
- Backstrap loom universal
- Cotton (Mesoamerica/Andes), camelid fiber (Andes)
- No silk, wool, or linen pre-contact

### Andes
- Camelid fiber (llama, alpaca, vicuña)
- Four-selvaged weaving (no cutting)
- Complex techniques: double cloth, discontinuous warp

## Social Organization

- **Gender**: Predominantly female (ethnographic, iconographic)
- **Household production**: Basic needs
- **Attached specialists**: Palace/temple workshops (Bronze Age)
- **Trade**: Textiles as currency, tribute, prestige goods
