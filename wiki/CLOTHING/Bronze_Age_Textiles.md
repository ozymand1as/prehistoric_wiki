# Bronze Age Textiles

The Bronze Age sees textiles become a major economic commodity, with specialized production, long-distance trade, and complex dye industries. Wool dominates in temperate zones, linen in the Mediterranean, and silk emerges in China. [1]

## Overview

Bronze Age textiles are characterized by: systematic wool economies, professional weavers attached to palaces/temples, advanced dye technology, standardized weights/measures, and textiles as prestige goods and currency. Textual records (Linear B, cuneiform) provide unprecedented detail.

## Materials & Fibers

### Wool (Dominant in Europe/Near East)
- **Sheep improvement**: Selection for woolly fleece (vs. hairy wild ancestors) [2]
- **Breeds**: Regional variation emerging; fat-tailed, fine-wool, long-wool
- **Shearing**: Bronze shears appear ~2,000 BCE (before: plucking/molting collection)
- **Yield**: 1-3 kg/fleece; processed into 1-2 kg clean wool

### Linen (Mediterranean/Near East)
- **Flax**: Continued cultivation, improved retting/processing [3]
- **Quality grades**: Fine (royal/elite), medium, coarse (sails, sacks)
- **Egypt**: Linen dominance, massive temple production, mummy wrappings

### Silk (China - Late Bronze Age)
- **Sericulture**: Domesticated silkworm (Bombyx mori) ~3,000 BCE [4]
- **Reeling**: Filament unwound from cocoon (unique to silk)
- **Early finds**: Yangshao culture (~3,000 BCE), Shang tombs (~1,200 BCE)
- **Trade**: Not yet major export (Silk Road = Iron Age/Han)

### Other Fibers
- **Hemp**: Northern Europe, China, Central Asia (rope, coarse cloth) [5]
- **Nettle**: Continuing wild harvest
- **Cotton**: Indus Valley (major), limited Near East
- **Camelid fiber**: Andes (llama, alpaca, vicuña) - independent tradition

## Production Organization

### Palace/Temple Workshops (Near East, Aegean)
- **Attached specialists**: Full-time weavers, spinners, dyers, finishers [6]
- **Rationed workers**: Paid in grain, oil, wool allocations (Linear B tablets)
- **Standardization**: Fixed warp counts, thread counts, dimensions
- **Scale**: Hundreds of workers at Knossos, Pylos, Ur, Mari

### Household Production (Europe, Periphery)
- **Domestic**: Basic needs met at home
- **Surplus**: Sold/traded at local markets
- **Gendered**: Women's work (spinning especially)

### Nomadic Production (Steppes, Central Asia)
- **Felt**: Non-woven, portable, essential for yurts/clothing [7]
- **Pile weaving**: Early knotted carpets (Pazyryk ~400 BCE, earlier inferred)
- **Trade**: Felt, wool, carpets to settled zones

## Weaving Technology Advances

### Looms
- **Warp-weighted loom**: Continued standard (Europe, Near East)
- **Two-beam vertical loom**: Near East, Egypt (fixed frame, tension on both beams)
- **Ground loom**: Egypt, Near East (horizontal, pegged)
- **Backstrap loom**: Americas, SE Asia, China (body-tensioned)
- **Drawloom precursors**: Pattern weaving (China, Near East late BA)

### Complex Weaves
- **Twill** (2/2, 2/1): Diagonal ribs, drapes better, warmer
- **Diamond twill**: Patterned, status marker (Hallstatt, Nordic BA)
- **Tabby (plain weave)**: Continued majority
- **Basket weave**: Variants for mats, heavy fabric
- **Tapistry/Slit tapestry**: Pictorial (Egypt, Near East)
- **Gauze/Leno**: Open, stable (China, Egypt)

## Dye Technology (Major Development)

| Dye | Source | Color | Region/Period |
|-----|--------|-------|---------------|
| **Madder** | Rubia tinctorum root | Red, orange | Near East ~3,000 BCE, Europe BA |
| **Woad** | Isatis tinctoria leaf | Blue | Europe BA, Near East |
| **Indigo** | Indigofera tinctoria | Deep blue | India BA, later West |
| **Weld** | Reseda luteola | Yellow | Europe BA |
| **Safflower** | Carthamus tinctorius | Yellow, red | Egypt, Near East |
| **Purple (Tyrian)** | Murex snails | Purple | Phoenicia ~1,500 BCE, elite only |
| **Kermes/Cochineal** | Scale insects | Scarlet | Near East, Americas (later) |
| **Tannins + Iron** | Oak galls + iron | Black, grey | Universal |

### Mordants
- **Alum** (aluminum potassium sulfate) - Most common, brightens colors
- **Iron** (ferrous sulfate) - Saddens, blacks, greys
- **Tannin** - Plant galls, bark - Pre-mordant for cellulose
- **Urine/Ammonia** - pH modifier, woad reduction

## Textile Trade & Economy

### Major Trade Items
- **Wool textiles**: Mesopotamia → Anatolia, Aegean, Iran
- **Linen**: Egypt → Levant, Aegean
- **Dyed cloth**: "Garments of many colors" as diplomatic gifts
- **Raw wool**: Flock movements, tribute
- **Finished garments**: Elite exchange, royal wardrobes

### Textiles as Currency
- **Standardized units**: "Talent" of wool, "piece" of cloth
- **Temple accounts**: Wool/cloth as accounting units (cuneiform)
- **Tribute**: Cloth demanded from vassals (Amarna letters, Hittite treaties)

## Regional Highlights

### Aegean (Minoan/Mycenaean, ~2,000-1,200 BCE)
- **Linear B tablets**: Detailed wool/cloth inventories, worker rations
- **Frescoes**: Elaborate patterned clothing (women's flounced skirts, men's kilts)
- **Dyes**: Purple, saffron, madder, woad
- **Trade**: Textiles for metals, ivory, ostrich eggs

### Central Europe (Únětice → Tumulus → Urnfield, ~2,300-800 BCE)
- **Hallstatt salt mines** (800-400 BCE but BA roots): Exceptional preservation
- **Diamond twill wool**: High-status, complex patterns
- **Women's graves**: Spindle whorls, loom weights, textile tools as grave goods
- **Trade**: Wool textiles for tin, copper, amber

### Nordic Bronze Age (~1,700-500 BCE)
- **Oak coffin burials**: Preserved wool garments (skirt, cape, belt, hairnet)
- **Patterned weaving**: Check, stripe, diamond twill
- **Felt**: Caps, shoe linings
- **Trade**: Amber for Mediterranean goods (including textiles)

### Near East (Mesopotamia, Anatolia, Levant)
- **Cuneiform texts**: Thousands of textile records
- **Royal workshops**: Hundreds of women, children producing tons of cloth
- **Standards**: Thread counts, dimensions regulated
- **Colors**: Red (madder), blue (woad/indigo), yellow (weld), purple (royal)

### China (Erlitou → Shang, ~1,900-1,000 BCE)
- **Silk**: Elite monopoly, ritual use
- **Hemp/Ramie**: Commoners' cloth
- **Pattern looms**: Early drawloom evidence (Shang)
- **Embroidery**: Chain stitch, couching on silk

## Preservation & Evidence

### Exceptional Preservation
- **Hallstatt/Dürrnberg salt mines** (Austria): 3,000+ textile fragments
- **Oak coffin burials** (Denmark): Egtved Girl, Skrydstrup Woman, Borum Eshøj
- **Tarim Basin mummies** (China): Wool, felt, twill, plaid patterns (2,000 BCE)
- **Pazyryk kurgans** (Altai, ~400 BCE): Pile carpet, embroidery, felt
- **Egyptian tombs**: Linen wrappings, garments, ritual cloths

### Indirect Evidence
- **Tools**: Bronze shears, needles, spindle whorls, loom weights
- **Impressions**: On clay sealings, pottery
- **Pseudomorphs**: Textile corrosion on bronze (swords, jewelry)
- **Iconography**: Frescoes, figurines, cylinder seals, stelae
- **Texts**: Linear B, cuneiform, oracle bones, Egyptian records
