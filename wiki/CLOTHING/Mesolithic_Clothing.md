# Mesolithic Clothing

Mesolithic clothing represents a transitional phase between Paleolithic tailored hide garments and Neolithic woven textiles. Evidence suggests continued reliance on animal hides with increasing use of plant fibers and decorative elements. [1]

## Overview

The Mesolithic (~12,000–6,000 BCE) saw climate warming, forest expansion, and new subsistence strategies. Clothing adapted to temperate forest environments with lighter, more versatile garments while maintaining hide-working traditions.

## Materials

### Animal Products
- **Red deer, roe deer**: Primary hide sources (Europe) [2]
- **Seal, fish skins**: Coastal/Arctic groups
- **Bird skins**: Feathers for decoration, insulation
- **Antler, bone**: Tools, fasteners, ornaments

### Plant Fibers (Increasing)
- **Nettle (Urtica dioica)**: Fine bast fiber, widespread [3]
- **Lime/basswood (Tilia)**: Inner bark, flexible
- **Willow, oak bast**: Coarser fibers
- **Grasses, reeds**: Twining, plaiting

## Garment Types (Inferred)

### Upper Body
- **Capes/cloaks**: Hide or woven, fastened at neck [4]
- **Shirts/tunics**: Pullover, hide or plant fiber
- **Vests**: Sleeveless, layered

### Lower Body
- **Leggings**: Separate, tied to belt
- **Loincloths**: Minimal, warm periods
- **Trousers**: Rare, possibly hide

### Footwear
- **Moccasins**: Single-piece hide, soft sole
- **Sandals**: Plant fiber, wooden sole
- **Boots**: Hide, fur-lined (colder regions)

### Headgear
- **Caps**: Fur, skin, woven
- **Hoods**: Attached to capes

## Technology

### Hide Processing
- **Brain tanning**: Continued from Paleolithic
- **Smoke tanning**: Water resistance
- **Scrapers**: Microlithic, hafted
- **Awls**: Bone, antler for sewing

### Plant Fiber Processing
- **Retting**: Dew/water for nettle, lime
- **Twining**: Two-ply cordage (no loom evidence)
- **Netting**: Knotless netting (fish traps, bags)
- **Plaiting**: Flat braids (belts, straps)

### Sewing
- **Needles**: Bone, antler (smaller than Paleolithic)
- **Thread**: Sinew, plant fiber cordage
- **Stitches**: Running, whip, overcast

## Archaeological Evidence

### Direct
- **Ötzi** (3,300 BCE, late Mesolithic/Neolithic transition): Complete outfit
- **Tyrol** (Austria): Grass cape, leather leggings, bear hat
- **Star Carr** (UK, ~9,000 BCE): Antler frontlets (headdresses)

### Indirect
- **Microliths**: Hafted as knives, scrapers
- **Bone/antler points**: Awls, needles
- **Perforated shells/teeth**: Sewn on garments
- **Ochre**: Body/garment decoration

### Figurative
- **Rock art** (Spain, Scandinavia): Human figures with clothing
- **Engraved plaquettes**: Schematic dressed figures

## Regional Variations

### Northern Europe (Maglemosian, Kongemose, Ertebølle)
- **Environment**: Temperate forest, coast
- **Materials**: Red deer, seal, fish skin, nettle
- **Ornaments**: Perforated shells, amber, tooth pendants

### Southern Europe (Sauveterrian, Castelnovian)
- **Environment**: Mediterranean, warmer
- **Materials**: Lighter hides, plant fibers
- **Less fur, more woven elements**

### Near East (Natufian, ~12,500–9,500 BCE)
- **Transition to sedentism**: Early villages
- **Materials**: Gazelle, wild sheep/goat hides
- **Early plant fiber**: Flax (wild), reed
- **Ornaments**: Dentalium shells, stone beads
