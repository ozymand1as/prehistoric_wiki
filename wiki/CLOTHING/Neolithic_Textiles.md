# Neolithic Textiles

The Neolithic Revolution brought agriculture, sedentism, and the systematic cultivation of fiber plants and domestication of wool-bearing animals. This period sees the transition from hide-based clothing to woven textiles as a major technological and economic development. [1]

## Overview

Neolithic textiles represent the birth of true cloth production. Flax cultivation begins in the Near East (~8,000 BCE), sheep are domesticated for wool (~9,000 BCE), and the warp-weighted loom becomes the standard weaving technology across Europe and the Near East.

## Fiber Sources

### Plant Fibers
- **Flax (Linum usitatissimum)** - Primary Near Eastern/European fiber; domesticated ~8,000 BCE [2]
- **Hemp (Cannabis sativa)** - East Asia early, Europe later; coarse, durable
- **Nettle (Urtica dioica)** - Wild-harvested, fine fiber, widespread Europe/Asia
- **Tree bast (lime, willow, oak)** - Non-cultivated, cordage and coarse cloth
- **Cotton (Gossypium spp.)** - Indus Valley (~6,000 BCE), Mesoamerica, Peru (later Neolithic/Chalcolithic)

### Animal Fibers
- **Sheep wool** - Domesticated ~9,000 BCE; initially hairy, woolly breeds selected later [3]
- **Goat hair/cashmere** - Coarse outer, fine inner; separate uses
- **Cattle hair** - Minor, coarse applications

## Textile Tools (Archaeological Signature)

| Tool | Material | Function | First Appearance |
|------|----------|----------|------------------|
| **Spindle whorl** | Clay, stone, bone | Weight for drop spinning | ~7,000 BCE (Near East) |
| **Loom weight** | Clay, stone | Tension warp threads on warp-weighted loom | ~6,000 BCE |
| **Shuttle** | Wood, bone | Carry weft through shed | Inferred (rarely preserved) |
| **Beater/Sword** | Wood, bone | Beat weft into place | Inferred |
| **Needle** | Bone, copper (late) | Sewing, netting | Paleolithic continuity |
| **Comb/Pick** | Bone, wood | Separate warp threads | Inferred |

## Weaving Technology: Warp-Weighted Loom

### Structure
- **Frame**: Two uprights, crossbeam (cloth beam), leaning against wall [4]
- **Warp**: Vertical threads tied to loom weights at bottom
- **Weights**: Fired clay (discoid, pyramidal), stone; 100-500g each
- **Shed**: Created by heddle rod + shed rod (counter-shed)
- **Weft**: Inserted horizontally, beaten up with sword/beater

### Advantages
- Simple construction, portable
- Unlimited cloth length (roll onto cloth beam)
- Adaptable width (add/remove weights)
- Suitable for wool, flax, hemp

### Archaeological Identification
- **Rows of loom weights** in situ = loom position
- **Weight clusters** near walls = weaving areas
- **Spindle whorl concentrations** = spinning areas [5]

## Regional Developments

### Near East (Earliest)
- **PPNA/PPNB** (9,500-7,000 BCE): Flax cultivation, spindle whorls
- **Çatalhöyük** (7,100 BCE): Textile tools, wall paintings of clothing
- **Ubaid/Uruk** (5,500-3,100 BCE): Wool economy, temple textile production, early administration

### Europe (LBK onwards)
- **LBK** (5,500 BCE): Flax, spindle whorls, loom weights standard
- **Alpine Lake Dwellings** (4,000-800 BCE): Exceptional preservation - textiles, tools, garments
- **Vinca Culture** (5,500-4,500 BCE): Advanced textiles, copper needles

### East Asia
- **Yangshao** (5,000-3,000 BCE): Hemp, silk beginnings, spindle whorls
- **Jōmon** (10,000+ BCE): Early plant fiber cords, netting, no loom evidence

### South Asia
- **Mehrgarh** (7,000 BCE): Cotton earliest evidence, spindle whorls
- **Indus Valley** (3,300 BCE): Cotton textiles major export

## Textile Types & Quality

| Type | Fiber | Technique | Use |
|------|-------|-----------|-----|
| **Fine linen** | Flax | Tight plain weave | Elite clothing, wrappings |
| **Coarse linen** | Flax/hemp | Loose weave | Work clothes, sacks |
| **Wool tabby** | Wool | Plain weave | Everyday clothing |
| **Wool twill** | Wool | 2/2 twill (late Neolithic) | Durable garments |
| **Nettle cloth** | Nettle | Plain weave | Fine summer wear |
| **Mixed** | Flax+wool | Various | Economy, properties |

## Social & Economic Role

- **Gendered labor**: Spinning/weaving predominantly female (ethnographic analogy, figurines)
- **Household production**: Basic needs met at household level
- **Surplus/Trade**: Finer cloths exchanged; flax/wool as commodities
- **Temple/Palace workshops** (Late Neolithic/Chalcolithic): Attached specialists, standardized production
- **Status markers**: Fine linens, dyed wool, imported fibers

## Preservation & Evidence

### Exceptional Sites
- **Alpine Lake Dwellings** (Switzerland, Germany, Italy): Waterlogged preservation - complete garments, tools
- **Çatalhöyük** (Turkey): Charred fragments, tool assemblages, wall art
- **Must Farm** (UK, ~900 BCE but Neolithic tradition): Bronze Age but shows continuity
- **Pebble beds** (Denmark): Textile impressions in clay

### Indirect Evidence
- **Tool kits**: Spindle whorls, loom weights, needles
- **Impressions**: On clay, pottery, daub
- **Pseudomorphs**: Metal corrosion preserving textile structure (bronze age+)
- **Iconography**: Figurines, wall paintings, seal impressions
