# American Textiles

The Americas developed independent textile traditions based on cotton, camelid fibers, and plant fibers, with no sheep, wool, flax, or silk before European contact. Andean and Mesoamerican traditions achieved exceptional technical and artistic sophistication.

## Overview

American textiles represent completely independent invention of spinning, weaving, and dyeing technologies. The backstrap loom, four-selvaged weaving, and camelid fiber use are distinctive American innovations.

## Mesoamerica (Maya, Aztec, etc.)

### Fibers
- **Cotton** (Gossypium hirsutum): Primary fiber, domesticated ~4,000 BCE
- **Agave fibers** (henequen, sisal): Coarse, ropes, nets
- **Rabbit hair**: Woven with cotton for warmth
- **Feathers**: Quetzal, macaw - elite garments

### Technology
- **Backstrap loom**: Universal, body-tensioned
- **Spindle whorl**: Clay, stone (malacate)
- **Natural dyes**: Cochineal (red), indigo (blue), purpura (purple), annatto (yellow)

### Garments
- **Huipil**: Women's blouse (woven, embroidered)
- **Tilma**: Men's cloak (cotton, painted)
- **Loincloth (maxtlatl)**: Men's basic garment
- **Quechquemitl**: Women's poncho-like overgarment

### Aztec (Postclassic, 1325–1521 CE)
- **Tribute textiles**: Standardized cloaks, warrior uniforms
- **Merchants (pochteca)**: Long-distance textile trade
- **Workshops**: Palace and merchant-controlled

## Andes (Inca, Moche, Paracas, etc.)

### Camelid Fibers (Unique to Andes)
| Animal | Fiber | Quality |
|--------|-------|---------|
| **Vicuña** | 12-14 µm | Finest, wild, royal Inca |
| **Alpaca** | 18-25 µm | Fine, uniform, domesticated |
| **Llama** | 20-40 µm | Coarse, double-coated |
| **Guanaco** | 16-18 µm | Wild, fine |

### Cotton
- **Domestication**: ~5,000 BCE (Peru coast)
- **Species**: Gossypium barbadense (Pima)
- **Colors**: Natural colored cotton (brown, green, tan)

### Weaving Technology
- **Backstrap loom**: Universal
- **Vertical loom**: Large pieces (Inca state)
- **Four-selvaged weaving**: Complete cloth, no cutting
- **Techniques**: 
  - Double cloth
  - Discontinuous warp/weft
  - Complementary warp
  - Tapestry, brocade, gauze

### Major Cultures
| Culture | Period | Signature |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| **Paracas** | 500 BCE–200 CE | Embroidered mantles, featherwork |
| **Nazca** | 200–600 CE | Painted cloth, camelid wool |
| **Moche** | 200–800 CE | Portrait vessels, textiles in burials |
| **Wari** | 600–1,000 CE | Standardized tunics, tie-dye |
| **Chimú** | 900–1,470 CE | Gauze, brocade, mass production |
| **Inca** | 1,400–1,532 CE | Qompi (state cloth), quipu |

### Inca State Textiles (1,400–1,532 CE)
- **Qompi**: Finest cloth, state monopoly
- **Acllas**: "Chosen women" weaving in state workshops
- **Standardization**: Thread counts, dimensions fixed
- **Quipu**: Knot records (textile-based information)

## North America

### Southwest (Anasazi/Ancestral Pueblo, Hohokam)
- **Cotton**: Cultivated ~1,000 CE
- **Loom**: Upright loom (vertical), backstrap
- **Fibers**: Cotton, yucca, rabbit hair, turkey feathers
- **Products**: Blankets, sashes, kilts

### Eastern Woodlands (Hopewell, Mississippian)
- **Fibers**: Nettle, milkweed, basswood, rabbit hair
- **Techniques**: Twining, plaiting, finger weaving
- **Products**: Bags, mantles, skirts

### Pacific Northwest (Coast Salish, etc.)
- **Fibers**: Mountain goat wool, dog hair, cedar bark
- **Products**: Chilkat blankets, cedar bark capes
- **Techniques**: Twining, braiding

## Dyes (Americas)

| Color | Source | Region |
|-------|--------|--------|
| **Red** | Cochineal (Dactylopius coccus) | Mesoamerica/Andes |
| **Purple** | Purpura (Purpura patula) | Pacific Mesoamerica |
| **Blue** | Indigo (Indigofera suffruticosa) | Mesoamerica/Andes |
| **Yellow** | Annatto (Bixa orellana) | Mesoamerica |
| **Brown/Black** | Tannins + iron, walnut | General |
