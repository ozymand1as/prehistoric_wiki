# Clothing/Woven-Textiles

---
**Period:** Neolithic – Present
**Region(s):** Global (Egypt, Mesopotamia, China, Andes, Europe)
**Culture(s):** Ancient Egyptian, Mesopotamian, Chinese, Andean, Neolithic European
**Time Range:** ~10,000 BCE – Present
**Confidence:** High
**Evidence Type:** Material

## Overview
Woven textiles are cloth made by interlacing threads on a loom, representing one of the oldest and most widespread textile technologies in human history. Weaving transformed raw fibers into durable, versatile fabric and became a cornerstone of ancient economies and daily life.

## History & Development
- ~10,000 BCE: Oldest known woven textiles in Neolithic Europe
- Development of the loom as the primary weaving tool
- Early fibers: plant-based (flax, hemp, cotton) and animal-based (wool, silk)
- Weaving techniques evolved from basic plain weave to twill and satin weave
- Textile production became a major industry in ancient civilizations including Egypt, Mesopotamia, China, and the Andes
- Key archaeological evidence: textile impressions in pottery, preserved textiles in dry climates

## Function & Use
- **Primary Use:** Clothing and personal adornment
- **Secondary Uses:** Shelter coverings, storage bags, ceremonial garments, trade goods
- **Cultural Significance:** Textile production was central to household economy; woven fabrics indicated social status and were used in religious rituals and burial practices

## Construction & Technique
Woven textiles are produced by interlacing warp (longitudinal) and weft (transverse) threads on a loom. The primary tools include various loom types:
- **Backstrap loom:** Simple loom where one end is attached to a fixed point and the other to the weaver's body
- **Ground loom:** Horizontal loom laid on the ground, used in many ancient cultures
- **Vertical loom:** Upright loom allowing larger pieces of fabric
- **Horizontal loom:** Floor-based loom that enabled more complex patterns and wider cloth

Fiber preparation includes spinning raw fibers into thread, which is then woven using techniques such as plain weave, twill, and satin weave.

## Regional Variations
- **Egyptian linen:** High-quality linen made from flax, prized for its fineness and used in both daily life and burial wrappings
- **Mesopotamian wool:** Wool-based textiles from sheep, central to Mesopotamian textile industry and trade
- **Chinese silk:** Silk production from silkworms, foundational to Chinese textile tradition and the Silk Road trade network
- **Andean textiles:** Complex woven textiles using camelid fibers (alpaca, llama), known for intricate patterns and vibrant dyes
