# Animal Skins and Hides

Animal skins and hides are the primary clothing material of the Paleolithic and remain important through the Bronze Age. This page covers hide procurement, processing methods, and use in clothing and shelter.

## Overview

Hides provide the most versatile and widely available material for prehistoric clothing. Every large mammal yields a potential garment, and the technologies for processing hides are among humanity's oldest.

## Hide Processing Methods

### Fresh Hide Preparation
1. **Skinning** - Removing hide with minimal cuts (stone knives, later metal)
2. **Fleshing** - Removing fat, meat, membrane (bone/stone/antler scrapers)
3. **Dehairing** - Scraping epidermis/hair off (grain side) or leaving on (fur)
4. **Braining/Tanning** - Softening with lipids (brain, egg, oil) or tannins
5. **Stretching/Drying** - Frame or hand-stretching while drying to prevent stiffening
6. **Softening** - Working dry hide over rope/beam/stake until supple

### Tanning Technologies
- **Brain tanning** (Paleolithic+) - Animal brain lipids emulsify fibers
- **Smoke tanning** - Formaldehyde from wood smoke preserves and waterproofs
- **Vegetable tanning** (Neolithic+) - Tannins from bark (oak, chestnut, hemlock)
- **Mineral tanning** (Late Bronze Age+) - Alum, salt combinations
- **Rawhide** - Untanned, dehaired, dried hard (containers, armor, cordage)

## Species & Applications

| Species | Hide Qualities | Primary Uses |
|---------|---------------|--------------|
| **Reindeer/Caribou** | Light, warm, hollow hair | Arctic parkas, sleeping robes |
| **Bison/Buffalo** | Thick, durable | Winter robes, tipi covers, moccasins |
| **Deer/Elk** | Soft, flexible | Shirts, leggings, bags |
| **Sheep/Goat** | With wool on = fleece; without = lightweight | Wool textiles, lightweight leather |
| **Cattle/Aurochs** | Large, thick | Heavy leather, shields, soles |
| **Bear** | Thick fur, tough hide | Ceremonial robes, bedding |
| **Fox/Wolf** | Luxurious fur | Trim, linings, trade goods |
| **Seal/Walrus** | Waterproof, oily | Arctic waterproof clothing |
| **Fish (shark, salmon)** | Unique textures | Specialty items, armor (sharkskin) |

## Archaeological Evidence

- **Lissoirs** (bone smoothers) - Neanderthal/Mousterian, hide working
- **End scrapers** - Upper Paleolithic standardized hide processing tools
- **Beamers** - Long bone/antler tools for dehairing large hides
- **Tanning pits** - Neolithic+ (waterlogged sites preserve)
- **Preserved hides** - Bog bodies (Bronze Age Denmark), ice mummies (Ötzi), permafrost burials (Pazyryk)

## Clothing Construction

- **Pattern pieces** - Tailored panels (sleeves, gores, gussets)
- **Seam types** - Running, whip, tunnel, lapped (water-resistant)
- **Thread** - Sinew (primary), plant fiber, gut, later wool/linen
- **Fasteners** - Toggles, buttons (bone/antler/wood), lacing, belts
- **Decoration** - Fringe, painting, quillwork, beadwork, fur mosaic

## Shelter Applications

- **Tipi/wigwam covers** - Bison, canvas (later)
- **Yurt covers** - Felted wool, hides
- **Boat coverings** - Umiak (walrus/seal), kayak (seal)
- **Door flaps, windbreaks** - Various species

## Trade & Economy

- **Raw hides** - Bulky, perishable, local use
- **Tanned leather** - Portable, high value, long-distance trade
- **Furs** - Luxury items, status markers, major trade goods (Silk Road, Fur Trade)
- **Parchment/vellum** - Calf/sheep/goat skin for writing (late periods)
