# Plant Fibers

Plant fibers (bast, leaf, seed, and grass fibers) were the primary textile raw materials before and alongside animal fibers. Their cultivation and processing represent a major technological achievement of the Neolithic Revolution. [1]

## Overview

The domestication of fiber plants—flax, hemp, cotton, ramie, and others—coincided with food plant domestication. These crops provided the raw material for the first true woven textiles and remained essential through the Bronze Age and beyond.

## Major Fiber Plants

### Flax (Linum usitatissimum) — Primary Old World Fiber
- **Domestication**: Near East ~8,000 BCE (founder crop) [2]
- **Plant**: Annual, 30-100 cm, blue flowers
- **Fiber**: Bast (phloem), 20-40 cm long
- **Properties**: Strong, smooth, lustrous, absorbent
- **Uses**: Linen cloth, ropes, nets, sailcloth
- **Processing**: Retting → breaking → scutching → hackling [3]
- **Regions**: Near East, Europe, Egypt, Central Asia

### Hemp (Cannabis sativa) — Coarse Fiber
- **Domestication**: East Asia ~6,000 BCE
- **Plant**: Annual, 1-4 m, dioecious
- **Fiber**: Bast, 50-200 cm, coarse
- **Properties**: Very strong, durable, rot-resistant [4]
- **Uses**: Rope, sailcloth, sacks, coarse cloth
- **Regions**: China, Central Asia, Europe (later)

### Cotton (Gossypium spp.) — Seed Fiber
- **Species**: G. arboreum (India), G. herbaceum (Africa), G. hirsutum (Mesoamerica), G. barbadense (Andes)
- **Domestication**: Indus Valley ~6,000 BCE; Peru ~5,000 BCE; Mesoamerica ~4,000 BCE
- **Fiber**: Seed trichomes (lint), 15-40 mm
- **Properties**: Soft, breathable, short staple
- **Uses**: Fine cloth, padding, wadding
- **Processing**: Ginning (seed removal), carding

### Ramie (Boehmeria nivea) — Fine Bast
- **Domestication**: China ~4,000 BCE
- **Fiber**: Bast, very long (60-250 cm), fine
- **Properties**: Silky, strong, stiff, mildew-resistant
- **Uses**: Fine cloth, fish nets, ropes
- **Processing**: Difficult degumming required

### Nettle (Urtica dioica) — Wild Bast
- **Source**: Wild harvest, widespread temperate
- **Fiber**: Bast, fine, strong
- **Period**: Mesolithic to present
- **Uses**: Fine cloth, cordage, fishing nets
- **Regions**: Europe, Asia

### Other Fibers
- **Jute** (Corchorus): India, coarse, sacks
- **Sisal** (Agave): Americas, hard leaf fiber, rope
- **Coir** (Coconut): Tropical, husk fiber, mats, rope
- **Bamboo**: Modern regenerated fiber
- **Grass fibers**: Esparto, sabai - paper, cordage

## Fiber Processing

### Retting (Microbial Separation)
- **Water retting**: Submerged in ponds/streams (7-14 days)
- **Dew retting**: Spread on grass (2-4 weeks)
- **Enzymatic**: Modern controlled retting
- **Critical**: Over-retting weakens fiber

### Mechanical Processing
- **Breaking**: Crush woody core (flax break)
- **Scutching**: Scrape away woody parts
- **Hackling/Combing**: Align fibers, remove tow (short fibers)
- **Cotton ginning**: Roller gin, saw gin (seed removal)

## Archaeological Evidence

### Macro-remains
- **Seeds**: Flax, hemp, cotton (charred, waterlogged)
- **Capsules**: Flax capsules, cotton bolls
- **Stems**: Flax stems in waterlogged sites

### Micro-remains
- **Phytoliths**: Silica bodies in leaves/stems
- **Pollen**: Cultivation evidence
- **Starch grains**: Processing tools

### Textile Analysis
- **Fiber ID**: Microscopy (twist test, cross-section)
- **Twist direction**: Cultural marker
- **Thread count**: Technology level
