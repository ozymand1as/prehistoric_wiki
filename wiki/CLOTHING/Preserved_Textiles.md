# Preserved Textiles

Exceptional preservation conditions—waterlogged, arid, frozen, or mineralized—have yielded the rare surviving textiles that provide direct evidence for ancient fiber, weave, color, and construction. [1]

## Overview

Textiles are among the most perishable archaeological materials. The few surviving examples come from specific preservation environments and provide irreplaceable data on ancient technology, trade, and aesthetics.

## Preservation Environments

### Waterlogged (Anaerobic)
- **Alpine lake dwellings** (Switzerland, Germany, Italy): Neolithic–Bronze Age [2]
  - Thousands of fragments, complete garments
  - Flax, wool, nettle, tree bast
- **Bogs** (Denmark, Germany, Netherlands): Bronze Age–Iron Age
  - Wool garments, skin capes, belts
  - Tannins preserve protein fibers
- **Riverine/coastal**: Wetland sites (Must Farm, UK; Torcello, Italy) [3]

### Arid (Desiccation)
- **Egyptian tombs**: Predynastic–Roman
  - Linen wrappings, tunics, sailcloth
  - Thousands of meters per burial
- **Tarim Basin** (Xinjiang, China): Bronze Age
  - Wool twill, plaid, felt, felt hats
  - Caucasian mummies, 2000 BCE
- **Peruvian coast**: Preceramic–Inca
  - Cotton, camelid, featherwork
  - Paracas, Nazca, Chancay textiles

### Frozen (Permafrost/Ice)
- **Pazyryk kurgans** (Altai, 4th c. BCE)
  - Felt, pile carpet, embroidery, silk
  - Horse trappings, clothing
- **Ötzi** (Alps, 3,300 BCE)
  - Grass cape, leather leggings, bear hat
- **Siberian kurgans**: Scythian, Xiongnu

### Mineralized (Pseudomorphs)
- **Metal corrosion**: Textile impressions on bronze/iron [4]
  - Weapons, jewelry, tools
  - Reveals weave, thread count, twist
- **Salt mines**: Hallstatt (Austria), Dürrnberg
  - 3,000+ fragments, Bronze Age–Iron Age
  - Wool, complex weaves, dyes

## Scientific Analysis Methods

### Fiber Identification
- **Light microscopy**: Cross-section, longitudinal [5]
- **SEM**: Surface morphology
- **FTIR**: Chemical composition
- **DNA**: Species identification (wool, silk)

### Weave Analysis
- **Thread count**: Warp/weft per cm
- **Weave structure**: Tabby, twill, satin, tapestry
- **Twist direction**: Z/S, angle, ply

### Dye Analysis
- **HPLC-MS/MS**: Dye compound identification
- **Raman spectroscopy**: Non-destructive
- **XRF**: Mordant metals

### Dating
- **Radiocarbon**: AMS on micro-samples
- **Stratigraphic**: Context association

## Information Revealed

| Aspect | Evidence |
|--------|----------|

| **Trade** | Non-local fibers, dyes, techniques |

| **Economy** | Standardization, mass production |
| **Artistry** | Pattern, color, symbolism |
