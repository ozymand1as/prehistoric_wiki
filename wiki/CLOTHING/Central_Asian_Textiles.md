# Central Asian Textiles

Central Asian textile traditions developed at the crossroads of steppe nomadism and oasis agriculture, producing distinctive felt, pile carpet, and woven traditions that influenced both East and West.

## Overview

The vast Central Asian region—from the Pontic-Caspian steppe to the Tarim Basin—developed textile technologies adapted to mobile pastoralism and oasis agriculture. Key innovations include felt making, pile carpet weaving, and the early use of camelid and sheep wool.

## Steppe Traditions (Pontic-Caspian, 4,000–500 BCE)

### Felt Making (Primary Technology)
- **Process**: Wool fibers matted by moisture, heat, pressure
- **No loom needed**: Portable, ideal for nomads
- **Products**: Yurt covers, clothing, saddle blankets, armor
- **Botai** (3,500 BCE): Early horse domestication, likely felt

### Pile Carpet Weaving
- **Pazyryk carpet** (4th c. BCE): Oldest known pile carpet
- **Technique**: Symmetrical knots on warp threads
- **Designs**: Animal style, geometric, rider scenes
- **Spread**: Steppe → Near East → Europe

### Fiber Sources
- **Sheep**: Fat-tailed, coarse wool
- **Goat**: Cashmere (fine undercoat)
- **Camel**: Bactrian camel hair
- **Horse**: Tail/mane hair (not spun)

## Oxus Civilization / BMAC (2,300–1,700 BCE)

### Oasis Agriculture
- **Cotton**: Early cultivation (Gossypium herbaceum)
- **Flax/Linen**: Local production
- **Wool**: Sheep, goat

### Urban Workshops
- **Sites**: Gonur, Togolok, Dashly
- **Tools**: Spindle whorls, loom weights, seals
- **Trade**: Cotton, wool textiles to Indus, Mesopotamia

### Textile Finds
- **Gonur**: Cotton fragments, dyed wool
- **Seal impressions**: Textile patterns on clay

## Tarim Basin (2,000–500 BCE)

### Exceptional Preservation
- **Arid climate**: Desiccation preserves textiles
- **Sites**: Xiaohe, Qäwrighul, Zaghunluq, Sampul
- **Mummies**: Caucasian populations, 2,000 BCE

### Textile Types
- **Wool twill**: 2/2 twill, plaid patterns
- **Felt**: Hats, socks, saddle covers
- **Pile**: Early knotted carpet fragments
- **Silk**: Han Dynasty imports (later)

### Technical Sophistication
- **Plaid twills**: Complex color patterns
- **Tablet weaving**: Borders, belts
- **Embroidery**: Chain stitch, couching

## Andronovo/Sintashta (2,100–1,400 BCE)

### Chariot & Textile Connection
- **Sintashta**: Chariot burials, felt, wool
- **Textile tools**: Spindle whorls, loom weights
- **Horse gear**: Felt saddle pads, wool trappings

## Fiber & Technology Summary

| Region | Primary Fibers | Key Technologies |
|--------|----------------|------------------|
| **Steppe** | Sheep wool, camel, goat | Felt, pile carpet, twining |
| **BMAC** | Cotton, flax, wool | Urban weaving, dyeing |
| **Tarim** | Wool, felt, silk (late) | Twill, plaid, embroidery |
| **Andronovo** | Wool, felt | Chariot gear, wagon covers |

## Trade Networks

### Silk Road Precursors
- **Jade**: Khotan → China (textile wrappings)
- **Lapis lazuli**: Badakhshan → Mesopotamia (textile bags)
- **Tin**: Central Asia → Near East/China
- **Silk**: China → West (Han+ but precursors earlier)

### Cultural Exchange
- **Pile carpet**: Steppe → Near East → Europe
- **Felt**: Steppe → China (Han military)
- **Cotton**: India/BMAC → Steppe → China
