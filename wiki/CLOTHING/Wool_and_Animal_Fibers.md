# Wool and Animal Fibers

Animal fibers—primarily sheep wool but also goat hair, camelid fiber, and others—became the dominant textile material in temperate Eurasia after the Neolithic. The transformation of wild hairy sheep into woolly breeds represents a major achievement in animal husbandry. [1]

## Overview

The domestication of sheep (~9,000 BCE) initially provided meat, milk, and hides. Selection for woolly fleece (replacing hairy coats) began ~6,000 BCE, with fully woolly sheep appearing by ~3,500 BCE. This "secondary products revolution" transformed pastoral economies.

## Major Animal Fibers

### Sheep Wool (Ovis aries)
- **Wild ancestor**: Mouflon (Ovis orientalis) - hairy, seasonal molt [2]
- **Domestication**: Near East ~9,000 BCE (meat/milk)
- **Woolly mutation**: Selection for continuous growth, no molt
- **Breed development**: Regional specialization by Bronze Age

### Goat Hair & Cashmere
- **Guard hair**: Coarse, protective outer coat
- **Cashmere/pashmina**: Fine undercoat (Kashmir, Mongolia, Iran) [3]
- **Mohair**: Angora goat, long lustrous curls
- **Uses**: Ropes, tents, fine cloth, carpets

### Camelid Fibers (Andes)
- **Llama**: Coarse, double-coated, guard hair + undercoat [4]
- **Alpaca**: Single-coated, fine, uniform (selected)
- **Vicuña**: Finest natural fiber (wild, protected)
- **Guanaco**: Intermediate
- **No sheep in pre-Columbian Americas**

### Other Fibers
- **Yak**: Tibetan plateau, fine undercoat
- **Muskox (Qiviut)**: Arctic, extremely fine
- **Rabbit (Angora)**: Long, soft, felts easily
- **Dog hair**: Coast Salish (Pacific NW), woven with wool
- **Horsehair**: Tail/mane, stiff, sieves, upholstery
- **Silk (Bombyx mori)**: Filament, not spun fiber (China)

## Sheep Breed Evolution

### Primitive Breeds (Surviving Relics)
- **Soay** (St Kilda): Moulting, hairy, small
- **Shetland**: Primitive, fine, variable
- **Icelandic**: Double-coated, hardy
- **Jacob**: Spotted, primitive horned

### Early Wool Types
- **Hairy**: Guard hair + fine underwool (moulting)
- **Generalized medium**: Dual-purpose (meat/wool)
- **Fine wool**: Merino-type (Spain, later)
- **Longwool**: Lincoln, Leicester (luster, length)
- **Carpet wool**: Coarse, durable (Near East, Central Asia)

## Fiber Properties

| Property | Wool | Hair/Kemp | Cashmere | Alpaca |
|----------|------|-----------|----------|--------|
| **Diameter** | 15-40 µm | >30 µm | 12-18 µm | 15-30 µm |
| **Crimp** | High (natural elasticity) | None | Low | Low |
| **Scale** | Overlapping (felting) | Smooth | Fine | Fine |
| **Length** | 5-15 cm | 10-30 cm | 3-5 cm | 7-15 cm |
| **Strength** | High | Medium | Medium | High |

## Wool Processing

### Shearing
- **Plucking/rooing**: Primitive (moulting breeds)
- **Shears**: Bronze Age+ (spring-loaded iron)
- **Timing**: Spring (before heat), once/year

### Skirting & Sorting
- **Fleece zones**: Shoulder (best), britch (coarsest), belly (short)
- **Grades**: Fine, medium, coarse, kemp, stained

### Scouring (Washing)
- **Suint**: Natural wool grease + sweat (potash source)
- **Lanolin**: Refined wool wax (cosmetics, pharmaceuticals)
- **Methods**: Cold water, hot water, solvent

### Carding & Combing
- **Carding**: Disentangle, align (woolen yarn - fuzzy)
- **Combing**: Remove short fibers, align parallel (worsted - smooth)
- **Tools**: Hand cards, combs, later drum carders

## Regional Traditions

### Near East & Mediterranean
- **Fat-tailed sheep**: Energy storage, cooking fat
- **Awassi**: Dairy + carpet wool
- **Merino origin**: Spain (North African stock)

### Europe
- **North**: Shortwool, hardy (Shetland, Icelandic)
- **Central**: Medium wool, dual-purpose
- **South**: Transhumance, Merino (Spain)

### Central Asia
- **Fat-rumped**: Karakul, fat storage
- **Karakul**: Persian lamb (fetal pelts)
- **Nomadic**: Multi-purpose (meat, milk, wool, felt)

### Andes (No Sheep Pre-Contact)
- **Alpaca**: Primary fiber animal
- **Llama**: Pack + coarse fiber
- **Vicuña**: Royal Inca fiber (wild harvest)
- **Chinchilla**: Not domesticated

## Economic Impact

### Bronze Age Wool Economy
- **Palace workshops**: Ur, Mari, Knossos, Pylos
- **Rationed workers**: Grain/oil for textile production
- **Standardized**: Thread counts, weights, dimensions
- **Trade**: Wool textiles for metals, timber, luxury goods

### Textile as Currency
- **Mesopotamia**: Wool measured in minas/talents
- **Linear B**: Detailed wool inventories
- **Tribute**: Cloth as tax payment
