# Clothing/Fursuits

---
**Period:** Paleolithic / Mesolithic / Neolithic
**Region(s):** Global (Arctic, Northern Europe, Siberia)
**Culture(s):** Denisovan, Inuit, Yupik, Northern European hunter-gatherers, Siberian peoples
**Time Range:** ~170,000 BP – present (prehistoric use from ~50,000 BP)
**Confidence:** High
**Evidence Type:** Material / Ethnographic / Inferred

## Overview

Fur clothing (fursuits) represents one of the earliest forms of human attire, providing essential insulation in cold climates. Made from animal hides and fur, these garments were critical for survival in northern environments and also served as trade goods and status symbols. While modern fursuits differ significantly from prehistoric fur clothing, the fundamental techniques of hide processing and garment construction have deep archaeological roots.

## History & Development

- **~170,000 BP:** Earliest inferred use of animal hides for body covering by archaic humans, based on cold-climate habitation patterns.
- **~50,000 BP:** Bone needles discovered at Denisova Cave provide the earliest direct evidence of sewn fur garments, indicating sophisticated hide-working techniques.
- **Mesolithic (~10,000–5,000 BP):** Diversification of fur processing techniques across northern Eurasia and Europe; introduction of scraping and tanning methods.
- **Neolithic (~5,000–3,000 BP):** Expansion of fur clothing production with domestication of animals such as reindeer; fur becomes a medium for trade and social differentiation.
- **Historic period:** Ethnographic records document complex fur garment traditions among Arctic and subarctic peoples, including the Inuit, Yupik, and Siberian groups.

## Function & Use

- **Primary Use:** Thermal insulation and protection from cold and wind in northern climates.
- **Secondary Uses:** Trade goods, status symbols, ceremonial adornment, bedding, and shelter lining.
- **Cultural Significance:** Fur garments signified social status and wealth; in many northern cultures, specific furs were reserved for elites or used in ritual contexts. Fur also played a role in intergroup trade networks spanning vast distances.

## Construction & Technique

- **Material sourcing:** Animal hides and fur obtained from hunting (reindeer, seal, fox, bear, rabbit, and other species).
- **Hide processing:** Scraping and tanning to remove hair and soften the hide; methods varied by culture and available resources.
- **Sewing:** Bone needles and sinew thread used to stitch hide pieces together; needle technology was critical to garment construction.
- **Garment types:** Cloaks, robes, tunics, and trousers, differentiated by region and intended use.
- **Decoration:** Embroidery, fringe, and ornamentation using bone, ivory, and shell.

## Regional Variations

- **Arctic peoples (Inuit, Yupik):** Utilized seal skin and caribou hide for parka-style garments; multi-layered construction for extreme cold; emphasis on waterproofing with seal intestine.
- **Northern European peoples:** Predominantly used reindeer and fox fur; tailored garments with bone-fastened closures; fur linings for wool and hide outerwear.
- **Siberian peoples:** Diverse use of bear, rabbit, and reindeer fur; distinctive cut and silhouette influenced by nomadic lifestyle; fur trim and hood designs adapted to taiga and tundra environments.
