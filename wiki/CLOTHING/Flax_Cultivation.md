# Flax Cultivation

Flax (Linum usitatissimum) is one of the founder crops of the Neolithic Revolution and the primary source of linen fiber for Old World textiles for over 8,000 years. [1]

## Overview

Flax was domesticated in the Near East alongside wheat, barley, and legumes. Its dual use for fiber (linen) and oil (linseed) made it exceptionally valuable. The cultivation and processing of flax represents one of the most labor-intensive agricultural-technical systems in prehistory.

## Domestication & Spread

### Wild Ancestor
- **Species**: Linum bienne (wild flax)
- **Region**: Fertile Crescent, Mediterranean basin [2]
- **Traits**: Small seeds, dehiscent capsules, short fibers

### Domestication Syndrome
- **Larger seeds**: Non-shattering capsules
- **Longer fibers**: Single tall stem, reduced branching [3]
- **Higher oil content**: Linseed oil
- **Loss of dormancy**: Uniform germination

### Archaeological Evidence
- **PPNB** (8,500 BCE): Wild flax at Jericho, Abu Hureyra [4]
- **Early Neolithic** (7,500 BCE): Domestic flax at Çayönü, Nevalı Çori
- **Egypt** (5,000 BCE): Flax in Predynastic graves
- **Europe** (5,500 BCE): LBK sites (flax seeds, capsule fragments)

## Cultivation

### Growing Requirements
- **Climate**: Cool, moist growing season
- **Soil**: Well-drained, fertile, pH 6-7
- **Rotation**: Not continuous (flax sickness - fungal buildup)
- **Density**: High (1,000+ plants/m²) for fiber; lower for seed

### Growth Cycle
- **Sowing**: Spring (fiber), autumn (seed in Mediterranean)
- **Flowering**: 50-60 days (blue flowers, self-pollinating)
- **Harvest**: 90-110 days (fiber), 120+ days (seed)
- **Pulling**: Whole plant uprooted (preserves fiber length)

## Fiber Processing (From Field to Spinning)

### 1. Retting (Critical Step)
- **Water retting**: Submerged 7-14 days (ponds, streams)
- **Dew retting**: Spread on field 2-4 weeks
- **Enzymatic**: Pectinases separate fibers from woody core
- **Risk**: Over-retting = weak fiber; under-retting = difficult separation

### 2. Drying
- **Stooks**: Bundles stood upright to dry
- **Moisture**: ~12% for storage

### 3. Breaking
- **Flax break**: Wooden tool crushes woody core (boon)
- **Mechanical**: Later rollers

### 4. Scutching
- **Scutching knife**: Scrapes away broken boon
- **Swinging board**: Removes remaining shives

### 5. Hackling (Combing)
- **Hackle**: Bed of iron spikes
- **Output**: 
  - **Line flax**: Long fibers (20-40 cm) for fine linen
  - **Tow**: Short fibers for coarse cloth, rope

## Products & Byproducts

| Product | Source | Uses |
|---------|--------|------|
| **Line flax** | Long fibers | Fine linen, lace, thread |
| **Tow** | Short fibers | Coarse cloth, sacking, caulking |
| **Linseed oil** | Seeds | Paint, varnish, lighting, nutrition |
| **Linseed cake** | Pressed meal | Animal feed |
| **Flax straw** | Stems | Paper, insulation, composites |

## Regional Traditions

### Ancient Egypt
- **Exclusive linen culture**: No wool in temples
- **State monopoly**: Pharaonic control
- **Quality grades**: Royal, temple, common
- **Mummy wrappings**: Thousands of meters per burial

### Europe (LBK to Medieval)
- **Every household**: Flax patch + processing tools
- **Seasonal labor**: Women's work (retting, spinning)
- **Trade**: Linen cloth exported from Flanders, Brittany

### Russia/Baltic
- **Major exporter**: 18th-19th century
- **Peasant production**: Communal retting ponds

## Archaeological Evidence

### Macro-remains
- **Seeds**: Charred, waterlogged (identifiable)
- **Capsules**: Fragments in settlements
- **Stems**: Waterlogged (rare)

### Tools
- **Flax breaks**: Wooden (rarely preserved)
- **Scutching knives**: Wood, bone
- **Hackles**: Iron spikes (metal ages)
- **Retting ponds**: Archaeological features

### Textile Analysis
- **Fiber ID**: Longitudinal striations, cross-section (polygonal)
- **Twist**: S-twist dominant in Egypt, Z in Europe
