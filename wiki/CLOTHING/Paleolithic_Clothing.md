# Paleolithic Clothing

Evidence for tailored clothing appears in the Upper Paleolithic (~40,000 BCE), coinciding with the expansion of modern humans into cold Eurasian environments. While earlier hominins likely used simple hide draping, the archaeological record shows sophisticated sewing technology by the Gravettian period.

## Overview

Upper Paleolithic clothing represents the first tailored, fitted garments in human history. The invention of the eyed bone needle (~40,000 BCE) enabled sewn, multi-piece garments essential for survival in glacial environments. This technology facilitated the peopling of the Arctic and high latitudes.

## Materials

### Animal Hides & Furs
- **Primary sources**: Reindeer, horse, bison, fox, wolf, bear, lion
- **Selection criteria**: Season of kill (winter coats), animal size, fur quality
- **Processing**: Fresh hides preferred for clothing; older for containers/structures

### Sinew & Thread
- **Material**: Animal tendon (sinew), particularly from large herbivores
- **Preparation**: Dried, pounded, separated into fine fibers
- **Properties**: High tensile strength, shrinks when wet (tightens seams)

### Decorative Elements
- **Ivory beads**: Thousands found at Sungir (Russia), sewn onto garments
- **Shell beads**: Marine shells traded hundreds of kilometers inland
- **Pigments**: Red ochre staining on clothing/hides

## Tools & Technology

### Bone & Ivory Needles
- **Earliest**: ~40,000 BCE (Denisova Cave, Siberia)
- **Materials**: Bird bone, mammoth ivory, antler
- **Features**: Minute eyes (0.5-1mm), polished shafts, tapered points
- **Distribution**: Europe, Siberia, China, North Africa

### Hide Scrapers
- **End scrapers**: Retouched flint blades for dehairing and thinning
- **Bone/antler beamers**: For heavy dehairing of thick hides
- **Polishing stones**: For softening processed hides

### Awls & Piercing Tools
- **Bone awls**: For pre-punching holes in thick hide
- **Ivory points**: Finer work, decorative stitching

## Garment Types (Reconstructed)

### Fitted Parkas/Anoraks
- **Construction**: Multiple panels, tailored to body
- **Features**: Hood, tapered sleeves, overlapping front closure
- **Evidence**: Figurines (Venus of Lespugue, Mal'ta), bead patterns on burials

### Trousers/Leggings
- **Construction**: Separate legs, tied to belt/girdle
- **Evidence**: Bead distributions on lower limbs at Sungir

### Footwear
- **Construction**: Hide uppers, separate soles, insulated with grass/hay
- **Evidence**: Ötzi (later, 3,300 BCE) shows continuity; impressions in Paleolithic caves

### Headgear
- **Caps**: Fur caps with ear flaps (bead evidence at Sungir)
- **Hoods**: Integrated into parkas

## Key Archaeological Sites

| Site | Date | Evidence |
|------|------|----------|
| **Denisova Cave** (Siberia) | ~40,000 BCE | Oldest eyed bone needle |
| **Kostenki** (Russia) | ~30,000 BCE | Needles, awls, beads, figurines |
| **Sungir** (Russia) | ~28,000 BCE | Elaborate beadwork on burials (thousands of ivory beads) |
| **Dolní Věstonice** (Czech) | ~26,000 BCE | Textile impressions on clay, Venus figurines with clothing detail |
| **Mal'ta** (Siberia) | ~24,000 BCE | Ivory figurines with tailored clothing |
| **Cussac Cave** (France) | ~28,000 BCE | Engravings showing hooded figures |

## Tanning Technology

### Brain Tanning (Most Likely)
- **Agent**: Animal brain lipids (lecithin)
- **Process**: Brain mash rubbed into dehaired hide, stretched while drying
- **Result**: Soft, washable, white buckskin
- **Ethnographic analogy**: Universal among circumpolar and subarctic peoples

### Smoke Tanning
- **Process**: Hides smoked over rotten wood/ fungus
- **Result**: Water-resistant, golden-brown color
- **Used for**: Footwear, outerwear

### Vegetable Tanning (Unlikely in Paleolithic)
- **Requires**: Tannin-rich bark, containers, time
- **Evidence**: First clear evidence in Neolithic

## Sewing Techniques

### Stitch Types
- **Running stitch**: Simple, fast, for seams
- **Whip stitch/overcast**: Edge finishing, joining panels
- **Blanket stitch**: Reinforced edges, decorative
- **Tunnel stitch**: Invisible seams (fur turned inward)

### Seam Construction
- **Butted seams**: Edges joined edge-to-edge (flat)
- **Lapped seams**: One edge overlapped (stronger, water-resistant)
- **Gussets**: Diamond inserts for 3D shaping (crotch, underarm)

## Cultural Significance

### Social Signaling
- **Beadwork**: Thousands of hours of labor = status display
- **Fur quality**: Rare species (lion, bear) = hunter prestige
- **Color**: Ochre staining = ritual/symbolic

### Gender & Age
- **Differentiated garments**: Figurines suggest sex-specific styles
- **Child clothing**: Smaller needles, simpler construction inferred
- **Burial clothing**: Elaborate = special status/ritual role

### Technological Package
Clothing technology co-occurs with:
- **Shelter**: Tailored clothing + structured shelters = Arctic adaptation
- **Storage**: Containers, bags for seasonal gear
- **Transport**: Sleds, toboggans for moving kit

## Experimental Archaeology

Modern reconstructions demonstrate:
- **Time investment**: 1 parka = 20-40 hours (hide prep + sewing)
- **Thermal performance**: Fitted fur parka = comfort to -40°C
- **Durability**: Brain-tanned hide lasts years with care
- **Mobility**: Fitted garments don't restrict movement like draped hides

## Uncertainties & Research Questions

- When exactly did eyed needles appear? (Denisova suggests 40k+ BCE)
- Did Neanderthals have tailored clothing? (Possible lissoirs, no needles)
- Regional variation in garment styles (inferred from bead distributions)
- Summer vs. winter wardrobes (limited evidence)
- Role of plant fibers (nettle, grass) in Paleolithic textiles
