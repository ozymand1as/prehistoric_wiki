# Experimental Archaeology of Textiles

Experimental archaeology reconstructs ancient textile technologies through hands-on replication, providing quantitative data on labor, skill, and material properties that cannot be obtained from artifacts alone. [1]

## Overview

Since textiles rarely preserve, experimental reconstruction of tools, techniques, and garments is essential for understanding prehistoric textile production. Modern experiments test hypotheses derived from archaeological evidence.

## Major Experimental Projects

### Spinning Experiments
- **Whorl weight vs. thread diameter**: Light whorls (5-10g) → fine thread; heavy (30-50g) → thick [2]
- **Production rates**: 
  - Novice: 5-10 m/hour
  - Experienced: 20-50 m/hour
  - Expert: 50-100 m/hour
- **Fiber consumption**: 100g wool → ~100m singles (varies by twist) [3]

### Weaving Experiments
- **Warp-weighted loom**:
  - Setup: 2-4 hours (warping, tensioning)
  - Weaving: 0.5-2 m²/hour (plain weave)
  - Complex patterns: 0.2-0.5 m²/hour
- **Two-beam loom**: 2-3x faster than warp-weighted [4]
- **Backstrap loom**: Portable, limited width (~60 cm)

### Garment Reconstruction
- **Ötzi's clothing** (3,300 BCE): 
  - 18+ pieces, 6 species, tailored fits
  - Experimental replicas: 300+ hours total
- **Hallstatt textiles**: Diamond twill, complex dye patterns
- **Egyptian tunics**: Pleated, zero-waste construction

### Tool Replication
- **Spindle whorls**: Clay, stone, bone - test weight/shape effects
- **Loom weights**: Clay firing temperatures, suspension methods
- **Needles**: Bone, bronze - eye formation techniques
- **Shears**: Bronze vs iron - cutting efficiency

## Quantitative Data from Experiments

### Labor Investment
| Product | Spinning | Weaving | Finishing | Total |
|---------|----------|---------|-----------|-------|
| **Simple tunic** (wool) | 20 hrs | 10 hrs | 5 hrs | 35 hrs |
| **Fine linen shirt** | 50 hrs | 15 hrs | 10 hrs | 75 hrs |
| **Wool cloak** | 30 hrs | 20 hrs | 10 hrs | 60 hrs |
| **Trousers** | 15 hrs | 8 hrs | 5 hrs | 28 hrs |

### Material Consumption
- **Wool**: 1 kg fleece → 600-800g clean → 500-600m yarn
- **Flax**: 1 kg stems → 150-200g fiber → 300-400m yarn
- **Dye**: 50g madder → 1 kg wool (deep red)

### Thermal Performance
- **Wool tunic**: +10°C comfort range
- **Fur parka** (Paleolithic): +20-30°C
- **Linen shirt**: Breathable, minimal insulation
- **Felt hat**: Wind/water resistant

## Skill Acquisition

### Learning Curves
| Skill | Novice → Competent | Competent → Expert |
|-------|-------------------|-------------------|
| **Drop spindle** | 20-40 hrs | 200+ hrs |
| **Warp-weighted loom** | 40-60 hrs | 500+ hrs |
| **Tablet weaving** | 10-20 hrs | 100+ hrs |
| **Natural dyeing** | 20-30 hrs | 200+ hrs |

### Apprenticeship Model
- **Age start**: 6-8 years (spinning), 10-12 (weaving)
- **Duration**: 5-7 years to journeyman
- **Knowledge transfer**: Oral, hands-on, gesture

## Experimental Methods

### Controlled Variables
- **Tool form**: Weight, shape, material
- **Fiber preparation**: Retting, carding, combing
- **Environmental**: Humidity, temperature, light
- **Body mechanics**: Posture, gesture, fatigue

### Data Collection
- **Time-motion**: Video analysis
- **Physiological**: Heart rate, calorie expenditure
- **Output metrics**: Length, weight, quality grades
- **Wear analysis**: Tool degradation patterns

## Key Insights

### From Experiments
1. **Spinning is bottleneck**: 80% of labor in thread production
2. **Loom type determines speed**: Two-beam > warp-weighted > backstrap
3. **Skill reduces waste**: Expert = 5% fiber loss vs 20% novice
4. **Group production efficient**: Shared warping, child labor for spinning
5. **Seasonal scheduling**: Retting (summer), spinning (winter), weaving (spring)

### Validating Archaeology
- **Whorl weights**: Match experimental optima for fiber types
- **Loom weight rows**: Match experimental warp spacing
- **Tool wear**: Matches experimental use patterns
- **Production scales**: Match textual records (Linear B, Ur III)
