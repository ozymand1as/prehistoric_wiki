# Aegean Textiles

The Aegean Bronze Age (Minoan and Mycenaean) developed sophisticated textile industries documented by Linear B tablets, frescoes, and archaeological finds, with palatial workshops producing textiles for elite consumption, religious use, and international trade.

## Overview

Aegean textiles are among the best-documented prehistoric textiles due to the combination of Linear B administrative records, vivid fresco depictions, and exceptional preservation in specific contexts. Palatial centers controlled production from raw material to finished cloth.

## Minoan Crete (2,000–1,450 BCE)

### Production
- **Palatial workshops**: Knossos, Phaistos, Malia, Zakros
- **Workers**: Hundreds of women in textile workgroups
- **Records**: Linear A (undeciphered), seals, nodules
- **Raw materials**: Wool (local), flax (local), imported dyes

### Fresco Evidence
- **Women's dress**: Flounced skirts, open bodices, aprons
- **Men's dress**: Kilts, loincloths, codpieces
- **Patterns**: Woven borders, embroidery, appliqué
- **Colors**: Red, blue, yellow, white, black

### Trade
- **Exports**: Textiles, dyed wool, saffron
- **Imports**: Tin, copper, ivory, gold, lapis lazuli

## Mycenaean Mainland (1,600–1,200 BCE)

### Linear B Records (Pylos, Knossos, Thebes)
- **Workgroups**: 10–50 women per supervisor
- **Rations**: Grain, figs, wool per woman/month
- **Specialization**: Carders, spinners, weavers, finishers
- **Output targets**: Fixed quantities per worker

### Palace Economies
- **Pylos**: 500+ textile workers
- **Knossos**: 900+ workers (post-Minoan)
- **Products**: Standard cloth units, garments, sailcloth
- **State control**: Centralized collection, redistribution

### Textile Types (Linear B Terms)
- **TE-PA**: Heavy cloth
- **A-PO-RE-WE**: Light cloth
- **PA-WE**: Linen
- **O-NO**: Wool

## Materials & Technology

### Fibers
| Fiber | Source | Use |
|-------|--------|-----|
| **Wool** | Local sheep (improved breeds) | Main textile fiber |
| **Flax/Linen** | Local cultivation | Fine garments, sailcloth |
| **Silk** | Imported (rare) | Elite only, late Mycenaean |

### Dyes
- **Tyrian purple**: Murex (local production)
- **Madder**: Red (local)
- **Woad/Indigo**: Blue (imported/woad local)
- **Saffron**: Yellow (Crocus, local cultivation)
- **Safflower**: Yellow (imported)

### Weaves
- **Tabby**: Basic
- **Twill**: 2/2, decorative
- **Tapestry**: Pictorial (fresco evidence)
- **Tablet weaving**: Borders, trim

## Garments (Iconographic Evidence)

### Women
- **Flounced skirts**: Multiple tiers, woven patterns
- **Open-front bodices**: Laced, embroidered
- **Aprons**: Decorated, ritual use
- **Veils/headcloths**: Pinned, decorated

### Men
- **Kilts**: Wrap-around, varying lengths
- **Codpieces**: Distinctive Mycenaean
- **Cloaks**: Pinned at shoulder

## Trade & Economy

### Exports
- **Perfumed oils + textiles**: To Egypt, Near East
- **Saffron**: Major export (Knossos frescoes)
- **Dyed wool**: Purple, madder, saffron

### Imports
- **Raw materials**: Tin, copper, ivory, gold
- **Luxury fibers**: Silk (late), fine linen
- **Dye materials**: Indigo, kermes

## Archaeological Evidence

### Preserved Textiles
- **Thessaly** (Greece): Carbonized fragments
- **Santorini** (Akrotiri): Impressions in volcanic ash
- **Mycenae**: Gold foil impressions on cloth

### Tools
- **Loom weights**: Thousands at palace sites
- **Spindle whorls**: Clay, stone, decorated
- **Needles**: Bronze, bone
- **Shears**: Bronze (Mycenaean)

### Iconography
- **Frescoes**: Knossos, Akrotiri, Mycenae, Tiryns
- **Sealstones**: Weaving scenes
- **Ivory plaques**: Dressed figures
