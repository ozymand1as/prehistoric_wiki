# Tanning and Hide Processing

Tanning is the process of converting raw animal hides into stable, durable leather through chemical treatment that prevents decomposition and imparts desirable physical properties.

## Overview

Hide processing represents one of humanity's oldest chemical technologies. From simple scraping and brain-tanning to complex vegetable tanning, the methods evolved to produce materials suited for clothing, footwear, containers, shelter, and armor.

## Processing Stages

### 1. Curing (Preservation)
- **Salting**: Dry salt or brine (commercial)
- **Drying**: Air/sun drying (traditional)
- **Freezing**: Modern industrial
- **Purpose**: Prevent bacterial decay before tanning

### 2. Beamhouse Operations
- **Soaking**: Rehydrate, remove salt/dirt
- **Liming**: Sodium sulfide + lime - remove hair, swell collagen
- **Deliming**: Ammonium salts - lower pH
- **Bating**: Enzymes - clean, soften grain
- **Pickling**: Salt + acid - prepare for tannage

### 3. Tannage (Core Process)
- **Vegetable tanning**: Plant tannins (bark, leaves, fruits)
- **Mineral tanning**: Alum, chromium, zirconium
- **Oil tanning**: Fish oil, cod liver oil (chamois)
- **Brain/smoke tanning**: Traditional brain lipids + smoke
- **Syntans**: Synthetic tannins (modern)

### 4. Post-Tanning
- **Neutralization**: Remove residual acid
- **Fatliquoring**: Oils for softness, flexibility
- **Dyeing**: Color penetration
- **Drying**: Air, vacuum, toggle
- **Finishing**: Buffing, coating, embossing

## Traditional Tanning Methods

### Brain Tanning (Paleolithic+)
- **Agent**: Animal brain (lecithin, phospholipids)
- **Process**: Brain mash worked into hide, stretched dry
- **Result**: Soft, washable, white buckskin
- **Cultures**: Universal among hunter-gatherers
- **Time**: Days to weeks

### Smoke Tanning
- **Agent**: Wood smoke (formaldehyde, phenols)
- **Process**: Hide smoked over rotten wood
- **Result**: Water-resistant, golden-brown
- **Use**: Moccasins, outerwear, tipis

### Vegetable Tanning (Neolithic+)
- **Agents**: Oak bark, chestnut, hemlock, quebracho, mimosa
- **Process**: Weeks to months in increasing tannin concentration
- **Result**: Firm, shapeable, carvable leather
- **Innovation**: Tanning pits, vats, drumming (industrial)

### Mineral Tanning (Late Bronze Age+)
- **Alum tanning** (tawing): Aluminum potassium sulfate
- **Result**: White, soft, heat-sensitive (glove leather)
- **Chrome tanning** (1858): Chromium III salts
- **Result**: Fast, soft, heat-stable (modern dominant)

## Archaeological Evidence

### Tools
- **Scrapers**: Stone (Paleolithic), metal (later) - fleshing, graining
- **Beamers**: Bone/antler - heavy dehairing
- **Lissoirs**: Bone smoothers - Neanderthal+
- **Tanning pits**: Lined depressions (Neolithic+)
- **Vats**: Wood, stone, ceramic (Bronze Age+)

### Preservation
- **Bog bodies** (Denmark): Bronze Age leather garments
- **Ötzi** (3,300 BCE): Bearskin hat, deerskin quiver, grass cape
- **Pazyryk** (Altai, 400 BCE): Saddles, felt, leather
- **Egyptian tombs**: Leather sandals, bags, chariot parts

## Leather Types & Uses

| Type | Tannage | Properties | Uses |
|------|---------|------------|------|
| **Buckskin** | Brain | Soft, stretchy, washable | Clothing, bags |
| **Rawhide** | None (dehaired, dried) | Hard, transparent, shrinks wet | Parfleches, drumheads, lacing |
| **Veg-tan** | Vegetable | Firm, moldable, carvable | Armor, shoes, saddles, bookbinding |
| **Chamois** | Oil (fish) | Extremely soft, absorbent | Gloves, cleaning |
| **Alum-tawed** | Alum | White, soft, heat-sensitive | Gloves, bookbinding |
| **Chromed** | Chrome | Soft, stable, water-resistant | Modern shoes, upholstery |

## Environmental & Health

### Traditional
- **Sustainable**: Local materials, biodegradable
- **Labor-intensive**: Weeks to months
- **Odor**: Significant (lime, putrefaction)

### Industrial (Chrome)
- **Speed**: Hours
- **Toxicity**: Chromium VI (carcinogenic) if poorly managed
- **Effluent**: High TDS, sulfides, chromium
- **Regulation**: Modern tanneries require treatment
