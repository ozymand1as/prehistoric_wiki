# Bone Needles and Tools

Bone, ivory, and antler needles represent the earliest sewing technology. Their appearance in the Upper Paleolithic (~40,000 BCE) coincides with the development of tailored clothing essential for cold-climate survival.

## Overview

The eyed needle is a hallmark of behavioral modernity. Before needles, hides were draped or tied; with needles, fitted multi-piece garments became possible, enabling human expansion into Arctic latitudes.

## Needle Evolution

### Upper Paleolithic (~40,000–12,000 BCE)
- **Materials**: Bird bone (hollow), mammoth ivory, antler
- **Manufacture**: Grooved and snapped, eyes drilled/gouged
- **Sites**: Denisova Cave (Siberia), Kostenki, Sungir, Dolní Věstonice
- **Dimensions**: 40-80 mm long, 0.5-1 mm eye diameter

### Gravettian (~33,000–22,000 BCE)
- **Standardization**: Consistent form, fine eyes
- **Ivory needles**: Polished, tapered, durable
- **Associated**: Beaded garments (Sungir - thousands of ivory beads)

### Magdalenian (~17,000–12,000 BCE)
- **Bone needles**: Dominant, mass-produced appearance
- **Awls**: Distinct from needles, no eye, for piercing
- **Decorative**: Incised patterns on shafts

### Neolithic (~10,000–3,000 BCE)
- **Copper needles**: Appear late (Balkans, Near East)
- **Bone continues**: Alongside metal
- **Mass production**: Standardized forms in settlements

### Bronze Age (~3,300–1,200 BCE)
- **Bronze needles**: Cast, durable, fine
- **Shapes**: Straight, curved, double-eyed
- **Workshop production**: Specialized metalworkers

## Manufacturing Techniques

### Bone/Ivory Needles
1. **Blank selection**: Long bone splinter, ivory sliver
2. **Shaping**: Abrasion on stone, knife whittling
3. **Eye formation**: 
   - **Gouged**: Stone point rotates
   - **Drilled**: Flint microdrill (microburin technique)
   - **Punched**: Fine punch on anvil
4. **Polishing**: Sand, leather, fat - smooth surface

### Metal Needles
- **Casting**: Two-part mold (Bronze Age)
- **Forging**: Hammered from sheet/wire (Iron Age)
- **Eye**: Punched (iron), cast (bronze)

## Archaeological Evidence

### Key Sites
| Site | Period | Finds |
|------|--------|-------|
| **Denisova Cave** | ~40,000 BCE | Oldest eyed needle (bird bone) |
| **Kostenki** (Russia) | ~30,000 BCE | Ivory needles, awls, beads |
| **Sungir** (Russia) | ~28,000 BCE | 1000s ivory beads on clothing |
| **Dolní Věstonice** | ~26,000 BCE | Textile impressions, needles |
| **Mal'ta** (Siberia) | ~24,000 BCE | Ivory figurines with clothing detail |
| **Tianyuan Cave** (China) | ~40,000 BCE | Early bone tools |

### Use-Wear Analysis
- **Tip polish**: Fabric penetration
- **Eye wear**: Thread friction
- **Shaft polish**: Hand grip, leather friction
- **Break patterns**: Tip snapping, eye splitting

## Experimental Archaeology

### Production Time
- **Bone needle**: 30-60 minutes (experienced)
- **Ivory needle**: 60-120 minutes
- **Eye drilling**: 10-30 minutes (microdrill)

### Performance
- **Thread capacity**: Sinew, plant fiber, gut
- **Fabric range**: Leather to fine linen
- **Durability**: Months to years of use

## Related Technologies

### Awls
- **Bone awls**: Long, sharp, no eye
- **Function**: Pierce holes for lacing, stitching thick leather
- **Ubiquitous**: Every Paleolithic site

### Beadwork Tools
- **Micro-drills**: Perforate shells, ivory, teeth
- **Bead spacing**: Needle-threading for patterns
