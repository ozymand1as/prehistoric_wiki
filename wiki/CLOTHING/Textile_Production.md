# Textile Production

Textile production in the Bronze Age represents the first industrial-scale manufacturing in human history. Palace and temple workshops employed hundreds of workers in standardized, rationed production systems documented by extensive written records. [1]

## Overview

The shift from household to institutional textile production marks a critical transition in economic organization. Textiles became a major form of wealth, tribute, and trade commodity, with production managed by state bureaucracies.

## Production Organization

### Household Production (Neolithic–Early Bronze Age)
- **Unit**: Nuclear/extended family
- **Labor**: Women, children, elderly
- **Output**: Subsistence + local exchange
- **Tools**: Domestic loom, spindle whorls

### Institutional Workshops (Bronze Age)
- **Institutions**: Palace, temple, royal estate
- **Scale**: Dozens to hundreds of workers
- **Management**: Scribes, overseers, accountants
- **Records**: Clay tablets (cuneiform, Linear B)

## Bronze Age Textile Economies

### Mesopotamia (Sumer/Akkad/Babylon)
- **Ur III** (2112–2004 BCE): Best documented
  - **Workers**: 500–1,000+ women per major center
  - **Rations**: Grain, wool, oil allocated monthly
  - **Standards**: Thread count, weight, dimensions fixed [2]
  - **Output**: Thousands of garments/year per center
- **Products**: Standardized cloth units (gú), garments, sailcloth

### Egypt (Old–New Kingdom)
- **Temple workshops**: Amun, Ptah, Hathor
- **State monopoly**: Linen for temples, royal court, trade [3]
- **Workers**: Corvée labor, specialized weavers
- **Products**: Fine linen, mummy wrappings, sailcloth

### Aegean (Minoan/Mycenaean)
- **Linear B** (Pylos, Knossos): Detailed records
- **Workgroups**: 10–50 women per supervisor
- **Rations**: Grain, figs, wool per woman/month
- **Specialization**: Carders, spinners, weavers, finishers [4]
- **Trade**: Textiles for metals, ivory, ostrich eggs

### Anatolia (Hittite)
- **Royal workshops**: Hattusa, regional centers
- **Tribute**: Textiles from vassal states
- **Craftsmen**: "Weaver" as professional title

## Workflow & Specialization

### Production Steps
| Stage | Workers | Tools | Output |
|-------|---------|-------|--------|
| **Wool sorting** | Sorters | Hands, tables | Graded fleeces |
| **Washing/Scouring** | Washers | Vats, alkali | Clean wool |
| **Carding/Combing** | Carders | Hand cards, combs | Roving/top |
| **Spinning** | Spinners (many) | Drop spindle, whorl | Yarn |
| **Plying** | Plyers | Spindle/wheel | Plied yarn |
| **Warping** | Warpers | Warping frame | Warp beam |
| **Weaving** | Weavers | Warp-weighted/two-beam loom | Cloth |
| **Finishing** | Fullers, dyers | Fulling vat, dye vats | Finished cloth |

### Labor Organization
- **Rationed workers**: Monthly grain, wool, oil allocations
- **Quotas**: Daily/weekly production targets
- **Skill grades**: Apprentice, journeyman, master
- **Gender**: Predominantly female (spinning, weaving)
- **Children**: Spinning, carding (from age 6–8)

## Standardization & Quality Control

### Technical Standards
- **Thread count**: Threads/cm (warp × weft)
- **Weight**: Grams per square meter
- **Dimensions**: Standard lengths/widths
- **Twist**: Z/S, singles/plied

### Quality Marks
- **Seal impressions**: On bale wrappings
- **Workshop identity**: Supervisor seal
- **Grade**: Royal, temple, common

## Economic Role

### Textiles as Wealth
- **Storage**: Palace magazines (thousands of units)
- **Tribute**: Cloth demanded from provinces/vassals
- **Diplomatic gifts**: Royal garments, luxury cloth
- **Trade**: Wool textiles = silver, copper, lapis, timber

### Textiles as Currency
- **Units**: "Garment" (túg), "bolt" (gú)
- **Values**: Fixed in silver equivalents
- **Wages**: Paid in cloth rations

## Archaeological Evidence

### Textual
- **Ur III**: 100,000+ tablets on textile administration
- **Linear B**: 2,000+ tablets on textile production
- **Amarna letters**: Diplomatic textile gifts
- **Egyptian tomb records**: Linen inventories

### Material
- **Loom weights**: Thousands at palace sites
- **Spindle whorls**: Standardized weights
- **Dye vats**: At workshop areas
- **Sealings**: On textile bales

### Preserved Textiles
- **Egypt**: Royal linen, thousands of meters
- **Hallstatt**: Wool, dyed, complex weaves
- **Pazyryk**: Felt, pile carpet, embroidery
