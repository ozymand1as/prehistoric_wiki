# Textile Tools Archaeology

The archaeological identification of textile production relies almost entirely on tools—spindle whorls, loom weights, needles, and combs—since textiles themselves rarely preserve. These artifacts provide the primary evidence for ancient textile technologies.

## Overview

Textile tools are among the most common artifacts at Neolithic and Bronze Age sites. Their distribution, standardization, and context reveal the scale, organization, and social role of textile production.

## Key Tool Types

### Spindle Whorls (Most Common)
- **Function**: Weight for drop spindle, maintains rotation
- **Materials**: Clay (fired/unfired), stone, bone, wood, metal
- **Shapes**: Discoid, spherical, biconical, truncated cone
- **Weight range**: 5-50g (fine to coarse thread)
- **Diagnostic**: Perforation diameter, weight, decoration

### Loom Weights
- **Function**: Tension warp threads on warp-weighted loom
- **Materials**: Fired clay, stone, unbaked clay
- **Shapes**: Discoid, pyramidal, truncated pyramid, spool
- **Weight range**: 100-500g typical
- **Groups**: Rows = loom position; clusters = storage

### Needles & Awls
- **Needles**: Eyed, for sewing (bone, bronze, iron)
- **Awls**: Un-eyed, for piercing (bone, antler, bronze)
- **Context**: Domestic, burial, workshop

### Combs & Beaters
- **Weaving combs**: Push weft into place (bone, wood)
- **Beaters/swords**: Heavy, for tight weaves (bone, wood, bronze)
- **Hackles**: Iron spikes for fiber prep (Iron Age+)

### Shuttles & Bobbins
- **Shuttles**: Carry weft (wood, bone - rarely preserved)
- **Bobbins/pirns**: Hold weft thread (clay, bone)
- **Evidence**: Ceramic "shuttles" in Bronze Age Near East

## Archaeological Analysis Methods

### Spatial Analysis
- **Intrasite distribution**: Household vs workshop areas
- **Activity areas**: Spinning (whorls), weaving (loom weights rows)
- **Craft specialization**: Concentration in specific structures

### Quantitative Analysis
- **Weight distributions**: Whorl weight = thread type
- **Standardization**: Coefficient of variation (CV)
- **Ratios**: Whorls:loom weights = spinning:weaving labor

### Use-Wear Analysis
- **Whorl perforation**: Thread friction polish
- **Loom weight suspension**: Cord wear
- **Needle tip**: Fabric penetration polish
- **Awl tip**: Leather/fabric piercing wear

## Contextual Interpretation

### Household Production
- **Few whorls** (1-5 per house)
- **Scattered loom weights**
- **Domestic context**: Living floors, storage

### Workshop Production
- **Many whorls** (dozens per room)
- **Loom weight rows** (in situ loom positions)
- **Specialized structures**: Large rooms, storage
- **Administrative artifacts**: Sealings, tablets nearby

### Ritual/Symbolic
- **Burial goods**: Whorls, needles in graves (gender marker)
- **Votive deposits**: Miniature tools
- **Foundation deposits**: Tools in walls/floors

## Regional Patterns

### Neolithic Europe
- **Clay whorls**: Discoid, decorated
- **Loom weights**: Pyramidal, unbaked clay
- **LBK**: Standardized weights across 1,000 km

### Near East
- **Clay whorls**: High standardization (state control?)
- **Loom weights**: Less common (two-beam loom)
- **Seal impressions**: On whorls/weights

### Egypt
- **Wooden tools**: Preserved in tombs
- **Spinning bowls**: For supported spindle
- **Models**: Wooden workshop scenes

### Central Asia
- **Stone whorls**: Mountain regions
- **Supported spindle**: Bowl + spindle
- **Felt tools**: Beaters, rollers

### Andes
- **Ceramic whorls**: Decorated, varied forms
- **Backstrap loom**: No loom weights
- **Bone tools**: Needles, awls, battens

## Experimental Insights

### Production Rates
- **Spinning**: 20-50 m/hour (drop spindle, experienced)
- **Weaving**: 0.5-2 m²/hour (warp-weighted loom)
- **Tool life**: Whorls last years; loom weights decades

### Skill Identification
- **Novice**: Uneven twist, broken threads
- **Expert**: Consistent, fast, minimal waste
- **Apprenticeship**: Years to proficiency
