# Spinning

Spinning is the process of drawing out and twisting fibers into continuous thread or yarn. It is the essential precursor to weaving, transforming loose fibers into a strong, continuous strand suitable for textile production.

## Overview

The invention of spinning transformed fiber processing from simple twisting by hand to a mechanical process capable of producing vast quantities of consistent thread. The spindle and later the spinning wheel represent key technological milestones.

## Spinning Methods

### Hand Spinning (Earliest)
- **Thigh spinning**: Rolling fibers on thigh
- **Hand spindle**: Drop spindle, supported spindle
- **Period**: Neolithic (~7,000 BCE) to present

### Drop Spindle (Most Common Ancient)
- **Components**: Shaft (wood/bone), whorl (clay/stone/wood)
- **Mechanism**: Gravity provides twist, spindle drops
- **Varieties**: Top whorl, bottom whorl, center whorl
- **Speed**: Slow but portable

### Supported Spindle
- **Support**: Bowl, ground, or stand
- **Advantage**: Finer threads, less strain
- **Regions**: Central Asia, Middle East, Russia

### Spinning Wheel (Medieval+)
- **Great wheel** (walking wheel): Horizontal drive, one hand drafts
- **Saxony wheel** (flyer wheel): Vertical, treadle, continuous
- **Charkha** (India): Horizontal, hand-cranked, cotton
- **Period**: China (Song), Europe (13th c.), India (medieval)

## Fiber Preparation

| Step | Process | Tools |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Retting** | Microbial separation of fibers | Water, dew |
| **Breaking** | Crush woody stems | Flax break |
| **Scutching** | Remove woody parts | Scutching knife |
| **Hackling/Combing** | Align fibers, remove short | Hackle, combs |
| **Carding** | Disentangle, align (wool) | Hand cards, drum carder |

## Spindle Whorls (Primary Archaeological Evidence)

### Materials
- **Clay**: Most common, fired/unfired
- **Stone**: Steatite, limestone, sandstone
- **Bone/Antler**: Perforated epiphyses
- **Wood**: Rarely preserved
- **Metal**: Bronze, lead (late)

### Typology
- **Discoid**: Flat, circular (Neolithic Europe)
- **Spherical**: Round (Near East)
- **Biconical**: Two cones base-to-base
- **Perforated**: Center hole for shaft

### Weight & Diameter
- **Light (5-15g)**: Fine threads (linen, silk)
- **Medium (15-30g)**: General purpose (wool)
- **Heavy (30-50g+)**: Thick threads, plying

## Spinning Mechanics

### Z-Twist vs S-Twist
- **Z-twist**: Clockwise (right-handed), dominant in Europe
- **S-twist**: Counter-clockwise, dominant in Egypt, Andes
- **Plying**: Opposite twist to singles (Z singles → S ply)

### Thread Structure
- **Singles**: Single twisted strand
- **Plied**: 2+ singles twisted together
- **Cabled**: Plied yarns plied again

## Regional Variations

### Europe (Neolithic-Bronze Age)
- Drop spindle, clay whorls
- Wool dominant after Neolithic
- Warp-weighted loom compatible

### Near East
- Supported spindle common
- Linen (flax) early, wool later
- High-status linen production

### Central Asia
- Supported spindle, metal whorls
- Nomadic, portable
- Sheep/goat/camel fiber

### Andes
- Drop spindle (puska), ceramic whorls
- Camelid fiber (llama, alpaca)
- S-twist dominant

### China
- Silk reeling (filament, not spun)
- Hemp/ramie spun on wheels (Han+)
- Cotton later (Song+)

## Archaeological Analysis

### Whorl Studies
- **Weight distribution**: Indicates thread type
- **Use-wear**: Shaft hole polish
- **Context**: Household vs workshop
- **Standardization**: State control (Bronze Age palaces)

### Experimental Data
- **Production rate**: 20-50 m/hour (drop spindle)
- **Fiber consumption**: ~100g wool → 100m thread
- **Skill curve**: Months to competency
