# European Textiles

European textile traditions developed from Neolithic flax cultivation through Bronze Age wool economies, with exceptional preservation in Alpine lake dwellings and Hallstatt salt mines providing detailed evidence. [1]

## Overview

Europe's textile history is characterized by the early adoption of flax (linen) in the Neolithic, the gradual introduction of wool in the Bronze Age, and regional diversification of weaving techniques. The Alpine lake dwellings and Hallstatt salt mines provide the world's best-preserved prehistoric textile assemblages.

## Neolithic (5,500–2,500 BCE)

### Flax Dominance
- **LBK** (5,500–4,500 BCE): Flax cultivation universal [2]
- **Tools**: Spindle whorls (clay), loom weights (clay, unbaked)
- **Weaves**: Plain tabby, basket weave
- **Garments**: Tunics, cloaks, simple construction

### Regional Variation
- **Central Europe (LBK)**: Standardized whorls/weights across 1,000 km [3]
- **Mediterranean**: Earlier wool, more diverse fibers
- **Northern Europe**: Later Neolithic, nettle, lime bast

### Alpine Lake Dwellings (4,000–800 BCE)
- **Preservation**: Waterlogged, anaerobic
- **Finds**: Complete textiles, tools, production debris [4]
- **Fibers**: Flax, wool, nettle, tree bast (lime, oak)
- **Techniques**: Tabby, twill (late), tablet weaving borders

## Bronze Age (2,300–800 BCE)

### Wool Economy Emergence
- **Early BA** (Únětice, 2,300–1,600 BCE): First woolly sheep
- **Middle BA** (Tumulus, 1,600–1,200 BCE): Wool dominant in graves
- **Late BA** (Urnfield, 1,200–800 BCE): Large-scale production

### Hallstatt Salt Mines (800–400 BCE)
- **Exceptional preservation**: 3,000+ fragments
- **Weaves**: Tabby, 2/2 twill, diamond twill, herringbone
- **Colors**: Madder red, woad blue, weld yellow, tannin brown
- **Garments**: Tunics, cloaks, leggings, caps, belts
- **Technique**: Warp-weighted loom, tablet weaving

### Regional Cultures
| Culture | Period | Textile Traits |
|---------|--------|----------------|
| **Únětice** | 2,300–1,600 BCE | Early wool, bronze tools |
| **Wessex** | 2,000–1,500 BCE | Gold-decorated, amber beads |
| **Tumulus** | 1,600–1,200 BCE | Wool, bronze pins/fibulae |
| **Urnfield** | 1,200–800 BCE | Cremation, textile wrappings |
| **Hallstatt** | 800–400 BCE | Complex twills, dyes, trade |

## Fiber & Weave Development

| Period | Primary Fiber | Weaves | Loom |
|--------|---------------|--------|------|
| **Early Neolithic** | Flax | Tabby, basket | Warp-weighted |
| **Late Neolithic** | Flax + wool | Tabby, simple twill | Warp-weighted |
| **Early Bronze Age** | Wool increasing | Tabby, 2/2 twill | Warp-weighted |
| **Late Bronze Age** | Wool dominant | Diamond twill, herringbone | Warp-weighted, two-beam |

## Trade & Exchange

### Imports
- **Tin**: Cornwall → Central Europe (textile trade?)
- **Amber**: Baltic → Mediterranean (textiles as return cargo)
- **Dyes**: Madder (Near East), woad (local), weld (local)

### Exports
- **Wool textiles**: Central Europe → Mediterranean
- **Felt**: Nomadic frontier trade
- **Tablet-woven bands**: High-value trim

## Social Organization

### Gender
- **Spinning/weaving**: Predominantly female (graves, iconography)
- **Tools in female graves**: Whorls, weights, needles
- **Male involvement**: Shearing, dyeing, trade

### Status
- **Diamond twill**: High-status (Hallstatt)
- **Dyed cloth**: Madder, woad = wealth
- **Gold/amber decoration**: Elite only
