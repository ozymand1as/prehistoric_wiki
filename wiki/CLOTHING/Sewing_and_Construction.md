# Sewing and Construction

Sewing—the joining of flexible materials using thread and needle—enabled the creation of fitted, tailored garments essential for cold-climate survival and complex clothing design.

## Overview

The invention of the eyed needle (~40,000 BCE) marks the beginning of true sewing technology. Before this, hides were draped, tied, or pegged. Sewing allowed multi-piece garments with shaped seams, closures, and decorative details.

## Needles & Tools

### Needle Evolution
| Period | Material | Eye Type | Regions |
|--------|----------|----------|---------|
| **Upper Paleolithic** | Bone, ivory, antler | Drilled/gouged | Europe, Siberia |
| **Neolithic** | Bone, copper (late) | Drilled | Near East, Europe |
| **Bronze Age** | Bronze, copper | Cast/drilled | Near East, Europe, China |
| **Iron Age** | Iron | Punched/drilled | Global |

### Needle Types
- **Fine needles**: Sewing, embroidery (1-2 mm eye)
- **Heavy needles**: Leather, sailcloth (3-5 mm)
- **Curved needles**: Upholstery, leather
- **Double-eyed**: Tapestry, shuttle needles
- **Awls**: Pre-punch holes (leather, thick fabric)

### Thread
- **Sinew**: Animal tendon, strong, shrinks wet (Paleolithic)
- **Plant fiber**: Flax, hemp, nettle (Neolithic+)
- **Wool**: Spun yarn (Bronze Age+)
- **Silk**: Filament (China, later)
- **Gut**: Marine mammal intestine (Arctic waterproof)

## Stitch Types

### Construction Stitches
| Stitch | Description | Use |
|--------|-------------|-----|
| **Running** | In-and-out, even | Seams, gathering |
| **Backstitch** | Overlapping, strong | Stress seams |
| **Whip/Overcast** | Edge finishing | Prevent fraying |
| **Flat-felled** | Enclosed raw edges | Durable seams (jeans) |
| **French** | Enclosed, narrow | Sheer fabrics |
| **Lapped** | Overlapped edges | Leather, heavy cloth |

### Decorative/Functional
- **Blanket/Buttonhole**: Edge reinforcement
- **Chain**: Embroidery, stretch seams
- **Cross-stitch**: Counted embroidery
- **Couching**: Laid threads secured
- **Smocking**: Gathered elasticity

## Seam Engineering

### Seam Types
| Type | Structure | Application |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Plain** | Right sides together | General |
| **Flat-felled** | Folded, double-stitched | Jeans, workwear |
| **French** | Enclosed raw edges | Lingerie, sheers |
| **Lapped** | Overlapped, stitched | Leather, yokes |
| **Princess** | Curved, shaped | Fitted bodices |
| **Gusset** | Diamond insert | Crotch, underarm |

### Shaping Techniques
- **Darts**: Triangular folds for 3D shape
- **Gathering**: Fullness control
- **Pleats**: Structured fullness
- **Tucks**: Decorative/functional
- **Easing**: Distribute fullness

## Garment Construction Methods

### Drape & Cut (Traditional)
- **Rectangular construction**: Tunics, shirts (minimal waste)
- **Zero-waste**: Geometric pieces (kimono, chiton)

### Pattern & Tailoring (Advanced)
- **Block/sloper**: Basic fitted template
- **Grading**: Size scaling
- **Draping**: Fabric on form

## Closures & Fasteners

| Type | Period | Materials |
|------|--------|-----------|
| **Ties/Laces** | Paleolithic+ | Leather, sinew, cord |
| **Toggle buttons** | Neolithic+ | Bone, antler, wood |
| **Pins/Fibulae** | Bronze Age+ | Bronze, iron, gold |
| **Hooks & eyes** | Medieval+ | Metal |
| **Buttons (shank)** | Medieval+ | Bone, metal, cloth |
| **Lacing rings** | Medieval+ | Metal, leather |
| **Zippers** | 1893+ | Metal, plastic |

## Leather Sewing (Specialized)

### Techniques
- **Glover's stitch**: Three-sided, hides edge
- **Saddle stitch**: Two needles, single thread, locked
- **Baseball stitch**: Decorative, strong
- **Running (glover's)**: Single needle, through holes

### Tools
- **Awls**: Diamond, round, curved
- **Stitching horse**: Clamp work
- **Pliers**: Pull needle through tough leather
- **Edge creaser**: Mark stitch line

## Archaeological Evidence

### Preserved Garments
- **Ötzi** (3,300 BCE): Leggings, loincloth, coat, shoes - complex construction
- **Hallstatt** (800-400 BCE): Wool tunics, twill, tailored
- **Egyptian tombs**: Linen tunics, pleated, beaded
- **Pazyryk** (4th c. BCE): Felt, leather, fur, embroidery
- **Tarim mummies** (2000 BCE): Plaid twill, tailored trousers

### Tools
- **Needles**: Bone, ivory, bronze, iron
- **Thimbles**: Bone, bronze, leather
- **Shears**: Bronze, iron (Roman+)
- **Measuring**: Cords, rods
