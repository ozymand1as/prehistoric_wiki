# Asian Neolithic Technology

The Asian Neolithic encompasses multiple independent centers of agricultural innovation across East Asia, South Asia, Southeast Asia, and Central Asia. China alone hosts two primary domestication centers (Yellow River millet, Yangtze River rice) plus diverse regional traditions. Key technologies include millet/rice agriculture, sophisticated pottery (painted, cord-marked, black burnished), jade working, lacquerware, rammed-earth architecture, and early silk production. [1]

## Overview

The Asian Neolithic encompasses multiple independent centers of agricultural innovation across East Asia, South Asia, Southeast Asia, and Central Asia. China alone hosts two primary domestication centers (Yellow River millet, Yangtze River rice) plus diverse regional traditions. Key technologies include millet/rice agriculture, sophisticated pottery (painted, cord-marked, black burnished), jade working, lacquerware, rammed-earth architecture, and early silk production.

## History & Development

- **Early Neolithic (~10,000–7,000 BCE):** Earliest pottery worldwide (Xianrendong, Yuchanyan ~18,000–16,000 BCE); millet cultivation at Cishan/Peiligang (Yellow River, ~8,000 BCE); rice cultivation at Shangshan/Hemudu (Yangtze, ~7,000–6,000 BCE); Jōmon pottery in Japan (~14,500 BCE, pre-agricultural) [2]
- **Middle Neolithic (~7,000–5,000 BCE):** Yangshao culture (painted pottery, semi-subterranean houses); Majiabang/Hemudu (rice paddies, lacquerware, stilt houses); Mehrgarh Phase I (wheat/barley, mud-brick, early dentistry); Jeitun (Central Asia, wheat/barley, copper beads)
- **Late Neolithic (~5,000–3,000 BCE):** Longshan culture (black burnished pottery, rammed-earth walls, divination bones); Liangzhu (jade cong/bi, hydraulic engineering, social stratification); Dawenkou (emerging elite burials); Jeulmun (Korea, comb-pattern pottery, millet/rice)
- **Chalcolithic/Early Bronze Transition (~3,000–2,000 BCE):** Qijia (copper/bronze, horses); Erlitou (early bronze, palace foundations); Harappan Early Phase (urban planning, seals); Southeast Asian bronze (Ban Chiang, ~2,000 BCE)

## Function & Use

- **Primary Use:** Intensive agriculture (millet, rice, wheat, barley, soy), food storage, textile production, ritual/ceremonial objects, defense/warfare [3]
- **Secondary Uses:** Long-distance trade (jade, obsidian, shells, salt), status signaling (elite burials, jade, lacquer), water management (paddies, canals, dams), record-keeping (oracle bones, early symbols)
- **Cultural Significance:** Rice/millet agriculture supported dense populations and early states; jade as cosmological symbol and elite marker; lacquer as luxury coating and adhesive; rammed earth enabled walls and foundations for urbanism; silk began as wild silk harvesting before domestication

## Construction & Technique

**Agriculture:**
- **Millet (foxtail, broomcorn):** Dry-farming on Loess Plateau; broadcast seeding; harvested with shell/stone knives [4]
- **Rice:** Wet paddy cultivation (Hemudu: wooden spades, bone hoe blades); transplanting seedlings; water control via dikes/canals (Liangzhu: large-scale hydraulic system)
- **Tools:** Stone/ bone spades, hoes, reaping knives (shell, stone, later bronze); grinding stones (saddle querns)

**Pottery:**
- **Early:** Coil-built, low-fired, cord-marked (Jōmon), red-slipped (Cishan/Peiligang) [5]
- **Yangshao:** Fine painted pottery (black/red geometric on buff); kiln firing ~1000°C
- **Longshan:** Wheel-thrown (fast wheel), black burnished (reduction firing), thin-walled "eggshell" pottery
- **Liangzhu:** High-fired gray/black pottery; ritual vessels (gui, dou)

**Jade Working:**
- Nephrite jade from Xinjiang (Khotan) and local sources [6]
- Techniques: sawing (string + abrasive), drilling (hollow bamboo/tube + sand), grinding, polishing
- Forms: *cong* (tubes), *bi* (discs), *yue* (axes), *huang* (arcs), animal pendants; Liangzhu elite burials with hundreds of jades

**Lacquerware:**
- Sap from *Toxicodendron vernicifluum* (lacquer tree); applied in layers, each dried in humidity-controlled pits [7]
- Red (cinnabar) and black (carbon) pigments; used on wood, bamboo, pottery, textiles
- Hemudu: lacquered bowls, combs (~5,000 BCE); Liangzhu: lacquered coffins, vessels

**Architecture:**
- **Semi-subterranean houses (Yangshao):** Circular/rectangular, plastered floors, central hearth [8]
- **Stilt houses (Hemudu, Majiabang, Southeast Asia):** Pile dwellings over wetlands; mortise-and-tenon joinery
- **Rammed earth (Longshan, Liangzhu, Erlitou):** Layered compressed earth for walls, platforms, city walls (up to 10 m thick)
- **Walled settlements:** Chengtoushan (earliest walled town, ~4,500 BCE), Liangzhu (hydraulic city, ~3,300 BCE)

**Textiles:**
- **Silk:** *Bombyx mori* domestication from wild *B. mandarina*; earliest silk fragments at Jiahu (~6,500 BCE), Qingtai (~4,000 BCE); reeling, throwing, weaving on frame looms [9]
- **Hemp/Ramie:** Bast fiber processing (retting, scraping, spinning); widespread before silk
- **Bone needles, spindle whorls, loom weights** common at Neolithic sites

## Regional Variations

- **Yellow River (North China):** Millet-based; Peiligang/Cishan (early), Yangshao (painted pottery), Longshan (black pottery, walls), Erlitou (early bronze); loess cave dwellings → rammed earth
- **Yangtze River (South China):** Rice-based; Shangshan (earliest rice, ~8,000 BCE), Hemudu (stilt houses, lacquer, ~5,000 BCE), Liangzhu (jade, hydraulics, social hierarchy, ~3,300 BCE)
- **Japan (Jōmon):** World's earliest pottery (~14,500 BCE); hunter-gatherer complexity; cord-marked pottery, lacquer, jade beads, pit houses; no agriculture until Yayoi (~900 BCE)
- **Korea (Jeulmun/Chulmun):** Comb-pattern pottery; millet/rice adoption ~3,500 BCE; dolmens (megaliths) in Late Neolithic/Bronze Age
- **South Asia (Mehrgarh, Indus Valley):** Wheat/barley from Near East (~7,000 BCE); mud-brick, early dentistry (drilling), figurines, beads; precursor to Harappan Civilization
- **Southeast Asia:** Hoabinhian (broad-spectrum foraging) → rice millet ~4,000 BCE (Banyan Valley, Khao Wong); cord-marked pottery; stilt houses; bronze at Ban Chiang (~2,000 BCE)
- **Central Asia:** Jeitun (wheat/barley, mud-brick, ~6,000 BCE), Anau (copper beads, ~4,500 BCE); interaction with Steppe and Iranian plateau

## Uncertainties & Research Questions

- Single vs. multiple origins of rice domestication (Yangtze vs. multiple centers)?
- Nature of Jōmon complexity: affluent foragers or incipient cultivators?
- Timeline and mechanism of wheat/barley spread from Near East to China (Hexi Corridor vs. Steppe route)?
- Liangzhu hydraulic system: state-level engineering or communal labor?
- Earliest silk: wild silk harvesting vs. domesticated sericulture at Jiahu (~6,500 BCE)?
