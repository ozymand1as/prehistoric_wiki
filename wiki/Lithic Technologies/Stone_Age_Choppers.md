# Stone Age Choppers

---
**Period:** Paleolithic (Lower to Upper)
**Region(s):** Europe, Africa, Asia
**Culture(s):** Oldowan, Acheulean, Mousterian, Magdalenian
**Time Range:** 3.3 million years ago to 12,000 BCE
**Confidence:** High
**Evidence Type:** Material

## Overview
Stone Age choppers are simple hand axes and chopping tools made from flaked stone, dating back over 3 million years. They represent some of the earliest stone tool technologies developed by hominins, particularly during the Lower Paleolithic period. Choppers were essential for butchering animals, processing plant materials, and general survival tasks.

## History & Development

### Oldowan Industry (~3.3 million - 2.6 million BCE)
- Earliest known stone tools from Lomekwi 3 site in Kenya
- Simple flakes and crude chopping tools
- Mode 1 technology

### Acheulean Industry (~2.6 million - 100,000 BCE)
- More refined biconvex blade tools
- Bilateral symmetry and controlled stone fracture
- Magdalenian period in Upper Paleolithic showed advanced retouching techniques

### Upper Paleolithic Innovations
- Standardized microliths
- Sophisticated retouching techniques
- Abstract symbolic marking on tools

## Function & Use
- **Primary Use:** Butchering, wood processing, general survival
- **Secondary Uses:** Cutting plant materials, digging, general utility
- **Cultural Significance:** Early evidence of cognitive development and tool standardization

## Construction & Technique
- **Materials:** Various stone types with conchoidal fracture properties
- **Tools Required:** Simple stone hammer, soft stone or wood anvil
- **Skills Needed:** Stone knapping, conchoidal fracture understanding
- **Technique:** Direct percussion, indirect percussion, pressure flaking

## Regional Variations
- **Europe:** Acheulean and Magdalenian cultures, strong retouching emphasis
- **Africa:** Olduvai Gorge sequence shows earliest stone tool industries
- **Asia:** Diverse lithic traditions with advanced flaking techniques
- **Americas:** Clovis and Monte Verde cultures show distinct trajectories
