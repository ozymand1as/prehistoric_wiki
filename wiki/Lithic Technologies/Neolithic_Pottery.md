# Neolithic Pottery

---
**Period:** Neolithic
**Region(s):** Fertile Crescent, Yangtze River Valley, Mesoamerica
**Culture(s:** Early Neolithic, Pottery Neolithic
**Time Range:** 12,000 BCE to 2,000 BCE
**Confidence:** High
**Evidence Type:** Material

## Overview
Neolithic pottery represents a revolutionary technological development in human history. The invention of pottery (wood, pit, and later kiln firing) marked the transition from hunter-gatherer to settled agricultural societies. Pottery served dual purposes: food storage and social signaling, with distinct firing methods reflecting local fuel availability and technical knowledge.

## History & Development

### Early Pottery (~12,000 BCE)
- **Fertile Crescent:** First pottery firing in Epipalaeolithic West Asia
- **Techniques:** Wood firing, pit firing, later kiln firing
- **Materials:** Local clay, pigments

### Regional Development
- **Europe:** Pottery Neolithic phase
- **Yangtze River Valley:** Independent Neolithic Revolution
- **Mesoamerica:** Distinct pottery traditions

### Neolithic Revolution
- Introduction of farming
- Domestication of animals
- Transition from hunter-gatherer to settled lifestyle
- Permanent villages and towns

## Function & Use
- **Primary Use:** Food storage, cooking, brewing
- **Secondary Uses:** Social signaling, ritual objects, trade goods
- **Cultural Significance:** First technologies requiring resource management and long-term planning

## Construction & Technique
- **Materials:** Local clay, temper (if needed), pigments
- **Techniques:** Hand-building, coil pottery, potter's wheel (later)
- **Firing:** Wood firing, pit firing, kiln firing
- **Tools Required:** Clay, water, firing materials

## Regional Variations
- **Fertile Crescent:** Early adoption, widespread influence
- **Europe:** Pottery Neolithic phase, distinct regional styles
- **Mesoamerica:** Independent pottery development
- **China:** Yangtze River Valley independently developed pottery
