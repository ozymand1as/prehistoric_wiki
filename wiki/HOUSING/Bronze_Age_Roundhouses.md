# Bronze Age Roundhouses

The roundhouse is the signature dwelling of the British and Atlantic European Bronze and Iron Ages. These circular post-built structures with conical thatched roofs represent a remarkably stable architectural tradition lasting over 1,500 years, reflecting both environmental adaptation and social organization.

## Overview

Roundhouses emerge in the Middle Bronze Age (~1,500 BCE) as the dominant house form across Britain, Ireland, and western France. They replace earlier rectangular Neolithic and Early Bronze Age houses and persist through the Iron Age into the Roman period. Their circular form is structurally efficient, sheds wind, and accommodates extended family groups.

## Construction

### Post-Ring Foundation
- **Wall posts**: 10-30 upright timbers (oak, ash, hazel) in continuous ring trench or individual postholes
- **Diameter**: 5-15 m (typically 8-10 m)
- **Posthole depth**: 0.5-1.0 m
- **Spacing**: 0.5-1.0 m between posts
- **Entrance**: Wider gap (1.5-2 m), often with porch/porch posts

### Wall Construction
- **Wattle-and-daub**: Hazel/willow woven between posts, daubed both sides
- **Daub**: Clay + straw/dung + sand, 5-10 cm thick
- **Alternative**: Vertical planks (where oak abundant), stone footings + timber

### Roof Structure
- **Rafters**: 20-40 timbers from wall plate to central ring
- **Central ring**: Horizontal timber ring (not single post - avoids point load)
- **Purlins**: 3-5 horizontal rings lashed to rafters
- **Thatch laths**: Hazel/birch rods for thatch attachment
- **Pitch**: 45-50° (optimal for thatch runoff)

### Thatch Roofing
- **Materials**: Water reed (Phragmites), wheat straw, heather, bracken
- **Thickness**: 30-50 cm
- **Ridge**: Clay saddle, turf, or overlapping bundles
- **Overhang**: 50-100 cm beyond walls
- **Lifespan**: 15-25 years (reed), 10-15 years (straw)

### Internal Features
- **Central hearth**: Stone-lined or clay-rimmed, sometimes raised
- **Floor**: Trampled earth, sometimes chalk/clay laid
- **Drainage**: Gully around inner wall, soak-away pit
- **Sleeping platforms**: Raised earth benches around perimeter
- **Storage pits**: Under floor or in entrance passage
- **Loft**: Possible in larger houses (central ring supports)

## Regional Variants

| Region | Culture | Diameter | Distinctive Features |
|--------|---------|----------|---------------------|
| **Wessex (S. England)** | Wessex/MBA | 8-12 m | Large, massive posts, deep ring trenches |
| **Dartmoor** | MBA-LBA | 5-8 m | Stone footings (granite), peat thatch |
| **Scotland (Mainland)** | MBA-IA | 8-15 m | Ring-ditches, timber post-rings |
| **Scotland (Orkney/Shetland)** | BA-IA | 5-10 m | Stone walls (thick), corbelled cells |
| **Ireland** | BA-IA | 6-12 m | Post-ring, sometimes souterrain attached |
| **Brittany/Normandy** | Atlantic BA | 8-14 m | Similar to S. England, coastal adaptation |

## Settlement Patterns

### Enclosed Settlements
- **Hillforts** (Late BA/IA): Roundhouses inside ramparts
- **Ringworks**: Small enclosed farmsteads (1-3 houses)
- **Palisaded enclosures**: Timber fence, ditch

### Open Settlements
- **Dispersed farmsteads**: 1-2 houses, fields around
- **Nucleated villages**: 5-20 houses (rare in BA, common in IA)
- **Linear arrangements**: Along ridges, valleys, coasts

## Experimental Reconstructions

| Site | Location | Findings |
|------|----------|----------|
| **Butser Ancient Farm** | Hampshire, UK | Long-term testing since 1970s; smoke, durability, thermal |
| **St Fagans** | Cardiff, Wales | Iron Age roundhouse, public demonstration |
| **The Crannog Centre** | Loch Tay, Scotland | Lake dwelling variant |
| **Flag Fen** | Peterborough, UK | Bronze Age platform + roundhouse |
| **Navan Fort** | Armagh, N. Ireland | Large ceremonial roundhouse (40 m) |

### Key Experimental Results
- **Thermal**: 10-15°C above external in winter (hearth only)
- **Smoke**: Managed by high roof, door control, roof vent (no chimney)
- **Air quality**: CO acceptable with proper door management
- **Durability**: Posts rot at ground level (20-40 yr); thatch 15-25 yr
- **Labor**: 2,000-4,000 person-hours for 10 m house

## Social Organization

### Household Unit
- **Occupants**: 10-20 people (extended family)
- **Economic unit**: Self-sufficient mixed farming
- **Gendered space**: Hearth/female, entrance/male (ethnographic analogy)
- **Life cycle**: Birth, marriage, death within house

### Symbolic Dimensions
- **Orientation**: Entrance typically SE-E (sunrise, avoiding prevailing SW winds)
- **Deposits**: Foundation offerings (pottery, querns, animals) in postholes
- **Abandonment**: Deliberate dismantling, burning, structured deposition
- **Continuity**: Rebuilding on same footprint (overlapping postholes)

## Archaeological Identification

### Primary Evidence
- **Ring of postholes** (circular/oval, 5-15 m)
- **Central hearth** (stone/clay, often with rake-out)
- **Ring ditch/gully** (drainage, ~1 m outside posts)
- **Entrance gap** (wider, often with porch postholes)
- **Internal features**: Pits, postholes (loft supports), partitions

### Preservation Biases
- **Upland**: Stone footings survive (Dartmoor, Scotland)
- **Lowland**: Only postholes, ditches (plough truncation)
- **Waterlogged**: Exceptional (Flag Fen, Glastonbury, crannogs)
- **Aerial photography**: Cropmarks of ring ditches

## Evolution & Continuity

| Period | Form | Key Changes |
|--------|------|-------------|
| **Neolithic** | Rectangular longhouses | Post-and-beam, three-aisled |
| **Early BA** | Rectangular/oval | Smaller, single-aisled |
| **Middle BA** | **Roundhouse emerges** | Post-ring, conical roof |
| **Late BA** | Standardized roundhouse | Ring ditches, enclosures |
| **Early IA** | Larger roundhouses | More posts, internal rings |
| **Middle IA** | Complex roundhouses | Multiple rings, annexes |
| **Late IA** | "Developed" roundhouses | Stone footings (some), sophisticated |
| **Roman** | Roundhouse + rectilinear | Hybrid, then rectangular dominates |
