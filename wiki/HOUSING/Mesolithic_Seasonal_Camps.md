# Mesolithic Seasonal Camps

Mesolithic seasonal camps represent the logistical mobility of hunter-gatherers tracking resources across the landscape. Unlike base camps with substantial huts, these camps feature light, rapidly erected shelters for short-term specialized activities. [1]

## Overview

Seasonal camps were occupied for days to weeks during resource peaks: fish runs, nut harvests, bird migrations, hunting seasons. Their archaeology is subtle—hearths, artifact scatters, postholes for light structures.

## Camp Types

### Fishing Camps
- **Location**: River mouths, lake shores, coasts
- **Shelter**: Lean-tos, bark shelters, dugouts
- **Duration**: Weeks (seasonal runs)
- **Features**: Fish processing areas, drying racks [2]

### Hunting Camps
- **Location**: Game trails, water sources, high ground
- **Shelter**: Lean-tos, hide tents, rock shelters
- **Duration**: Days to weeks
- **Features**: Butchery areas, tool maintenance

### Gathering Camps
- **Location**: Nut groves, berry patches, tuber fields [3]
- **Shelter**: Windbreaks, lean-tos
- **Duration**: Days to weeks
- **Features**: Processing pits, storage pits

### Coastal/Shellfish Camps
- **Location**: Rocky shores, estuaries
- **Shelter**: Rock shelters, driftwood lean-tos
- **Features**: Shell middens, hearths, steaming pits

## Shelter Construction

### Lean-To (Most Common)
- **Frame**: Ridge pole on two forked uprights
- **Roofing**: Bark slabs, thatch, hides
- **Orientation**: Back to prevailing wind
- **Construction time**: 1-2 hours

### Windbreak
- **Structure**: Row of stakes, brush/bark fill
- **Function**: Wind protection for hearth
- **Minimal investment**: Minutes to build

### Platform Shelter
- **Base**: Raised platform (logs, brush)
- **Floor**: Above damp/cold ground
- **Superstructure**: Lean-to or dome

## Archaeological Visibility

### Ephemeral Traces
- **Hearths**: Stone-lined, clay-rimmed, simple pits
- **Postholes**: Few, shallow, widely spaced
- **Artifact scatters**: Knapping debris, tool maintenance
- **Food remains**: Bone, shell, charred plant

### Site Identification
- **Palynology**: Local vegetation disturbance
- **Phytoliths**: Plant processing evidence
- **Micromorphology**: Trampled floors, hearth rake-out
- **GIS modeling**: Least-cost paths to resources

## Seasonal Round (Example: Ertebølle, Denmark)

| Season | Location | Activity | Shelter |
|--------|----------|----------|---------|
| **Spring** | River/estuary | Fish runs, bird migration | Lean-tos, bark huts |
| **Summer** | Coast/islands | Seal hunting, shellfish | Rock shelters, driftwood lean-tos |
| **Autumn** | Inland forests | Nut gathering, hunting | Platform huts, lean-tos |
| **Winter** | Base camp (inland) | Hunting, trapping | Substantial huts (post-built) |
