# Housing & Shelter Technologies
**Period:** Paleolithic to Bronze Age  
**Region(s):** Global  
**Culture(s):** All known prehistoric cultures  
**Time Range:** ~2.6 million years ago to 1,200 BCE  
**Confidence:** Variable by topic  
**Evidence Type:** Material, Archaeological, Architectural, Experimental

## Overview
Shelter is a fundamental human technology. From simple windbreaks to complex multi-room dwellings, housing technologies reflect environmental adaptation, social organization, and technological capability. The archaeological record preserves shelters through postholes, foundations, hearths, and occasionally exceptional preservation of organic materials.

## Key Sites
- **Terra Amata** (France) - ~400,000 BCE, possible hut foundations
- **Dolní Věstonice** (Czech) - ~26,000 BCE, mammoth bone structures
- **Kostenki/Mezhirich** (Russia) - ~20,000 BCE, multiple bone hut complexes
- **Howick House** (UK) - ~7,600 BCE, well-preserved Mesolithic post house
- **Star Carr** (UK) - ~9,000 BCE, earliest British house, platform
- **Jericho PPNA** (Palestine) - ~9,500 BCE, round mudbrick houses, tower
- **Çatalhöyük** (Turkey) - ~7,100 BCE, dense mudbrick, roof entry
- **LBK Longhouses** (Central Europe) - ~5,500 BCE, 20-45m three-aisled
- **Alpine Pile Dwellings** (Alps) - ~4,000 BCE, preserved organic architecture
- **Skara Brae** (Orkney) - ~3,100 BCE, stone furniture, drainage
- **Mycenae/Tiryns** (Greece) - ~1,400 BCE, megaron palaces, cyclopean walls
- **Nuraghe Su Nuraxi** (Sardinia) - ~1,500 BCE, corbelled tower complex
