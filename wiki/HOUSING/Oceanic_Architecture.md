# Oceanic Architecture

Oceanic architecture encompasses the diverse building traditions of the Pacific Islands, from Lapita stilt houses to Polynesian meeting houses and Micronesian latte stone structures. Adapted to tropical marine environments, these traditions share a reliance on timber, thatch, and coral stone. [1]

## Overview

Pacific architecture is shaped by the marine environment: cyclone-resistant designs, elevated floors for flood protection, and materials sourced from forest and reef. Despite vast distances, shared Austronesian heritage creates underlying similarities.

## Regional Traditions

### Polynesia

#### Meeting Houses (Fale, Whare, Fare)
- **Samoa (Fale tele)**: Oval, domed roof, no walls (open), central posts [2]
- **Tonga (Fale fakatu'i)**: Rectangular, raised platform, curved roof
- **Hawaii (Hale)**: Thatch, pili grass, stone platforms (heiau)
- **New Zealand (Wharenui)**: Carved, painted, ancestral embodiment
- **Construction**: Lashed timber frame, thatch (pandanus, grass), no nails

#### Domestic Houses
- **Fale o'o (Samoa)**: Small, thatched, raised floor [3]
- **Hale pili (Hawaii)**: Grass-thatched, stone foundation
- **Materials**: Timber posts, pandanus/grass thatch, cordage lashing

### Melanesia

#### Men's Houses (Haus Tambaran, Haus Boi)
- **Sepik River (PNG)**: Towering gables, carved posts, painted facades [4]
- **Highlands (PNG)**: Round, woven bamboo, thatch, earth floor
- **Vanuatu/Fiji**: Bure (Fiji), nakamal (Vanuatu) - community centers

#### Domestic
- **Stilt houses**: Coastal, riverine, flood adaptation
- **Materials**: Bamboo, sago palm, pandanus, mangrove timber

### Micronesia

#### Latte Stone Structures (Mariana Islands)
- **Latte**: Two-piece stone pillars (haligi + tasa)
- **Function**: House supports, ceremonial platforms
- **Period**: 800–1700 CE
- **Construction**: Quarried limestone, coral

#### Traditional Houses
- **Raised**: Post-and-beam, thatch roof
- **Materials**: Breadfruit wood, pandanus thatch, coconut cordage

## Lapita Architecture (1500–500 BCE)
- **Stilt houses**: Over lagoon/shore
- **Evidence**: Postholes, shell middens, dentate pottery
- **Settlements**: Small hamlets, coastal

## Construction Techniques

### Timber Framing
- **Posts**: Hardwood (breadfruit, ironwood, mahogany)
- **Joinery**: Lashed (coconut sennit, hibiscus bark), mortise-tenon (later)
- **No nails**: Pre-contact entirely lashed/pegged

### Thatching
- **Pandanus**: Durable, water-resistant (primary)
- **Coconut leaf**: Temporary, easy
- **Grass**: Miscanthus, Miscanthus (high islands)
- **Sago palm**: Lowlands, swamps

### Stonework
- **Coral limestone**: Quarried, faced
- **Basalt**: Volcanic islands (Hawaii, Samoa)
- **Latte stones**: Two-piece, quarried
- **Marae/Ahu**: Ceremonial platforms

## Environmental Adaptation

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| **Cyclones** | Low profile, aerodynamic, deep posts |
| **Flooding** | Stilt construction, raised floors |
| **Heat** | High ceilings, cross-ventilation, open walls |
| **Rain** | Steep pitch, wide overhangs, pandanus thatch |
| **Salt** | Durable woods (ironwood), coconut cordage |
