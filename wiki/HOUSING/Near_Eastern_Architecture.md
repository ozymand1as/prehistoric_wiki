# Near Eastern Architecture

Near Eastern architecture spans from the world's earliest mudbrick villages to the monumental palaces and ziggurats of the Bronze Age. The region pioneered rectilinear planning, mudbrick construction, and urban planning.

## Overview

The Near East saw the earliest transition to sedentary life and the invention of mudbrick (~9,500 BCE). Architecture evolved from round huts to rectangular houses, then to palaces, temples, and city walls, establishing patterns that influenced Mediterranean and European architecture.

## Neolithic (9,500–4,500 BCE)

### PPNA (9,500–8,500 BCE)
- **Jericho**: Round semi-subterranean houses, stone tower, perimeter wall
- **Göbekli Tepe**: Monumental stone circles (pre-domestication)
- **Architecture**: Communal before domestic

### PPNB (8,500–6,000 BCE)
- **Rectangular houses**: Mudbrick, lime plaster floors
- **Ain Ghazal**: Multi-room, plastered skulls under floors
- **Çatalhöyük** (7,100 BCE): Dense, roof-access, wall art

### Pottery Neolithic (6,000–4,500 BCE)
- **Hassuna/Samarra**: Mudbrick, painted pottery
- **Halaf**: Tholoi (circular), painted ware
- **Ubaid**: First temples, social differentiation

## Chalcolithic / Early Bronze Age (4,500–3,000 BCE)

### Uruk Period (4,000–3,100 BCE)
- **Urbanization**: First cities (Uruk >50 ha)
- **Temples**: Platform, tripartite, buttressed facades
- **Administration**: Cylinder seals, tablets, sealings
- **Materials**: Mudbrick, bitumen waterproofing

### Early Dynastic (2,900–2,350 BCE)
- **City-states**: Ur, Lagash, Kish
- **Palaces**: Courtyard, audience hall, archives
- **Ziggurats**: Stepped platforms, temple summits
- **Royal tombs**: Ur (Puabi), rich grave goods

## Middle/Late Bronze Age (2,000–1,200 BCE)

### Akkadian / Ur III (2,350–2,000 BCE)
- **Imperial administration**: Standardized architecture
- **Ur-Nammu ziggurat**: Best preserved
- **Bureaucratic records**: Building inscriptions

### Old Babylonian (2,000–1,600 BCE)
- **Hammurabi's Code**: Building regulations
- **House plans**: Courtyard, rooms around
- **Temples**: Esagila (Marduk), Etemenanki

### Assyrian (1,400–600 BCE)
- **Palaces**: Nimrud, Nineveh, Khorsabad
- **Lamassu**: Guardian colossi at gates
- **Reliefs**: Narrative wall panels
- **Aqueducts**: Jerwan (Sennacherib)

### Hittite Anatolia (1,650–1,200 BCE)
- **Hattusa**: Cyclopean walls, gates, temples
- **Rock reliefs**: Yazılıkaya
- **Posterns**: Corbelled gates
- **Materials**: Stone, timber, mudbrick

## Building Materials & Techniques

| Material | Period | Use |
|----------|--------|-----|
| **Mudbrick** | 9,500 BCE+ | Primary wall material |
| **Lime plaster** | PPNB+ | Floors, walls, waterproofing |
| **Bitumen** | Uruk+ | Waterproofing, mortar |
| **Stone** | Limited | Foundations, monuments |
| **Timber** | All periods | Roofs, doors, upper floors |

## Urban Planning
- **Temple-centered**: Early periods
- **Palace-centered**: Bronze Age
- **Walled cities**: Gates, towers, moats
- **Canals**: Transport, irrigation, defense
