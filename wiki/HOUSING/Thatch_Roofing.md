# Thatch Roofing

Thatch roofing uses dry vegetation—water reed, straw, heather, grasses—to create waterproof, insulating roof coverings. It is one of humanity's oldest and most widespread roofing technologies. [1]

## Overview

Thatch has been used from Paleolithic shelters to medieval great halls and continues in vernacular architecture worldwide. Materials and techniques vary by region based on available vegetation.

## Thatching Materials

| Material | Region | Properties | Lifespan |
|----------|--------|------------|----------|
| **Water reed (Phragmites)** | Europe, Asia, wetlands | Durable, water-resistant | 20-50 years |
| **Wheat straw** | Europe, temperate zones | Available, flexible | 15-25 years |
| **Rye straw** | Northern Europe | Durable, long stems | 20-30 years |
| **Heather (Calluna)** | Scotland, uplands | Durable, wind-resistant | 20-40 years |
| **Bracken (Pteridium)** | Uplands, acidic soils | Light, insulating | 10-15 years |
| **Rushes (Juncus)** | Wetlands | Flexible, available | 10-20 years |
| **Palm leaves** | Tropics | Large, waterproof | 5-10 years |
| **Banana leaves** | Tropics | Large, biodegradable | 1-3 years |

## Thatching Techniques

### Long Straw (Europe)
- **Material**: Wheat/rye straw, uncombed
- **Method**: Bundles laid, pinned with hazel spars [2]
- **Pitch**: 45-50°
- **Ridge**: Clay saddle or straw roll

### Combed Wheat Reed (England)
- **Material**: Wheat straw, combed (ears removed)
- **Method**: Similar to water reed
- **Appearance**: Smooth, uniform

### Water Reed (Norfolk, Netherlands, Danube)
- **Material**: Phragmites australis
- **Method**: Bundles tied to battens, dressed flush [3]
- **Lifespan**: 40-60 years (best)
- **Ridge**: Sedge grass or clay

### Heather & Bracken (Scotland, Uplands)
- **Material**: Heather, bracken, mixed
- **Method**: Turf underlay, heather thatch
- **Wind resistance**: Excellent

## Construction Details

### Roof Structure
- **Rafters**: 45-50° pitch (optimal for thatch)
- **Battens/Purlins**: Horizontal, spaced 30-40 cm
- **Spars/Ligatures**: Hazel/willow rods, twisted

### Layers
1. **Base coat**: Thin layer on battens
2. **Main coat**: Bundles overlapped, 30-50 cm thick
3. **Ridge**: Clay, turf, or decorative straw

### Fixings
- **Spars**: Hazel rods, twisted into staples
- **Sways**: Horizontal rods, tied to rafters
- **Crooks**: Metal hooks (modern)

## Performance

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| **Insulation (U-value)** | ~0.35 W/m²K (30 cm) |
| **Breathability** | High (moisture regulation) |
| **Fire risk** | Moderate (fire retardants used) |
| **Wind uplift** | Good (flexible, weighted) |
| **Weight** | 30-50 kg/m² (wet) |

## Maintenance

- **Inspection**: Annually
- **Re-ridging**: Every 10-15 years
- **Patching**: As needed
- **Full re-thatch**: 15-60 years (material dependent)

## Archaeological Evidence

- **Phytoliths**: Silica bodies from reeds/grasses
- **Charred thatch**: Fire-destroyed structures
- **Impressions**: In daub, clay
- **Tool marks**: Thatching needles, knives
