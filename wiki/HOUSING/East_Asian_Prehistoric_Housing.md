# East Asian Prehistoric Housing

East Asian housing traditions developed distinctive solutions for continental climates: semi-subterranean pit houses in the Neolithic, stilt houses in the south, and the early development of rammed earth (pisé) that would later become the Great Wall technique.

## Overview

East Asia's diverse environments—from Siberian taiga to subtropical coasts—produced varied but connected architectural traditions. Key innovations include the Jōmon pit house, Yangshao semi-subterranean dwellings, and the early use of rammed earth.

## Paleolithic

### Zhoukoudian (China, ~500,000–200,000 BCE)
- **Homo erectus**: "Peking Man"
- **Cave occupation**: Hearths, ash layers, stone tools
- **Debate**: Controlled fire evidence

### Japanese Paleolithic (~30,000 BCE)
- **Cave/rock shelter**: Limited evidence
- **Open-air**: Possible pit dwellings

## Jōmon Japan (14,000–300 BCE)

### Pit Houses (Early–Middle Jōmon)
- **Form**: Circular/oval, 3-10 m diameter
- **Depth**: 0.5-1.5 m
- **Posts**: 4-6 central + peripheral
- **Roof**: Conical, thatch/bark, central smoke hole
- **Entrance**: Tunnel or ladder through roof
- **Evolution**: Deeper over time, internal → peripheral hearths

### Later Jōmon
- **Larger**: Communal longhouses (30+ m)
- **Raised-floor**: Granaries (takakura)

## Neolithic China

### Yangshao (5,000–3,000 BCE)
- **Semi-subterranean**: Round/square, 0.5-1 m deep
- **Walls**: Plastered earth, wattle
- **Roof**: Thatch on pole frame
- **Hearth**: Central or wall
- **Storage**: Subfloor pits

### Longshan (3,000–2,000 BCE)
- **Rectangular**: Surface or shallow pit
- **Rammed earth**: Early pisé foundations/walls
- **Wells**: Deep, lined
- **Walls**: Perimeter walls (early urban)

### Hongshan (4,700–2,900 BCE, NE China)
- **Pit houses**: Semi-subterranean
- **Ritual centers**: Goddess temples, jade

## Korea

### Chulmun (4,000–1,500 BCE)
- **Pit houses**: Similar to Japan/China
- **Ondol precursor**: Subfloor heating (later)

### Mumun (1,500–300 BCE)
- **Surface houses**: Post-built, raised floor
- **Ondol**: Subfloor flue heating develops

## Bronze Age

### Erlitou (China, 1,900–1,500 BCE)
- **Palace complex**: Rammed earth foundations
- **Urban layout**: Grids, walls
- **Rammed earth**: Perfected for walls

### Shang (1,600–1,046 BCE)
- **Palaces**: Rammed earth platforms
- **Tombs**: Rammed earth chambers
- **Great Wall precursors**: Rammed earth walls
