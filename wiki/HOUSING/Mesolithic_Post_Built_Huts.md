# Mesolithic Post-Built Huts

Mesolithic post-built huts represent the earliest substantial evidence for wooden architecture in northern Europe. These circular or oval structures, identified by posthole patterns, mark a significant development in hunter-gatherer residential technology. [1]

## Overview

As the climate warmed and forests expanded after the Last Glacial Maximum, Mesolithic groups built more substantial, semi-permanent dwellings. The classic examples from Britain (Howick, Star Carr) and Scandinavia show sophisticated carpentry and long-term occupation.

## Key Sites

### Howick House (Northumberland, UK, ~7,600 BCE)
- **Structure**: 6 m diameter, 18 postholes
- **Construction**: Oak posts, central hearth
- **Occupation**: 100+ years, multiple rebuilds
- **Significance**: Oldest known house in Britain

### Star Carr (Yorkshire, UK, ~9,000 BCE)
- **Structures**: Multiple circular platforms
- **Construction**: Split timber platforms, post settings [2]
- **Environment**: Lake edge, peat preservation
- **Artifacts**: Antler frontlets, barbed points

### Mount Sandel (Northern Ireland, ~7,500 BCE)
- **Structure**: 6 m diameter, post ring
- **Hearth**: Central, clay-lined
- **Occupation**: Seasonal, repeated

### Friesack (Germany, ~6,000 BCE)
- **Preservation**: Waterlogged
- **Finds**: Plant fibers, nets, wooden artifacts
- **Structures**: Post-built huts

## Construction

### Post-Ring Design
- **Diameter**: 4-8 m typical
- **Posts**: 12-24, oak, pine, hazel
- **Depth**: 0.3-0.6 m postholes
- **Spacing**: 0.5-1.0 m

### Superstructure (Inferred)
- **Roof**: Conical, thatch/turf/bark
- **Walls**: Wattle, hide, split plank
- **Entrance**: South-east facing (dawn, away from wind) [3]

### Hearth
- **Central**: Stone-lined or clay-rimmed
- **Rake-out area**: Ash, charcoal spread
- **Function**: Heat, light, cooking, social focus

## Carpentry Evidence

### Tool Marks
- **Axes**: Ground stone, flint
- **Adzes**: For hollowing, shaping
- **Wedges**: For splitting timber
- **Chisels**: Bone, antler for detail

### Joints (Experimental)
- **Mortise & tenon**: Post to plate
- **Half-lap**: Plate connections
- **Pegged**: Wooden pins

## Settlement Pattern

### Base Camps
- **Duration**: Seasonal to multi-year
- **Location**: Lake/river edges, resource-rich
- **Activities**: Tool making, food processing, social [4]

### Logistical Camps
- **Duration**: Days to weeks
- **Light structures**: Windbreaks, lean-tos
- **Specialized**: Hunting, fishing, gathering

## Environmental Adaptation

### Temperature
- **Insulation**: Double walls, turf banking
- **Draft control**: Entrance passage, curtains
- **Ventilation**: Smoke hole, door control

### Moisture
- **Drainage**: Perimeter ditches
- **Floor**: Raised, gravel, sand
- **Roof pitch**: 45-50° for runoff
