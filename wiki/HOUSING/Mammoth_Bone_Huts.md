# Mammoth Bone Huts

Mammoth bone huts are circular or oval dwellings constructed from the bones and tusks of woolly mammoths. Found primarily on the Eastern European Plain, they represent a remarkable adaptation to the treeless mammoth steppe environment where wood was scarce but mammoth remains were abundant.

## Overview

These structures appear during the Last Glacial Maximum (~26,000-19,000 BCE) when human groups intensively hunted mammoths. The bones—mandibles, skulls, scapulae, pelvises, long bones, and especially tusks—were used as structural elements for dwellings that could house extended families through brutal winters.

## Construction

### Structural Elements
| Bone Element | Function | Typical Count per Hut |
|--------------|----------|----------------------|
| **Tusks** | Roof supports, entrance arches, perimeter | 10-100+ |
| **Mandibles** | Wall uprights, foundation ring | 20-60 |
| **Skulls** | Foundation blocks, corner posts | 5-20 |
| **Scapulae** | Wall infill, roofing | 10-30 |
| **Pelvises** | Wall infill, roofing | 10-30 |
| **Long bones** | Rafters, horizontal braces | 20-50 |
| **Vertebrae/Ribs** | Fill, insulation | Many |

### Building Sequence (Inferred)
1. **Foundation**: Circular trench or cleared area, skulls/mandibles as base ring
2. **Wall uprights**: Mandibles driven into ground, tusks as corner posts
3. **Wall infill**: Scapulae, pelvises, ribs stacked between uprights
4. **Roof frame**: Tusks and long bones as rafters to central point or ridge
5. **Covering**: Hides, turf, bark, bone sheets over frame
6. **Insulation**: Earth, snow, grass packed against walls
7. **Entrance**: Tusk arch, hide flap, sometimes tunnel entry

### Dimensions
- **Diameter**: 4-10 m (typically 5-7 m)
- **Floor area**: 15-50 m²
- **Height**: ~2-3 m (center)
- **Capacity**: 10-20 people (extended family)

## Mezhirich (Type Site)
- **4 huts** arranged in rough line
- **Hut 1**: 6 m diameter, 112 mammoths represented (min. 15 individuals)
- **Bone patterning**: Mandibles in herringbone, tusks radial
- **Internal features**: 5 hearths in Hut 1 (sequential), storage pits
- **Artifacts**: 20,000+ flint, bone tools, ivory ornaments, "venus" figurines
- **Economy**: Mammoth hunting, reindeer, horse, fur trapping

## Social Implications

### Labor Organization
- **Bone transport**: Moving 10+ ton of bone = group effort
- **Construction**: Coordinated building (days to weeks)
- **Hunting**: Communal mammoth drives or scavenging
- **Seasonality**: Winter occupation, summer dispersal?

### Symbolic Dimensions
- **Geometric arrangements**: Non-functional bone patterns
- **Ochre staining**: Bones colored red
- **Art objects**: Figurines, engraved bones in huts
- **Ritual**: Possible ceremonial aspect to construction

## Experimental Reconstructions
- **Mehrin (Ukraine)**: Full-scale replica at site
- **Archeon (Netherlands)**: Experimental hut
- **Butser Ancient Farm (UK)**: Mammoth bone structure
- **Findings**: Internal temp 15-20°C above external; smoke management critical; bone grease preserves bone; hides essential for weatherproofing

## Environment & Adaptation
- **Mammoth Steppe**: Treeless, cold, dry, productive grassland
- **Wood scarcity**: No trees = no timber construction
- **Bone abundance**: Mass mammoth mortality (natural + human)
- **Thermal efficiency**: Bone + hide + earth = excellent insulation
- **Mobility**: Semi-permanent (seasonal reoccupation)

## Comparison with Other Paleolithic Shelters

| Feature | Mammoth Bone Hut | Hide Tent | Cave/Rock Shelter |

| **Durability** | Years-decades | Seasonal | Permanent |

| **Labor investment** | Very high | Low | Low (modification) |
| **Insulation** | Excellent | Good (with liner) | Excellent |
| **Group size** | Extended family | Nuclear family | Band |
