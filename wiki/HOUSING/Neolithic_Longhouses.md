# Neolithic Longhouse (Linear Pottery Culture)

The Neolithic longhouse is the signature architecture of Europe's first farmers. Built by the Linear Pottery Culture (LBK) and successors, these massive timber structures housed extended families, livestock, and grain stores under a single roof—representing a revolutionary integration of domestic and economic space.

## Overview

LBK longhouses appear suddenly around 5,500 BCE with the spread of agriculture from the Near East through the Danube corridor. They represent a fully formed architectural tradition with standardized dimensions, construction methods, and internal organization. Over 1,000 examples have been excavated across Germany, Austria, Czech Republic, Poland, Netherlands, Belgium, and France.

## Dimensions & Proportions

| Parameter | Range | Typical |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| **Length** | 15 - 50+ m | 20 - 30 m |
| **Width** | 5 - 7 m | 6 - 7 m |
| **Wall height** | ~2 - 3 m | ~2.5 m |
| **Ridge height** | ~4 - 6 m | ~5 m |
| **Floor area** | 75 - 350 m² | 120 - 200 m² |
| **Orientation** | NW-SE (dominant) | Perpendicular to slope |

**Standard module**: ~2 m bay spacing (post intervals), creating 10-25 bays per house.

## Construction

### Foundation & Posts
- **Wall posts**: Oak or elm, 20-30 cm diameter, set in individual postholes 50-80 cm deep
- **Ridge posts**: Larger timbers (30-40 cm), central row supporting ridge beam
- **Posthole pattern**: Double row of wall posts + single ridge row = three-aisled plan
- **No foundation trenches**: Posts set individually, not in continuous trenches

### Frame & Carpentry
- **Joints**: Mortise-and-tenon, half-lap, scarf joints (inferred from wood preservation)
- **Wall plates**: Horizontal timbers connecting wall post tops
- **Ridge beam**: Central longitudinal beam on ridge posts
- **Rafters**: Paired timbers from wall plates to ridge beam (~45° pitch)
- **Purlins**: Horizontal roof timbers supporting thatch
- **No nails**: Wooden pegs (oak), lashings (lime bast, hide)

### Walls
- **Wattle-and-daub**: Hazel/willow wattle between wall posts, daubed both sides
- **Daub mix**: Clay + straw/dung + sand, 5-10 cm thick
- **Plaster finish**: Fine clay slip, sometimes painted (red/white)
- **Alternative**: Split plank walling (where oak abundant)

### Roof
- **Thatch**: Water reed (Phragmites), wheat straw, heather
- **Thickness**: 30-50 cm for insulation
- **Ridge cap**: Clay saddle or overlapping thatch bundles
- **Overhang**: ~50 cm beyond walls for rain protection

### Floors
- **Earthen**: Trampled clay/straw, sometimes with chalk/lime
- **Upper floor/loft**: Possible in larger houses (ridge post spacing allows)
- **Drainage**: Shallow perimeter ditches, internal sump pits

## Internal Organization

### Three Functional Zones (West to East typical)

#### 1. Western End - Animal Byre (Stall)
- **Evidence**: Phosphate enrichment, posthole partitions, manure layers
- **Capacity**: 4-12 cattle, plus sheep/goats
- **Features**: Drainage gully, tethering posts, fodder storage
- **Function**: Winter housing, manure production, heat generation

#### 2. Central Zone - Living/Sleeping
- **Hearth**: Central or slightly offset, clay-rimmed or stone-lined
- **Sleeping platforms**: Raised earthen benches along walls
- **Storage**: Built-in bins, suspended from roof (grain, goods)
- **Activities**: Cooking, tool maintenance, social, craft

#### 3. Eastern End - Grain Storage / Workspace
- **Granary**: Raised floor (sleeper beams), rodent-proof
- **Capacity**: 2-5 tonnes grain (annual household surplus)
- **Access**: Narrow doorway, sometimes sealed with clay
- **Alternative**: Second living area, craft workshop

### Entrances
- **Main**: Center of long side (south-facing typical), porch possible
- **Secondary**: End doors for animal access, grain loading
- **Dimensions**: ~1 m wide, 1.8 m high

## Construction Sequence (Experimental Reconstruction)

1. **Layout**: String lines, measuring rods (2 m module)
2. **Postholes**: Augered/chiseled, 60-80 cm deep
3. **Posts erected**: 20-30 people, levers, ropes, A-frames
4. **Wall plates & ridge beam**: Lifted into mortises, pegged
5. **Rafters**: Paired, assembled on ground, lifted as trusses
6. **Purlins & laths**: Lashed to rafters
7. **Wattle panels**: Woven in situ between posts
8. **Daubing**: Applied wet, dried 2-3 weeks
9. **Thatching**: Bundles laid bottom-up, secured with hazel rods
10. **Finishing**: Plaster, paint, drainage ditches

**Labor estimate**: 3,000-5,000 person-hours (20 people × 3-4 weeks)
**Materials**: 50-80 oak trees, 2-3 tonnes clay, 5-10 tonnes thatch

## Life Cycle & Maintenance

- **Lifespan**: 20-40 years (posts rot at ground level)
- **Repair**: Individual post replacement possible
- **Rebuilding**: Often on same footprint (overlapping postholes)
- **Abandonment**: Deliberate dismantling (posts pulled), burning

## Regional Variations

| Region | Culture | Distinctive Features |
|--------|---------|---------------------|
| **Rhine/Danube** | LBK classic | Three-aisled, 20-30m, NW-SE |
| **Paris Basin** | LBK/Rössen | Shorter (15-20m), two-aisled variants |
| **North European Plain** | LBK/STK | Longer (30-45m), deeper byres |
| **Loess belt** | LBK | Standardized, dense settlements |
| **Upland margins** | Late LBK | Smaller, more variable orientation |

## Social Implications

### Household Unit
- **Occupants**: 10-20 people (extended family + dependents)
- **Economic unit**: Self-sufficient production/consumption
- **Identity**: House as corporate group (ancestors, heirlooms, territory)

### Settlement Pattern
- **Villages**: 5-15 longhouses in loose rows
- **Spacing**: 15-30 m between houses
- **Communal**: Central open area, shared wells, middens
- **Defense**: Palisades/ditches at some sites (late LBK)

### Symbolic Dimensions
- **Orientation**: NW-SE = cosmological (sunrise/sunset, land/death)
- **Deposits**: Foundation offerings (axes, pottery, grain) in postholes
- **Ancestors**: Burials under floors (children), near houses (adults)

## Experimental Reconstructions

- **Freilichtmuseum Steinzeitpark Dithmarschen** (Germany) - Full-scale LBK house
- **Pfahlbaumuseum Unteruhldingen** (Germany) - Neolithic pile dwellings + longhouse
- **Archeon** (Netherlands) - LBK house with living history
- **Butser Ancient Farm** (UK) - Neolithic rectangular house (British variant)

**Findings**: Internal temperatures 10-15°C above external in winter; smoke management critical; thatch lasts 15-20 years; daub requires annual patching.

## Uncertainties & Debates

- **Two-aisled vs. three-aisled**: Early vs. late, regional, or functional?
- **Upper floors**: Loft storage vs. sleeping—limited direct evidence
- **Roof access**: Smoke holes vs. door-only ventilation
- **Byre separation**: Physical barrier between animals/humans?
- **Social hierarchy**: House size variation = status differences?
