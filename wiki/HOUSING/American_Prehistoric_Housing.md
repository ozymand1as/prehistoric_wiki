# American Prehistoric Housing

American housing traditions developed independently from Old World models, producing distinctive forms: pit houses and pueblos in the Southwest, earth lodges on the Plains, longhouses in the Northeast, platform mounds in the Southeast, and stone cities in Mesoamerica and the Andes.

## Overview

American architecture evolved entirely independently, with no domesticates for traction or heavy transport. Building materials—earth, stone, wood, thatch—were shaped by local environments and social organization.

## North America

### Southwest (Ancestral Pueblo, Hohokam, Mogollon)
| Type | Culture | Features |
|------|---------|----------|
| **Pit house** | Basketmaker (500 BCE–500 CE) | Circular, ventilator shaft, sipapu |
| **Pueblo** | Pueblo (700–1300 CE) | Multi-room, multi-story, adobe/masonry |
| **Cliff dwelling** | Late Pueblo (1150–1300 CE) | Alcove sites, defensive |
| **Great house** | Chaco (900–1150 CE) | Planned, D-shaped, core-and-veneer |
| **Ballcourt** | Hohokam (700–1450 CE) | Mesoamerican influence |

### Plains (Earth Lodges)
- **Mandan/Hidatsa/Arikara**: Circular, 4-post, earth-covered, cache pits
- **Pawnee/Wichita**: Similar, larger, communal
- **Construction**: 4 central posts, radiating beams, willow, grass, earth

### Northeast (Longhouses)
- **Iroquois/Huron**: 20-60 m, bark-covered, matrilineal households
- **Construction**: Elm bark sheets, sapling frame, central fire pits
- **Villages**: Palisaded, 20-150 longhouses

### Southeast (Mississippian)
- **Platform mounds**: Earthen pyramids, summit structures
- **Wattle-daub houses**: On mounds and plazas
- **Cahokia**: Monk's Mound (30 m), 120+ mounds

### Pacific Northwest
- **Plank houses**: Cedar, 15×30 m, posts and beams, painted fronts
- **Villages**: Ranked households, potlatch spaces

### Arctic/Subarctic
- **Inuit**: Snow house (iglu), sod house (qarmaq), tent (tupiq)
- **Aleut**: Semi-subterranean barabara
- **Dene/Algonquian**: Conical lodge, hide-covered

## Mesoamerica

### Maya (2000 BCE–1500 CE)
- **Commoner**: Pole-and-thatch, limestone platform
- **Elite**: Vaulted masonry, corbelled arches
- **Palaces**: Multi-room, courtyards, corbelled vaults
- **Temples**: Pyramidal, roof combs, stucco decoration

### Aztec (1325–1521 CE)
- **Tenochtitlan**: Island city, canals, chinampas
- **Commoner**: Adobe, thatch, courtyard
- **Templo Mayor**: Twin temples, double staircase
- **Calpolli**: Ward organization, communal spaces

### Olmec (1500–400 BCE)
- **San Lorenzo/La Venta**: Earthen mounds, basalt columns
- **Residential**: Thatch on platforms

## Andes

### Inca (1400–1532 CE)
- **Kancha**: Rectangular enclosure, stone walls, thatch roof
- **Kallanka**: Great hall, state gatherings
- **Qolqa**: Storehouses, ventilation ducts
- **Machu Picchu**: Dry stone, trapezoidal doors/windows

### Earlier Andean
- **Caral** (3000 BCE): Sunken plazas, platform mounds
- **Chavín** (900 BCE): Gallery architecture, ventilation
- **Moche** (200–800 CE): Adobe platforms, murals
- **Chimú** (900–1470 CE): Adobe ciudadelas (walled compounds)
