# Neolithic Mudbrick Houses

Neolithic mudbrick houses of the Near East represent the world's earliest substantial rectangular architecture. From the round houses of PPNA Jericho to the dense, roof-accessed complexes of Çatalhöyük, these structures document the transition to sedentary agricultural life.

## Overview

The invention of sun-dried mudbrick (~9,500 BCE) enabled the construction of durable, rectilinear dwellings. This technology spread with agriculture from the Levant to Anatolia, Mesopotamia, and beyond.

## Key Sites

### Jericho (PPNA, ~9,500 BCE)
- **Architecture**: Round/oval semi-subterranean houses
- **Walls**: Mudbrick, stone foundations
- **Tower**: 8.5 m stone tower (communal defense/ritual)
- **Wall**: 3 m thick perimeter wall

### Ain Ghazal (PPNB, ~7,200 BCE)
- **Architecture**: Rectangular, multi-room
- **Plaster**: Lime-plastered floors, walls
- **Plastered skulls**: Ritual caches under floors

### Çatalhöyük (7,100–5,700 BCE)
- **Density**: 8,000+ people, no streets
- **Access**: Roof hatches, ladders
- **Layout**: Standardized rooms, platforms, ovens
- **Art**: Wall paintings, reliefs, bucrania

## Mudbrick Technology

### Brick Production
- **Mix**: Clay, silt, sand, straw/chaff
- **Molds**: Wooden, rectangular (40×20×10 cm typical)
- **Drying**: Sun, 7-14 days
- **Production**: Household or communal

### Construction
- **Foundations**: Stone, 2-3 courses
- **Bonding**: Running bond, header/stretcher
- **Mortar**: Clay-silt slurry
- **Wall thickness**: 40-60 cm (load-bearing)

### Roofs
- **Structure**: Timber beams, reeds, mud
- **Access**: Roof hatch, ladder
- **Activities**: Sleeping, processing, social

## Interior Organization

### Standard Rooms
- **Main room**: Hearth, platforms, storage bins
- **Side rooms**: Storage, specialized (obsidian, beads)
- **Platforms**: Sleeping, working, burial beneath

### Features
- **Ovens**: Domed, clay (baking)
- **Hearths**: Open, clay-rimmed
- **Storage**: Built-in bins, niches
- **Burials**: Under platforms (ancestor veneration)

## Plaster & Decoration

### Lime Plaster
- **Production**: Limestone burned → slaked → applied
- **Floors**: Multiple coats, polished
- **Walls**: White, painted (red ochre, geometric)

### Wall Art (Çatalhöyük)
- **Themes**: Hunting, vultures, geometric, bucrania
- **Techniques**: Fresco secco, relief
- **Symbolism**: Ritual, identity, cosmology
