# Neolithic Pile Dwellings (Alpine Lake Dwellings)

The Alpine pile dwellings (Pfahlbauten) are a unique prehistoric settlement type built on wooden piles over lake margins and wetlands. Their waterlogged preservation provides unparalleled insight into Neolithic and Bronze Age architecture, economy, and daily life.

## Overview

Over 1,000 pile dwelling sites are known around the Alps. UNESCO World Heritage status (2011) recognizes 111 sites across 6 countries. The anaerobic, waterlogged conditions preserve wood, textiles, food remains, and tools.

## Construction

### Pile Foundations
- **Material**: Oak, ash, elm, pine (dendro-dated)
- **Diameter**: 10-30 cm
- **Length**: 2-5 m (driven into lakebed)
- **Spacing**: 0.5-1.0 m grid
- **Driving**: Stone mauls, levering

### Superstructure
- **Floor joists**: On pile heads, notched/pegged
- **Floor boards**: Split planks, bark
- **Walls**: Wattle and daub, split plank
- **Roof**: Thatch (reed, straw), 45° pitch
- **Access**: Ladders, gangways from shore

### Settlement Layout
- **Rows**: Parallel to shore
- **Spacing**: 2-3 m between houses
- **Palissades**: Perimeter, wave protection
- **Jetties**: Access, boat mooring

## Preservation & Finds

### Organic Materials
- **Textiles**: Flax, wool, nettle, bast (thousands of fragments)
- **Food**: Carbonized grain, nuts, fruit, fish bones
- **Tools**: Wooden handles, bows, digging sticks
- **Containers**: Wooden vessels, bark boxes
- **Cordage**: Bast, lime, nettle fiber

### Dendrochronology
- **Exact dating**: Tree-ring sequences to single year
- **Building phases**: Multiple rebuilds per site
- **Lifespan**: 15-30 years per structure (pile rot)

## Daily Life Evidence

### Economy
- **Agriculture**: Naked wheat, barley, flax, poppy
- **Animal husbandry**: Cattle, sheep, goat, pig
- **Hunting/fishing**: Red deer, fish, waterfowl
- **Gathering**: Hazelnuts, berries, wild apples

### Crafts
- **Textile production**: Spindle whorls, loom weights, needles
- **Pottery**: Distinctive cultural styles
- **Lithics**: Flint, obsidian, rock crystal
- **Metalwork**: Copper, bronze (late)

## UNESCO World Heritage
- **Inscribed**: 2011
- **Sites**: 111 of ~1,000 known
- **Countries**: Switzerland (56), Germany (18), Austria (5), France (11), Italy (19), Slovenia (2)
- **Criteria**: (iv) (v) - Settlement adaptation, cultural tradition
