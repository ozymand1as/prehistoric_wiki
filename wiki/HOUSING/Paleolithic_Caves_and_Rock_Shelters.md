# Paleolithic Caves & Rock Shelters

Caves and rock shelters were the primary durable dwellings of the Paleolithic. They provided ready-made protection from elements and predators, and their sediments preserve the richest archaeological records of early human behavior.

## Overview

Natural rock shelters and caves were not merely "homes" but structured living spaces with designated activity areas. Archaeological evidence shows sophisticated spatial organization: hearths, sleeping areas, tool production zones, butchery areas, and refuse disposal—all within the shelter.

## Types of Natural Shelter

| Type | Formation | Advantages | Limitations |
|------|-----------|------------|-------------|
| **Deep Cave** | Limestone dissolution | Constant temperature, secure | Dark, poor ventilation, limited space |
| **Rock Shelter/Abri** | Differential erosion | Light, ventilation, overhang protection | Exposure on sides, limited depth |
| **Sea Cave** | Wave action | Coastal resources, defense | Tidal flooding, damp |
| **Lava Tube** | Volcanic flow | Stable temperature, extensive | Limited locations, rough floor |
| **Talus Shelter** | Boulder accumulation | Expandable, drainable | Unstable, cold air pooling |

## Spatial Organization (Ethnographic & Archaeological Model)

### Activity Areas (Documented at Multiple Sites)
1. **Hearth Zone** - Central or back wall; cooking, warmth, light, social focus
2. **Sleeping/Resting** - Rear/sides, often raised on bedding (grass, leaves, hides)
3. **Tool Production** - Near entrance (light), specific knapping stations
4. **Butchery/Processing** - Near entrance or exterior; blood, bone waste
5. **Refuse/Midden** - Periphery, rear, or exterior slope
6. **Ritual/Symbolic** - Deep cave (art), specific niches (burials, caches)

### Evidence for Zoning
- **Phosphate mapping** - Elevated in sleeping, butchery, refuse areas
- **Microartifact distribution** - Knapping debris clusters, bone fragments
- **Hearth sequences** - Superimposed hearths = long-term structured reuse
- **Postholes/stakeholes** - Internal partitions, windbreaks, structures

## Construction & Modification

### Internal Structures
- **Windbreaks**: Stones, bones, posts at entrance (reduce drafts)
- **Partitions**: Posthole rows dividing space (rare but documented)
- **Flooring**: Grass, leaves, bark, hides over damp floors
- **Wall niches**: Natural or excavated for storage
- **Drainage**: Shallow trenches directing water out

### External Modifications
- **Entrance walls**: Stone walls narrowing opening (wind, heat)
- **Platforms**: Leveled areas for sleeping/working
- **Midden management**: Designated discard zones

## Hearth Technology

| Type | Description | Period |
|------|-------------|--------|
| **Simple pit** | Shallow depression, stone-lined | Lower Paleolithic+ |
| **Basin hearth** | Clay-lined, plastered | Middle Paleolithic+ |
| **Stone-ringed** | Circle of stones containing fire | Upper Paleolithic+ |
| **Multiple hearths** | Simultaneous use (cooking, heat, light) | Upper Paleolithic |
| **Fire-cracked rock** | Boiling stones (indirect cooking) | Middle Paleolithic+ |

### Fire Evidence
- **Charcoal** - Wood species identification (fuel choice)
- **Ash layers** - Repeated use, cleaning episodes
- **Burned bone** - Fuel (fat-rich) or waste disposal
- **Thermally altered sediments** - Reddened bases = in situ burning

## Symbolic & Ritual Use

### Deep Cave Art
- **Parietal art**: Paintings, engravings in dark zones (Lascaux, Chauvet, Altamira)
- **Access difficulty**: Correlated with ritual significance
- **Acoustics**: Resonant chambers selected for art/music

### Portable Art & Objects
- **Figurines**: Venus statuettes (Gravettian), animal carvings
- **Personal ornaments**: Shell beads, ivory pendants, pierced teeth
- **Engraved objects**: Plaquettes, tools, weapons

### Burials in Caves
- **Primary burials**: Shallow graves within occupation layers
- **Secondary**: Bone collections, skull caches
- **Grave goods**: Tools, ornaments, ochre, animal parts

## Taphonomy & Preservation

### Advantages
- **Sediment traps**: Roof fall, slope wash, aeolian dust = rapid burial
- **Stable climate**: Constant T/RH preserves organics (bone, charcoal, pollen)
- **Stratigraphic integrity**: Layered sequences, datable
- **Mineralization**: Calcite flows seal layers

### Biases
- **Overrepresentation**: Caves preserve better than open-air
- **Palimpsest**: Multiple occupations mixed (esp. slow sedimentation)
- **Carnivore activity**: Hyena dens, bear hibernation mix deposits
- **Water movement**: Slope wash redistributes artifacts
