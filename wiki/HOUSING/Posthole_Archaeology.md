# Posthole Archaeology

Posthole archaeology is the primary method for reconstructing prehistoric timber architecture. Since wood rarely preserves, the negative features left by decayed posts provide the main evidence for building form, size, and construction technique.

## Overview

A posthole is the negative impression of a wooden post in the archaeological record. Systematic excavation and analysis of posthole patterns allows reconstruction of otherwise invisible structures.

## Posthole Formation

### Deposition
1. **Post driven/set** into ground
2. **Backfill** packed around post
3. **Post decays/removed** leaving void
4. **Void fills** with surrounding sediment
5. **Archaeologist excavates** the fill

### Posthole Anatomy
- **Cut**: Edge of original hole
- **Fill**: Sediment replacing post
- **Postpipe**: Central zone of post decay
- **Packing**: Stones, clay around post
- **Base**: Post tip impression

## Excavation & Recording

### Identification
- **Color contrast**: Fill vs. natural
- **Shape**: Circular, oval, rectangular
- **Depth**: 0.2-1.0 m typical
- **Profile**: Vertical sides, flat/pointed base

### Data Collected
- **Diameter**: Post size proxy
- **Depth**: Post length proxy
- **Fill composition**: Packing, postpipe
- **Artifacts**: Dating, function
- **Spatial coordinates**: 3D position

## Pattern Analysis

### Building Reconstruction
- **Post rings**: Circular/oval = roundhouses
- **Rectangular grids**: Longhouses, halls
- **Parallel rows**: Aisled buildings
- **Entrance gaps**: Wider spacing, porch posts

### Structural Inference
| Feature | Indicates |
|---------|-----------|
| **Deep, wide holes** | Large posts, heavy loads |
| **Shallow, many holes** | Light structure, wattle walls |
| **Continuous trench** | Wall plate, sill beam |
| **Paired holes** | Opposed posts, trusses |
| **Central holes** | Roof support, hearth |

### Statistical Methods
- **Nearest neighbor**: Spacing regularity
- **Orientation analysis**: Alignment patterns
- **Density mapping**: Activity areas
- **Seriation**: Building phases

## Experimental Validation

### Post Decay Rates
- **Oak**: 20-50 years in situ
- **Pine**: 10-20 years
- **Hazel**: 5-10 years (wattle)
- **Charred bases**: Extend life 2-3x

### Reconstruction Accuracy
- **Post position**: ±5-10 cm typical
- **Building dimensions**: ±5-15%
- **Orientation**: ±2-5°

## Case Studies

### Neolithic LBK Longhouses
- **Pattern**: Three parallel rows, 2 m spacing
- **Posts**: 20-30 cm diameter, 0.5-0.8 m deep
- **Trenches**: Continuous for wall plates

### Iron Age Roundhouses (Britain)
- **Pattern**: Single ring, 6-15 m diameter
- **Porch**: Projecting entrance posts
- **Internal**: Central post, partition posts

### Mississippian (North America)
- **Pattern**: Rectangular, wall trenches
- **Posts**: Small, closely spaced
- **Platform mounds**: Summit structures
