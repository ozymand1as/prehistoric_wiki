# Paleolithic Open Air Shelters

Upper Paleolithic open-air shelters represent the mobile architectural response of modern humans to diverse environments. Unlike cave dwellings, these structures were built from scratch using local materials and could be transported or rapidly reconstructed. [1]

## Overview

Open-air shelters include tents, lean-tos, windbreaks, and semi-subterranean huts. They reflect the mobility of Upper Paleolithic hunter-gatherers and their adaptation to the Last Glacial Maximum environments across Eurasia.

## Shelter Types

### Lean-To / Windbreak
- **Structure**: Single sloping roof against ridge/log [2]
- **Materials**: Poles, hides, brush, branches
- **Orientation**: Back to prevailing wind
- **Use**: Temporary, hunting camps

### Conical Tent (Tipi-like)
- **Structure**: 3-4 poles, hide cover
- **Materials**: Poles, reindeer/bison hides, sinew lashings [3]
- **Ventilation**: Smoke hole at apex
- **Mobility**: High - poles and hides portable

### Dome Hut
- **Structure**: Bent saplings, hide/bark cover
- **Materials**: Flexible poles, hides, bark sheets [4]
- **Shape**: Hemispherical, shed snow/wind
- **Use**: Longer-term base camps

### Semi-Subterranean Hut
- **Structure**: Shallow pit, pole frame, earth cover
- **Insulation**: Earth berms, sod roof
- **Entrance**: Tunnel or covered doorway
- **Use**: Winter occupation

## Materials

| Material | Source | Properties |
|----------|--------|------------|
| **Poles** | Saplings, young trees | Light, flexible, portable |
| **Hides** | Reindeer, bison, horse | Waterproof, insulating |
| **Bark** | Birch, pine, larch | Rigid sheets, waterproof |
| **Sod/Turf** | Grassland | Insulating, heavy |
| **Bone** | Mammoth ribs, tusks | Structural (Mammoth Bone Huts) |
| **Sinew/Cordage** | Animal tendon, plant fiber | Lashings, stitching |

## Archaeological Evidence

### Direct
- **Postholes**: Circular/oval patterns (Dolní Věstonice, Mezhyrich)
- **Hearths**: Central, peripheral, multiple
- **Bone concentrations**: Structural use (mammoth bone huts)
- **Pits**: Storage, refuse

### Indirect
- **Ethnographic analogy**: Arctic/subarctic peoples (Inuit, Evenki, Nenets)
- **Experimental reconstruction**: Butser, Archeon, Mezhirich
- **Spatial analysis**: Activity areas around hearths

## Construction Techniques

### Frame Assembly
1. **Pole selection**: Straight, flexible saplings
2. **Ground preparation**: Clear, level, drainage
3. **Erection**: Tripod or pole ring, lashed with sinew
4. **Covering**: Hides stitched, stretched over frame
5. **Anchoring**: Stones, pegs, turf weighting

### Insulation
- **Floor**: Grass, leaves, fur, pine needles
- **Walls**: Double hide layer, grass stuffing
- **Roof**: Overlap, smoke hole flap

## Social Organization

- **Construction**: Communal (10-20 people)
- **Ownership**: Family/clan
- **Mobility**: Seasonal moves, materials transported
- **Gender**: Men - heavy frame; Women - covering, interior
