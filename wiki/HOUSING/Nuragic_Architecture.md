# Nuragic Architecture

Nuragic architecture is the distinctive Bronze Age building tradition of Sardinia, characterized by thousands of stone towers (nuraghi) and associated village complexes. It represents one of the most remarkable indigenous architectural developments in the Mediterranean. [1]

## Overview

Over 7,000 nuraghi dot the Sardinian landscape, ranging from simple single towers to complex multi-tower fortresses with surrounding villages. They demonstrate sophisticated drystone construction and corbelling without mortar.

## Nuraghe Types

### Simple Nuraghe (Tholos Nuraghe)
- **Form**: Single conical tower
- **Chamber**: Circular, corbelled dome (tholos)
- **Entrance**: Low, lintelled, often with niche
- **Stairs**: Internal spiral stair in wall thickness [2]
- **Height**: 10-20 m
- **Diameter**: 8-15 m base

### Complex Nuraghe (Polylobate)
- **Form**: Central tower + 2-4 subsidiary towers
- **Connection**: Curtain walls, courtyards
- **Plan**: Bilobate, trilobate, quadrilobate, pentalobate [3]
- **Function**: Defense, residence, storage, ritual

### Nuragic Villages
- **Huts**: Circular, stone foundations, conical roofs
- **Layout**: Clustered around nuraghe
- **Features**: Silos, cisterns, meeting huts
- **Population**: 100-500 per complex

## Construction Techniques

### Dry Stone Masonry
- **No mortar**: Stones fitted precisely
- **Courses**: Horizontal, slightly battered
- **Through-stones**: Tie faces together
- **Core**: Rubble fill

### Corbelling
- **Chamber dome**: Each course projects inward
- **Keystone**: Single closing slab
- **Precision**: Courses leveled with stone chips
- **Height**: Up to 10-12 m

### Stair Construction
- **Location**: Within wall thickness
- **Form**: Spiral, corbelled steps
- **Lighting**: Small windows/loopholes

## Materials
- **Stone**: Local basalt, granite, limestone, schist
- **No mortar**: Pure drystone
- **Wood**: Roof beams, floors (rarely preserved)
- **Thatch/earth**: Roof covering

## Major Sites

| Site | Type | Features |
|------|------|----------|
| **Su Nuraxi (Barumini)** | Complex | 4-tower, village, UNESCO |
| **Sant'Imbenia** | Coastal | Trade goods, Mycenaean pottery |
| **Arrubiu** | Large | 5-tower, central courtyard |
| **Losa** | Well-preserved | Trilobate, 13 m high |
| **Seruci** | Extensive | Village, giant tombs nearby |

## Function & Society

### Defensive
- **Strategic locations**: Hilltops, valleys
- **Intervisible**: Signaling network
- **Refuge**: Population shelter

### Residential
- **Elite**: Tower chambers
- **Commoners**: Village huts
- **Storage**: Silos, cisterns in huts

### Ritual
- **Sacred wells**: Water cult
- **Meeting huts**: Circular, benches
- **Giant tombs**: Collective burial (tombe dei giganti)

## Chronology
- **Protonuragic** (~1,800–1,500 BCE): Corridor nuraghi
- **Early Nuragic** (~1,500–1,200 BCE): Tholos nuraghi
- **Middle Nuragic** (~1,200–900 BCE): Complex nuraghi
- **Late Nuragic** (~900–500 BCE): Villages, sanctuaries
- **Final Nuragic** (~500–238 BCE): Roman contact
