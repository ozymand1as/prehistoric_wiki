# Bronze Age Longhouses

Bronze Age longhouses evolved from Neolithic LBK ancestors into substantial byre-houses integrating human and animal habitation under one roof. They represent the dominant farmhouse form across temperate Europe for over a millennium.

## Overview

Bronze Age longhouses show remarkable continuity with Neolithic predecessors while developing distinctive regional forms. The three-aisled byre-house—with central human living space flanked by animal stalls—becomes the standard across temperate Europe.

## Development from Neolithic

| Feature | Neolithic LBK | Bronze Age |
|---------|---------------|------------|
| **Aisles** | Three | Two or three |
| **Byre** | End room | Integrated side aisles |
| **Posts** | Individual holes | Continuous trench (some) |
| **Length** | 20-30 m | 15-45 m |
| **Orientation** | NW-SE | Variable (often E-W) |

## Regional Types

### Nordic Bronze Age (Denmark, S. Sweden, N. Germany)
- **Two-aisled**: Central nave + one side aisle (byre)
- **Three-aisled**: Central + two side aisles (larger farms)
- **Construction**: Massive oak posts in deep trenches
- **Length**: 20-40 m
- **Roof**: Thatch, low pitch
- **Entrance**: Gable end, often south-facing

### Urnfield / Late Bronze Age (Central Europe)
- **Three-aisled**: Standard
- **Sunken floor**: 0.3-0.5 m (insulation)
- **Posts**: Oak, in continuous foundation trench
- **Walls**: Wattle and daub, plank
- **Byre**: Side aisles, drainage gullies

### British Bronze Age
- **Less common**: Roundhouses dominant
- **Longhouses**: Wessex, Orkney (rare)
- **Construction**: Stone footings, timber frame

## Internal Organization

### Typical Three-Aisled Plan (West to East)
1. **Western byre**: Cattle stalls, drainage gully, fodder storage
2. **Central living**: Hearth, sleeping platforms, storage
3. **Eastern byre/work**: Sheep/goats, grain storage, workshop

### Features
- **Hearth**: Central, clay-rimmed or stone
- **Sleeping**: Raised platforms along walls
- **Storage**: Overhead loft (grain, hay)
- **Lighting**: Smoke hole, door, grease lamps

## Construction Details

### Foundation
- **Trench**: Continuous, 0.5-0.8 m deep
- **Posts**: Oak, 25-35 cm diameter, charred bases
- **Wall plates**: Notched onto posts, pegged

### Roof
- **Rafters**: Paired, on wall plates + ridge posts
- **Purlins**: 3-5 horizontal
- **Thatch**: Reed, straw, heather (30-50 cm)
- **Pitch**: 40-45°

### Byre Features
- **Stall divisions**: Low wattle partitions
- **Drainage**: Central gully to soak pit
- **Tethering**: Post holes for stanchions
- **Manure**: Collected for fields

## Social & Economic Role

### Household Unit
- **Occupants**: Extended family (10-20 people)
- **Animals**: 8-20 cattle, sheep/goats
- **Economy**: Mixed farming, textile production

### Status Indicators
- **House length**: 20 m (small) to 45 m (chief's)
- **Construction quality**: Oak vs. pine, trench depth
- **Internal fittings**: Loom, bronze objects, imports
