# Wattle and Daub

Wattle and daub is a composite building technique using woven wooden lattice (wattle) plastered with a sticky mixture of clay, sand, straw, and dung (daub). It is one of the most widespread and enduring wall construction methods in human history. [1]

## Overview

Wattle and daub combines the tensile strength of woven wood with the compressive strength and weather resistance of earth plaster. It appears independently across Eurasia, Africa, and the Americas from the Neolithic onward.

## Construction

### Wattle (Lattice)
- **Uprights (staves)**: Hazel, willow, oak, ash - driven into ground or fitted into frame [2]
- **Withies (weavers)**: Flexible rods (hazel, willow) woven horizontally
- **Spacing**: 15-30 cm between uprights
- **Tension**: Withies pulled tight, self-locking

### Daub (Plaster)
| Component | Proportion | Function |
|-----------|------------|----------|
| **Clay** | 50-70% | Binder, plasticity |
| **Sand/silt** | 20-30% | Aggregate, reduce cracking |
| **Straw/hay** | 10-20% | Tensile reinforcement |
| **Dung** | 5-15% | Plasticity, water resistance |
| **Water** | As needed | Workability |

### Application
1. **First coat (pricking up)**: Thrown onto wattle, keyed into lattice [3]
2. **Second coat (floating)**: Smoothed, leveled
3. **Finish coat**: Fine clay slip, sometimes limewash
4. **Thickness**: 5-15 cm typical

## Archaeological Evidence

### Direct
- **Daub fragments**: Impressions of wattle, finger marks
- **Postholes**: Upright positions
- **Burned daub**: Preserved by fire (vitrified)
- **Tool marks**: Trowel, float impressions

### Key Sites
- **Neolithic Europe**: LBK longhouses, Vinča
- **Iron Age Britain**: Roundhouses, hillforts
- **Mississippian**: Cahokia, Moundville
- **Ancient Near East**: Çatalhöyük, Jericho

## Regional Variations

| Region | Wattle | Daub | Period |
|--------|--------|------|--------|
| **Neolithic Europe** | Hazel/oak | Clay+straw+dung | LBK+ |
| **Iron Age Britain** | Hazel/willow | Clay+chaff | 800 BCE-100 CE |
| **Mississippian** | Cane/river cane | Clay+grass | 800-1500 CE |
| **West Africa** | Bamboo/millet stalk | Laterite clay | Medieval+ |
| **Mesoamerica** | Reed/bamboo | Lime+earth | Preclassic+ |

## Advantages & Limitations

### Advantages
- **Local materials**: No transport
- **Thermal mass**: Temperature buffering
- **Flexibility**: Earthquake resistant
- **Repairable**: Patch easily

### Limitations
- **Moisture**: Requires roof overhang, foundation
- **Maintenance**: Annual inspection, re-daubing
- **Labor**: Skilled, time-consuming
- **Durability**: 20-50 years with maintenance
