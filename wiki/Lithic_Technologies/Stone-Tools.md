# Stone Tools

Stone tools are the earliest and most widespread technology of the Stone Age, used by hominins across the globe for millions of years. They represent the foundational material culture of human technological development and provide the primary archaeological record for early human behavior. The study of stone tools, known as lithic analysis, reveals insights into cognitive evolution, subsistence strategies, and cultural transmission.

## History & Development

- **Lower Paleolithic (~3.3 Ma – 300 ka):** Earliest stone tool production, including Oldowan choppers and Acheulean handaxes. Key sites include Lomekwi (Kenya, ~3.3 Ma) and Olduvai Gorge (Tanzania, ~1.8 Ma).
- **Middle Paleolithic (~300 ka – 50 ka):** Development of prepared-core techniques (Levallois method) by Neanderthals and early Homo sapiens. Key site: Denisova Cave (Siberia).
- **Mesolithic (~50 ka – 10 ka):** Diversification of microlithic toolkits, adaptation to post-glacial environments.
- **Neolithic (~10 ka – present):** Polished stone tools, megalithic construction, and the rise of sedentary societies. Key site: Blombos Cave (South Africa) provides evidence of early symbolic behavior alongside stone tool production.

## Function & Use

- **Primary Use:** Utility tasks including cutting, scraping, piercing, and grinding
- **Secondary Uses:** Hunting (projectile points), butchering (scrapers, knives), food preparation (mortars, pestles), shelter construction, and art
- **Cultural Significance:** Stone tools reflect cognitive development, social learning, and cultural identity; some tools served ritual or status-related functions in later periods

## Construction & Technique

Stone tools were produced from various raw materials including flint, obsidian, chert, quartzite, and basalt. The primary manufacturing techniques are lithic reduction, which involves removing flakes from a core to shape a tool. Two main methods exist:

- **Percussion flaking:** Striking the core with a hammerstone or antler bat to detach flakes
- **Pressure flaking:** Applying controlled force with a pointed tool (bone, antler, or copper) to remove narrow flakes along the edge

The choice of material and technique depends on the desired tool form, the raw material available, and the intended function.

## Regional Variations

- **East Africa:** Oldowan and Acheulean traditions at Olduvai Gorge and Lomekwi; earliest known stone tool production
- **Southern Africa:** Bladelet and microlithic technologies at Blombos Cave; evidence of early symbolic behavior alongside tool production
- **Western Europe:** Mousterian Levallois techniques associated with Neanderthals; later Mesolithic microlithic assemblages
- **Southeast Asia:** Hoabinhian traditions with distinctive flaking techniques; evidence of early maritime technology
