# Hammerstone

Hammerstones are among the oldest stone tools, used as percussive tools for knapping other stones to produce flakes. They are hard, rounded stones used to strike other rocks, and they were essential for producing all other stone tools throughout human prehistory.

## History & Development

Hammerstones date back to the earliest Stone Age, with the oldest known examples associated with the Lomekwi industry (~3.3 Ma). They were used continuously through the Acheulean period and remained central to lithic production through the Neolithic. Their development paralleled the increasing complexity of knapping techniques, from simple hard-hammer percussion to more refined soft-hammer methods using bone or antler.

## Function & Use

- **Primary Use:** Percussive tool for knapping stone to produce flakes and cores
- **Secondary Uses:** Crushing, grinding, and shaping materials; ore crushing for early metallurgy
- **Cultural Significance:** Fundamental to all stone tool production; enabled the creation of increasingly complex toolkits across all periods of the Stone Age

## Construction & Technique

Hammerstones are naturally hard, rounded stones selected for their density and durability. They require no construction but are chosen based on size, shape, and hardness. Two main techniques are employed: hard hammer percussion (using the stone hammer directly to strike the target rock) and soft hammer percussion (using bone or antler as an intermediary to produce more controlled, thinner flakes).

## Regional Variations

- **East Africa:** Earliest hammerstones associated with Lomekwi and Olduvai Gorge industries; simple, rounded cobbles used for hard-hammer percussion
- **Acheulean Sites:** Wider distribution across Africa and Europe; hammerstones used alongside handaxes for systematic flintknapping
- **Neolithic Europe:** More refined hammerstones, often with evidence of soft-hammer technique using antler or bone percussors
