# Paleolithic Stone Tools

Paleolithic stone tools represent the earliest technological innovations in human history, spanning over 3 million years of human development. These tools, made from knapped stone, were essential for survival and represent significant cognitive and social complexity. The evolution from simple flakes to sophisticated composite tools marks key transitions in human technological development.


## Overview

Paleolithic stone tools represent the earliest technological innovations in human history, spanning over 3 million years of human development. These tools, made from knapped stone, were essential for survival and represent significant cognitive and social complexity. The evolution from simple flakes to sophisticated composite tools marks key transitions in human technological development.
## History & Development

### Lower Paleolithic (3.3 million - 300,000 BCE)

- **Oldowan Industry:** Simple flakes and crude chopping tools
- **Acheulean Industry:** Biconvex blade tools with bilateral symmetry
- **Lomekwi 3 Site:** Earliest known stone tools in Kenya (3.3 million years ago)

### Middle Paleolithic (300,000 - 30,000 BCE)

- **Mousterian:** More advanced prepared-core tool-making
- **Control of Fire:** Earliest evidence dates to Lower or Middle Paleolithic
- **Bone Artifacts:** First bone artifacts and early art

### Upper Paleolithic (50,000 - 12,000 BCE)

- **Behavioral Modernity:** Marked increase in artifact diversity
- **Artistic Work:** Cave painting, petroglyphs, carvings
- **Organized Settlements:** Campsites with storage pits
- **Projectile Points:** Sophisticated hunting tools

## Function & Use

- **Primary Use:** Hunting, butchering, plant processing, general survival
- **Secondary Uses:** Fishing, tool making, symbolic expression
- **Cultural Significance:** Early evidence of human cognition, social organization, and artistic expression

## Construction & Technique

- **Materials:** Various stone types with conchoidal fracture properties
- **Techniques:** Direct percussion, indirect percussion, pressure flaking
- **Raw Material Selection:** Non-crystalline or glassy materials, conchoidal fracturing
- **Risk Management:** High risk contexts favor high-quality material acquisition

## Regional Variations

- **Europe:** Bohunician, Châtelperronian, Aurignacian, Gravettian, Pavlovian
- **Africa:** Lomekwian technology, Olduvai Gorge sequence
- **Fertile Crescent:** Emiran (50-40 ka), Baradostian (36-18 ka)
- **Americas:** Clovis, Monte Verde, Mississippian cultures
