# Community & Social Organization Technologies
**Period:** Paleolithic to Bronze Age  
**Region(s):** Global  
**Culture(s):** All known prehistoric cultures  
**Time Range:** ~3.3 million years ago to 1,200 BCE  
**Confidence:** Variable by topic  
**Evidence Type:** Archaeological, Anthropological, Ethnographic, Comparative

## Overview
Social organization is a technology—systems for coordinating labor, distributing resources, resolving conflict, and transmitting knowledge. From the egalitarian bands of the Paleolithic to the bureaucratic states of the Bronze Age, these "social technologies" co-evolved with material technologies and enabled increasingly complex societies.

## Quick Reference: Social Evolution Timeline

| Period | Typical Structure | Population | Leadership | Economy | Key Evidence |
|--------|------------------|------------|------------|---------|--------------|
| Lower Paleolithic | Band | 20-50 | Situational | Foraging/sharing | Stone tools, campsites |
| Middle/Upper Paleolithic | Band | 25-100 | Consensus/elders | Foraging/gift | Art, burial, trade |
| Mesolithic | Band/Tribe | 50-200 | Big man emerging | Foraging/horticulture | Cemeteries, huts |
| Early Neolithic | Tribe/Village | 100-500 | Council/big man | Farming/reciprocity | Longhouses, shrines |
| Late Neolithic | Chiefdom | 1,000-5,000 | Hereditary chief | Redistribution | Monuments, prestige goods |
| Chalcolithic | Chiefdom/Early State | 5,000-20,000 | Paramount chief | Trade/tribute | Copper, urbanization |
| Early Bronze Age | State | 10,000-100,000+ | King/bureaucracy | Market/taxation | Writing, palaces, armies |
| Late Bronze Age | Empire | 100,000+ | Emperor | International trade | Diplomacy, standardization |

## Key Concepts & Debates
- **Unilineal vs. Multilinear Evolution** - Single path vs. diverse trajectories
- **Managerial vs. Conflict Theory** - Integration vs. coercion in state formation
- **Agency vs. Structure** - Individual action vs. systemic constraints
- **Archaeological Visibility** - How to identify social organization from material remains
- **Ethnographic Analogy** - Using modern foragers/pastoralists to interpret past

## Key Sites/Case Studies
- **Göbekli Tepe** (Turkey) - ~9,600 BCE, pre-agricultural monument building
- **Çatalhöyük** (Turkey) - ~7,100 BCE, large egalitarian(?) village
- **Varna Necropolis** (Bulgaria) - ~4,500 BCE, earliest gold, status differentiation
- **Uruk** (Iraq) - ~3,500 BCE, first city, writing, state administration
- **Mycenae** (Greece) - ~1,600 BCE, palatial bureaucracy, Linear B
- **Stonehenge** (UK) - ~3,000-2,000 BCE, communal monument construction
- **Norte Chico/Caral** (Peru) - ~3,000 BCE, early urbanism without ceramics
- **Erlitou** (China) - ~1,900 BCE, early Bronze Age state, palace complex
