# Tribal Organization

Tribal organization represents an intermediate stage between band society and chiefdom, characterized by larger populations, segmentary lineage systems, and achieved leadership (big men) rather than hereditary rank.

## Overview

Tribes emerge with the transition to food production (horticulture, pastoralism) and sedentism. They typically comprise multiple bands or villages linked by kinship, shared identity, and reciprocal obligations, but lack centralized political institutions.

## Defining Characteristics

| Feature | Tribal Organization |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Population** | 100–5,000+ |
| **Subsistence** | Horticulture, pastoralism, mixed |
| **Settlement** | Villages, hamlets, seasonal |
| **Leadership** | Big men, councils, achieved |
| **Integration** | Kinship, age grades, sodalities |
| **Stratification** | None (egalitarian ethos) |
| **Conflict** | Feuding, raiding, limited war |

## Social Structure

### Segmentary Lineage System
- **Maximal lineage**: Tribe (mythical ancestor)
- **Major segments**: Clans/phratries
- **Minor segments**: Lineages
- **Minimal segments**: Extended families
- **Principle**: Complementary opposition (unite against outsiders)

### Age & Gender Organization
- **Age sets/grades**: Cohorts with shared responsibilities
- **Men's houses**: Ritual, political, social center
- **Women's groups**: Economic, ritual, mutual aid
- **Initiation**: Rites of passage, knowledge transmission

### Sodalities (Cross-cutting Groups)
- **Age grades**: Warrior, elder, ritual roles
- **Secret societies**: Esoteric knowledge, power
- **Religious cults**: Ancestor, fertility, war
- **Work parties**: Reciprocal labor exchange

## Leadership: The Big Man

### Achieved Status
- **Not hereditary**: Earned through ability, generosity
- **Skills**: Oratory, mediation, horticulture, warfare
- **Wealth**: Accumulates to give away (not hoard)
- **Influence**: Persuasive, not coercive

### Big Man Functions
- **Feasting**: Competitive generosity (moka, potlatch)
- **Mediation**: Dispute resolution, alliance building
- **Mobilization**: Labor for gardens, defense, ceremonies
- **Exchange**: Manages external trade, prestige goods

### Instability
- **No succession**: Death = power vacuum
- **Competition**: Multiple aspirants
- **Fission**: Group splits if no successor

## Economy

### Reciprocity
- **Generalized**: Kin, no expectation of return
- **Balanced**: Direct exchange, equivalence expected
- **Negative**: Get more than give (sharp practice)

### Redistribution (Incipient)
- **Big man collects**: Surplus from supporters
- **Redistributes**: Feasts, gifts to allies
- **Not institutionalized**: Personal, not office-based

## Conflict & Resolution

### Feuding
- **Blood revenge**: Kin obligation to avenge
- **Escalation**: Segmentary alignment
- **Termination**: Compensation (bridewealth, pigs, shells)

### Raiding
- **Low-intensity**: Cattle, women, prestige goods
- **Seasonal**: Post-harvest, dry season
- **Rules**: Avoid killing women/children (ideally)

## Archaeological Visibility

### Settlement
- **Village clusters**: 50–500 people
- **Defensive**: Palisades, ditches (late)
- **Communal structures**: Men's houses, plazas

### Material Culture
- **Uniform**: No elite goods
- **Prestige items**: Circulated, not hoarded
- **Exchange goods**: Obsidian, shells, pigments
