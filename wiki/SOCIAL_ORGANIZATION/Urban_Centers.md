# Urban Centers

Urban centers are large, permanent settlements with specialized functions (administrative, religious, economic, military) that serve as the political and cultural capitals of early states. They represent the culmination of sedentism, agricultural surplus, and social stratification. [1]

## Overview

Cities are not merely large villages. They are functionally differentiated spaces with monumental architecture, heterogeneous populations, centralized institutions, and hinterland integration. The "urban revolution" (Childe) marks the transition from village-based to state-level society.

## Defining Features

| Feature | Village | Urban Center |
|---------|---------|--------------|
| **Population** | <5,000 | >5,000 (often 10,000–100,000+) |
| **Functions** | Subsistence | Admin, religious, craft, trade, military |
| **Architecture** | Domestic | Monumental, specialized |
| **Planning** | Organic | Planned sectors, zoning |
| **Institutions** | Kin-based | Impersonal, bureaucratic |
| **Hinterland** | Local | Regional/imperial |

## Early Urban Centers

### Mesopotamia (Uruk, 4,000–3,100 BCE)
- **Uruk**: >50 ha, 20,000–50,000 people
- **Eanna precinct**: Temple complex, early writing [2]
- **Administration**: Cylinder seals, tablets, seals
- **Trade**: Long-distance (lapis, copper, timber)

### Egypt (Memphis, Hierakonpolis, 3,100 BCE+)
- **Memphis**: Capital, administrative center
- **Hierakonpolis**: Early urbanization, palace
- **Urban planning**: Grid (Kahun), organic (Thebes) [3]

### Indus Valley (Mohenjo-daro, Harappa, 2,600–1,900 BCE)
- **Grid planning**: Streets, drainage, standardization
- **Citadel**: Elite/ritual zone
- **Great Bath**: Ritual center
- **No palaces/temples**: Corporate organization?

### China (Erlitou, Anyang, 1,900–1,046 BCE)
- **Erlitou**: Palace complex, bronze workshops
- **Anyang (Yinxu)**: Oracle bones, royal tombs, workshops [4]
- **Urban layout**: Palace-temple core, workshops, residential

### Mesoamerica (Teotihuacan, Maya cities, 100 BCE–900 CE)
- **Teotihuacan**: 200,000+, planned grid, pyramids
- **Maya**: Dynastic centers, plazas, ballcourts, reservoirs
- **Urbanism**: Low-density, dispersed

### Andes (Caral, Chan Chan, 3,000 BCE–1,470 CE)
- **Caral**: 3,000 BCE, platform mounds, sunken plazas
- **Chan Chan**: Adobe ciudadelas, 30,000+
- **Cusco**: Inca capital, puma-shaped plan

## Urban Functions

### Administrative
- **Palace**: Governance, archives, treasury
- **Bureaucracy**: Scribes, tax collectors, judges
- **Law**: Codes, courts, enforcement

### Religious
- **Temple**: Cult, priesthood, calendar, economy
- **Ritual**: Public ceremony, pilgrimage, sacrifice
- **Ideology**: Legitimization, cosmology

### Economic
- **Markets**: Central, periodic, specialized
- **Workshops**: Attached, independent
- **Storage**: State granaries, warehouses
- **Trade**: Long-distance, caravans, ports

### Military
- **Walls**: Defense, symbolism
- **Garrisons**: Standing troops, reserves
- **Logistics**: Armories, stables, supply

## Urban Planning

| Element | Example |
|---------|---------|
| **Grid** | Mohenjo-daro, Teotihuacan, Kahun |
| **Radial** | Erlitou, Anyang |
| **Organic** | Uruk, Thebes, Maya |
| **Zoning** | Citadel/lower town, industrial/residential |
| **Infrastructure** | Drains, wells, roads, granaries |
