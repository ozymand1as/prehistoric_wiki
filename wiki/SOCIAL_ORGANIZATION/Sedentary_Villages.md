# Sedentary Villages

Sedentary villages are permanent, year-round settlements based on food production (agriculture, pastoralism, or intensive foraging). They represent the fundamental social unit of the Neolithic and the building block of later urbanism. [1]

## Overview

Sedentism precedes agriculture in some regions (Natufian, Jōmon) but becomes universal with farming. Villages are the primary context for the development of new technologies, social institutions, and demographic growth.

## Characteristics

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Population** | 50–500 (typical Neolithic), up to 5,000 (large) |
| **Duration** | Generations to millennia (tell formation) |
| **Layout**: | Clustered, linear, circular, planned |
| **Architecture**: | Domestic, communal, storage, ritual |
| **Economy**: | Food production + craft + exchange |

## Village Types

### Nuclear Village
- **Layout**: Compact cluster of houses
- **Social**: Single corporate community
- **Example**: Çatalhöyük, LBK settlements

### Dispersed Settlement
- **Layout**: Farmsteads/hamlets across territory
- **Social**: Kin-based neighborhoods
- **Example**: LBK longhouse clusters, Jōmon

### Nucleated / Planned
- **Layout**: Grid, radial, axial
- **Social**: Central authority, planning
- **Example**: Uruk, Mohenjo-daro, Kahun

### Linear / Streamside
- **Layout**: Along river, ridge, road
- **Social**: Households front water/source
- **Example**: Nile, Tigris-Euphrates, LBK

## Social Organization

### Household
- **Unit**: Nuclear/extended family
- **Production**: Subsistence + craft
- **Autonomy**: Variable (independent vs corporate) [2]

### Corporate Groups
- **Lineage/clan**: Kin-based corporate landholding
- **Age sets**: Cross-cutting labor/ritual groups
- **Sodalities**: Religious, craft, military associations [3]

### Community Institutions
- **Council**: Elders, lineage heads
- **Ritual center**: Shrine, plaza, men's house
- **Storage**: Communal granaries
- **Defense**: Palisade, ditch, wall

## Economy

### Subsistence
- **Agriculture**: Intensive, irrigated, terraced
- **Pastoralism**: Integrated (byre-houses, transhumance) [4]
- **Storage**: Household + communal (risk buffering)

### Craft Specialization
- **Part-time**: Household surplus
- **Full-time**: Attached (temple/palace) or independent
- **Markets**: Periodic, central place

### Exchange
- **Local**: Reciprocity, periodic markets
- **Regional**: Obsidian, salt, ceramics, metals
- **Long-distance**: Prestige goods, exotics

## Demography & Health

### Population Growth
- **Sedentism**: Shorter birth intervals, higher fertility
- **Carrying capacity**: Agriculture supports 10-100x forager density
- **Carrying capacity limits**: Soil depletion, disease, conflict

### Health Impacts
- **Dental**: Caries (carbohydrates), wear (grit)
- **Infectious**: Crowding, waste, zoonoses
- **Nutritional**: Iron deficiency, growth stunting
- **Violence**: Raiding, warfare, sacrifice

## Archaeological Visibility

| Feature | Identification |
|---------|----------------|
| **Tells/mounds** | Long-term occupation layers |
| **House foundations** | Postholes, foundations, floors |
| **Storage pits/silos** | Subfloor, freestanding |
| **Communal structures** | Large, non-domestic |
| **Middens** | Refuse, diet, craft waste |
| **Burials** | Intramural, extramural, cemeteries |
