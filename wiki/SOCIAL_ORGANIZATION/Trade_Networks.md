# Trade Networks

Prehistoric trade networks moved raw materials, finished goods, and ideas across vast distances. From Paleolithic shell beads to Bronze Age tin routes, exchange systems were vital for acquiring scarce resources, establishing social alliances, and driving technological innovation.

## Overview

Trade in prehistory operated through multiple mechanisms simultaneously: direct procurement, down-the-line exchange, gift exchange, redistribution, and market trade (late). The archaeological signature includes exotic materials at distances from sources, standardized artifacts, and source analysis (geochemical, petrographic).

## Exchange Mechanisms

| Mechanism | Description | Social Context | Archaeological Signature |
|-----------|-------------|----------------|-------------------------|
| **Direct procurement** | Consumers go to source | Mobile foragers, specialized trips | Source-area debris, no middlemen |
| **Down-the-line** | Passed group-to-group | Bands, tribes; reciprocal | Distance-decay curves, no single direction |
| **Gift/Reciprocal** | Obligatory return gift | Allies, kin, leaders | High-value items, non-utilitarian |
| **Redistribution** | Center collects → reallocates | Chiefdoms, states | Central storage, sealings, records |
| **Market exchange** | Price, supply/demand | Late Bronze Age+, urban | Weights, coins, marketplaces |

## Major Trade Materials & Routes

### Stone Age Networks

#### Obsidian (Volcanic Glass)
- **Sources**: Anatolia (Cappadocia), Melos (Aegean), Lipari (Italy), Pantelleria, Armenia, Peru, Mexico, Japan, Kenya
- **Paleolithic**: Up to 300-500 km (direct/down-the-line)
- **Neolithic**: 1,000+ km (Anatolia → Levant, Balkans, Cyprus)
- **Method**: Geochemical fingerprinting (XRF, NAA, LA-ICP-MS)
- **Significance**: Best cutting edges; first "global" commodity

#### Flint/Chert
- **Sources**: Local (100s km) to regional (Grand Pressigny France → Europe; Rijckholt Netherlands → NW Europe)
- **Neolithic**: Specialized mining (Spiennes, Grimes Graves, Krzemionki)
- **Trade**: Finished blades, rough-outs, cores

#### Amphibolite/Greenstone (Axes)
- **Sources**: Alps (jadeitite), Cornwall (gabbro), Great Langdale (tuff), Poland (świeciechów flint)
- **Neolithic**: Polished axes traded 500-1,000+ km
- **Social**: Prestige, ceremonial, not just utilitarian

### Shell & Organic Materials

#### Marine Shells
- **Species**: Spondylus (spiny oyster), Glycymeris, Conus, Dentalium, Nassarius
- **Paleolithic**: Mediterranean → Central Europe (500+ km, Gravettian)
- **Neolithic**: Spondylus from Aegean → Central Europe (LBK, 1,000+ km)
- **Andes**: Spondylus from Ecuador → Peru, Bolivia, Chile (Inca precursors)
- **North America**: Gulf/Atlantic shells → Great Plains, Midwest

#### Amber
- **Source**: Baltic coast (Eocene deposits)
- **Routes**: "Amber Roads" → Mediterranean, Egypt, Near East, Britain
- **Bronze Age**: Major commodity; Mycenaean, Egyptian, Baltic elite graves
- **Significance**: Solar symbolism, electrostatic properties

#### Ivory, Jet, Coral, Pearls
- **Ivory**: Elephant (Africa, India), mammoth (Siberia), hippo, walrus
- **Jet**: Whitby (Yorkshire) → Britain, Europe (Neolithic/BA)
- **Coral**: Mediterranean → Alps, Central Europe (Neolithic)
- **Pearls**: Persian Gulf, Red Sea → Near East, India

### Metal Age Networks (Chalcolithic/Bronze Age)

#### Copper
- **Sources**: Balkans (Rudna Glava, Ai Bunar), Anatolia, Cyprus, Oman, Iran, Caucasus, Great Lakes (native copper), Andes
- **Early**: Native copper (cold-hammered) → smelted (Chalcolithic)
- **Trade**: Ingots (oxhide), tools, weapons, prestige items

#### Tin (Critical for Bronze)
- **Sources**: Rare! Cornwall (UK), Erzgebirge (Germany/Czech), Brittany, Central Asia (Afghanistan, Uzbekistan), Turkey (Kestel/Göltepe), Iberia, Nigeria
- **Bronze Age**: Long-distance trade essential (Cornwall → Mediterranean, Central Asia → Mesopotamia/China)
- **Uluburun shipwreck** (1,300 BCE): 10 tons copper + 1 ton tin ingots

#### Gold
- **Sources**: Egypt (Nubia), Balkans (Varna), Ireland, Spain, Transylvania, Caucasus, Colombia, Peru
- **Early**: Varna necropolis (4,500 BCE) - oldest worked gold
- **Trade**: Prestige, ritual, elite display

#### Silver, Lead, Arsenic, Antimony
- **Silver**: Anatolia, Laurion (Greece), Spain, Iran
- **Lead**: Isotope analysis traces ores to artifacts
- **Arsenic/antimony**: Bronze alloying elements

### Other Materials
- **Lapis lazuli**: Badakhshan (Afghanistan) → Egypt, Mesopotamia, Indus, China
- **Turquoise**: Sinai, Iran, SW USA → Mesoamerica, Near East
- **Carnelian**: India (Gujarat) → Mesopotamia, Egypt, China
- **Ostrich eggshell**: Africa → Near East, Mediterranean (beads, containers)
- **Bitumen/Asphalt**: Dead Sea, Iran, Trinidad → waterproofing, adhesives
- **Salt**: Hallstatt (Austria), Hallein, Wieliczka (Poland), Sahara → essential, heavy trade

## Bronze Age Trade Systems

### Mediterranean
- **Minoan/Mycenaean**: Palatial redistribution, maritime network
- **Uluburun shipwreck** (Turkey, ~1,300 BCE): 10 tons copper (Cyprus), 1 ton tin (Central Asia), glass, ebony, ivory, ostrich eggs, resin, pottery (Cypriot, Mycenaean, Egyptian, Canaanite)
- **Cape Gelidonya** (Turkey, ~1,200 BCE): Merchant ship, tools, scrap metal

### Near East
- **Amarna letters** (1,350 BCE): Diplomatic gift exchange (gold, lapis, horses, chariots)
- **Assyrian merchant colonies** (Kanesh, ~1,900 BCE): Private trade, textiles for silver/gold
- **Dilmun (Bahrain)**: Gulf entrepôt (copper, pearls, dates)

### Central Asia
- **Oxus Civilization / BMAC** (2,300-1,700 BCE): Lapis, tin, copper, camel caravans
- **Steppe networks**: Sintashta/Andronovo → China (chariots, bronze, horses)

### Europe
- **Amber routes**: Baltic → Adriatic (Este), Mediterranean
- **Tin routes**: Cornwall → Brittany → Mediterranean
- **Urnfield/Hallstatt**: Iron, salt, textiles, prestige goods

### China
- **Erlitou/Shang**: Jade (Khotan), turquoise, bronze, cowries (currency)
- **Steppe contact**: Chariots, horses, bronze technology from West

## Social Implications

### Trade & Complexity
- **Specialization**: Craft producers depend on imported materials
- **Elite control**: Chiefs/kings monopolize long-distance trade
- **Innovation diffusion**: Technology spreads with materials (metallurgy, wheels, writing)
- **Alliance building**: Gift exchange creates political ties

### Middlemen & Entrepreneurs
- **Neolithic**: No professional traders (down-the-line)
- **Chalcolithic/Bronze Age**: Merchant classes (Assyrian, Minoan, Indus)
- **Caravanserais**: Organized stops (later periods)

## Archaeological Methods

### Provenance Studies
- **Geochemistry**: XRF, NAA, ICP-MS, LA-ICP-MS (obsidian, metals, pottery)
- **Petrography**: Thin-section (pottery, stone)
- **Isotopes**: Lead (metals), Strontium (human/animal mobility), Oxygen
- **Trace elements**: Fingerprinting sources

### Network Analysis
- **Fall-off curves**: Quantity vs. distance from source
- **Centrality measures**: Nodes, hubs, gateways
- **Least-cost paths**: GIS modeling of routes
- **Social network analysis**: Communities, clusters
