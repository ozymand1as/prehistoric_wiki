# Intergroup Violence

Intergroup violence encompasses the spectrum of violent conflict between human groups, from interpersonal homicide to organized warfare. Its archaeological visibility and social causes provide key insights into the evolution of human cooperation and conflict. [1]

## Overview

Violence is not a single phenomenon but a spectrum. Its form, scale, and social meaning change with subsistence, demography, and political organization. Bioarchaeology (skeletal trauma) provides the most direct evidence.

## Violence Spectrum

| Type | Scale | Organization | Motive |
|------|-------|--------------|--------|
| **Homicide** | Individual | Personal | Dispute, jealousy, theft |
| **Feud** | Kin groups | Segmentary | Revenge, honor |
| **Raid** | Task group | Opportunistic | Loot, captives, prestige |
| **Battle** | Warrior bands | Planned | Territory, resources |
| **Warfare** | Armies | Institutional | Conquest, ideology |

## Bioarchaeological Evidence

### Trauma Patterns
| Trauma Type | Weapon | Interpretation |
|-------------|--------|----------------|
| **Parry fractures** | Blunt/sharp | Defense, interpersonal |
| **Cranial depression** | Club, mace | Close combat, execution |
| **Projectile points embedded** | Arrow, spear | Distance attack, warfare |
| **Sharp force (cut marks)** | Knife, sword | Slashing, butchery |
| **Perimortem** | At death | Lethal violence |
| **Antemortem (healed)** | Survived | Chronic conflict |

### Demographic Patterns
- **Male bias**: Young adult males overrepresented
- **Age**: 15-35 peak
- **Sex**: Males > females (warfare); females (raids, domestic) [2]

## Archaeological Contexts

### Mass Graves
- **Crow Creek** (SD, USA, 1325 CE): 500+ massacred [3]
- **Schöningen** (Germany, 12,000 BCE): Spear victims
- **Talheim** (Germany, 5,000 BCE): LBK massacre
- **Jebel Sahaba** (Sudan, 13,000 BCE): Early warfare

### Fortifications
- **Jericho** (9,500 BCE): Tower, wall
- **Çatalhöyük**: No walls (dense housing as defense) [4]
- **Mycenae**: Cyclopean walls, citadel
- **Danebury**: Iron Age hillfort, multiple phases

### Weapon Deposits
- **Hjortspring** (Denmark, 350 BCE): War booty
- **Illerup Ådal** (Denmark, 200 CE): Roman-era arsenal
- **Ritual "killing"**: Bent, broken weapons (Celtic, Nordic)

## Social Causes

### Resource Competition
- **Territory**: Agricultural land, water, hunting grounds
- **Resources**: Flint, obsidian, salt, metal ores
- **Population pressure**: Carrying capacity exceeded

### Social Dynamics
- **Revenge/Feud**: Kin obligation, honor culture
- **Prestige**: Warrior prowess, status
- **Ideology**: Cosmic order, divine mandate

### Structural
- **State formation**: Coercion, expansion
- **Trade control**: Monopoly, tariffs
- **Demographic**: Youth bulge, polygyny
