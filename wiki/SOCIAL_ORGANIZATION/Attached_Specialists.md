# Attached Specialists

Attached specialists are full-time craft producers who work directly for elite or state institutions. Unlike independent artisans, they do not control their labor, materials, or distribution—their production serves elite political and economic strategies.

## Overview

Attached specialization emerges with the first states and complex chiefdoms. It represents a critical shift from kin-based production to institutional labor, enabling the mass production of prestige goods, weapons, and administrative tools.

## Characteristics

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Employment** | Full-time, year-round |
| **Patron** | Palace, temple, royal estate |
| **Materials** | Provided by institution (often imported) |
| **Schedule** | Fixed quotas, deadlines |
| **Output** | Owned by institution |
| **Compensation** | Rations (grain, wool, oil), housing, status |
| **Mobility** | Low (tied to institution) |

## Institutional Contexts

### Palace Workshops (Near East, Aegean, China)
- **Ur III Mesopotamia**: 500–1,000+ textile workers per city
- **Mycenaean Palaces**: 500+ textile workers at Pylos, Knossos
- **Shang China**: Bronze foundries, jade workshops, bone carvers
- **Egypt**: Temple/palace workshops (Amarna, Deir el-Medina)

### Temple Workshops
- **Mesopotamia**: Temple estates (Eanna at Uruk)
- **Egypt**: Temple of Amun (Karnak), Ptah (Memphis)
- **Mesoamerica**: Aztec calmecac, Inca acllawasi

### Royal Estates
- **Inka**: Acllas ("chosen women") weaving qompi cloth
- **Moche**: Royal workshops at Huaca de la Luna
- **Shang**: Royal bronze foundries, jade workshops

## Craft Categories

### Textiles (Most Documented)
- **Workers**: Predominantly women
- **Organization**: Workgroups (10–50), supervisors, ration accounts
- **Products**: Standardized cloth units, garments, sailcloth
- **Records**: Linear B (Pylos), cuneiform (Ur III), quipu (Inka)

### Metallurgy
- **Products**: Weapons, tools, vessels, prestige items
- **Organization**: Specialized smelters, casters, smiths
- **Control**: Bronze = state monopoly (tin scarcity)
- **Evidence**: Crucibles, molds, slag at palace sites

### Ceramics
- **Mass production**: Standardized forms, molds, stamps
- **Kilns**: Large, efficient, palace-controlled
- **Distribution**: Ration vessels, storage jars, prestige ware

### Stone & Lapidary
- **Materials**: Obsidian, flint, lapis, carnelian, jade, turquoise
- **Products**: Beads, seals, vessels, inlays, weapons
- **Context**: Palace workshops, tomb goods

### Other Specialties
- **Glass/faience**: Late Bronze Age, palace monopolies
- **Ivory/bone**: Combs, inlays, furniture fittings
- **Leather/wood**: Chariots, furniture, shields
- **Scribal**: Seal cutters, tablet makers

## Labor Organization

### Ration System
- **Standard rations**: Grain, oil, wool, beer
- **Differentiated**: By skill, rank, gender
- **Dependents**: Workers' families also fed
- **Accounting**: Monthly tablets, quipu

### Workgroups
- **Size**: 10–50 workers
- **Supervision**: Foremen, scribes, overseers
- **Quotas**: Daily/monthly production targets
- **Quality control**: Inspectors, seals

### Hierarchy
```
Patron (King/Temple) 
  → Overseer/Steward
    → Scribe/Accountant
      → Foreman/Overseer
        → Specialized Workers (teams)
          → Apprentices/Helpers
```

## Social Status

### Privileges
- **Provisioned**: Rations, housing, tools
- **Protection**: Patronage, legal protection
- **Status**: Above commoners, below elite
- **Knowledge**: Literacy (some), technical secrets

### Constraints
- **No autonomy**: Fixed schedules, quotas
- **Hereditary**: Often hereditary castes
- **Surveillance**: Accountability, penalties
- **Mobility**: Cannot leave without permission

## Archaeological Evidence

| Evidence | Indicates |
|----------|-----------|
| **Concentrated workshops** | Institutional production |
| **Standardized tools/products** | Institutional control |
| **Ration tablets/quipu** | Rationed labor |
| **Seal impressions** | Institutional control |
| **Prestige goods in elite contexts** | Attached output |
| **Worker housing clusters** | Institutional housing |
