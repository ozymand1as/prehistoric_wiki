# Gift Economy and Reciprocity

Gift economy and reciprocity are the fundamental mechanisms of social integration in pre-market societies. They create and maintain social bonds, distribute resources, and establish hierarchies through the obligations to give, receive, and reciprocate. [1]

## Overview

Unlike market exchange (simultaneous, equivalent, impersonal), gift exchange is delayed, non-equivalent, and deeply personal. The "spirit of the gift" (Mauss) creates enduring social ties that structure kinship, politics, and religion.

## Three Forms of Reciprocity (Sahlins)

### 1. Generalized Reciprocity
- **Context**: Close kin, intimate relations
- **Expectation**: No immediate return, no calculation [2]
- **Logic**: "From each according to ability, to each according to need"
- **Examples**: Parent-child, spouse-spouse, close siblings
- **Social effect**: Reinforces intimacy, trust

### 2. Balanced Reciprocity
- **Context**: Distant kin, friends, allies
- **Expectation**: Direct return, rough equivalence, defined timeframe [3]
- **Logic**: Quid pro quo, but socially embedded
- **Examples**: Gift exchange (Kula, potlatch), bridewealth, trade partnerships
- **Social effect**: Creates/maintains social distance, defines relationships

### 3. Negative Reciprocity
- **Context**: Strangers, enemies, out-groups
- **Expectation**: Get more than give, minimize loss [4]
- **Logic**: Maximize self-interest, "caveat emptor"
- **Examples**: Haggling, theft, sharp practice, warfare plunder
- **Social effect**: Reinforces boundaries, hostility

## Key Concepts

### The Hau (Spirit of the Gift) — Mauss
- **Inalienable**: Gift carries giver's identity
- **Obligation**: Threefold — give, receive, reciprocate
- **Refusal**: Rejects social relationship
- **Circulation**: Gift must keep moving

### Total Prestations (Mauss)
- **Whole society**: Economic, legal, moral, religious
- **Not just economy**: Marriage, ritual, politics, war
- **Examples**: Potlatch, Kula, moka, bridewealth

## Major Ethnographic Systems

### Kula Ring (Trobriand Islands, Malinowski)
- **Items**: Soulava (red shell necklaces), mwali (white armbands)
- **Direction**: Clockwise (necklaces), counterclockwise (armbands)
- **Partners**: Lifelong, inherited, across islands
- **Prestige**: Not owning, but giving
- **Magic**: Ensures safe voyage, successful exchange

### Potlatch (Northwest Coast, Boas/Kwakiutl)
- **Occasions**: Birth, marriage, death, naming, crest transfer
- **Host**: Chief/big man, distributes vast wealth
- **Guests**: Rival groups, validate status by accepting
- **Destruction**: Sometimes burning copper, blankets
- **Function**: Rank validation, redistribution, alliance

### Moka (Highlands Papua New Guinea, Strathern)
- **Currency**: Pigs, pearl shells, cash (modern)
- **Big men**: Competitive generosity
- **Escalation**: Each moka larger than last
- **Collapse**: When no successor, debt unpayable

### Bridewealth / Dowry
- **Bridewealth** (Africa, Oceania): Groom's kin → Bride's kin
  - Compensates loss of labor/reproductive capacity
  - Creates affinal alliance
- **Dowry** (Europe, India, China): Bride's kin → Groom/kin
  - Daughter's inheritance portion
  - Status competition

## Archaeological Visibility

### Exchange Networks
- **Exotic materials**: Obsidian, shells, amber, copper
- **Distance decay**: Quantity drops with distance
- **Prestige goods**: Non-utilitarian, high labor

### Standardized Items
- **Currency precursors**: Shell beads, copper ingots
- **Standard weights**: Mesopotamian shekel, Andean quipu

### Feasting Evidence
- **Middens**: Concentrated faunal remains
- **Vessels**: Oversized serving pottery
- **Structures**: Communal feasting halls

## Evolutionary Trajectory

| Stage | Reciprocity | Integration |
|-------|-------------|-------------|
| **Band** | Generalized | Kin-based |
| **Tribe** | Balanced (big man) | Kinship + achieved |
| **Chiefdom** | Redistribution | Rank-based |
| **State** | Taxation/Market | Institutional |
