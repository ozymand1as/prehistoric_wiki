# State Religion

State religion is the institutionalized, official cult of early states, characterized by professional priesthoods, standardized rituals, monumental temples, and the fusion of political and religious authority in the person of the ruler. [1]

## Overview

State religion transforms local, kin-based cults into a unified system that legitimizes the state, integrates diverse populations, and mobilizes resources for elite projects. It is a primary tool of political integration and ideological control.

## Characteristics

| Feature | Local/Clan Cult | State Religion |
|---------|-----------------|----------------|
| **Scope** | Kin/village | Entire polity |
| **Priesthood** | Part-time, kin-based | Professional, hierarchical |
| **Temples** | Shrines, household | Monumental, standardized |
| **Calendar** | Local, seasonal | Official, standardized |
| **Authority** | Elders, shamans | King/priesthood |
| **Resources** | Voluntary offerings | Taxation, endowments |

## Key Institutions

### Priesthood
- **Hierarchy**: High priest → priests → attendants [2]
- **Training**: Scribal, ritual, astronomical
- **Privileges**: Tax-exempt, land, rations
- **Roles**: Ritual, divination, administration, education

### Temples
- **Economic**: Landholders, employers, redistributors [3]
- **Architectural**: Monumental, symbolic, axial
- **Functions**: Ritual, storage, administration, education
- **Endowments**: Land, labor, tribute, trade

### Calendar & Festivals
- **Standardized**: Agricultural, royal, cosmic cycles [4]
- **Public**: Processions, sacrifices, feasts, games
- **Political**: Coronation, victory, renewal, legitimacy

## Ruler as Religious Figure

### Divine Kingship
- **Mesopotamia**: Steward of city-god (not divine)
- **Egypt**: Living god (Horus/Osiris)
- **China**: Son of Heaven (Mandate of Heaven)
- **Mesoamerica**: Shaman-king, portal to gods
- **Andes**: Son of Sun, ancestor of people

### Ritual Duties
- **First fruits**: Agricultural fertility
- **New Year**: Cosmic renewal
- **War**: Divine sanction, victory offerings
- **Building**: Temple foundation, dedication
- **Crisis**: Drought, flood, epidemic, eclipse

## Temple Economy

### Resources
- **Land**: Temple estates (up to 1/3 land in Sumer)
- **Labor**: Corvée, attached specialists, dependents
- **Trade**: Temple merchants, foreign expeditions
- **Storage**: Granaries, treasuries, workshops

### Redistribution
- **Rations**: Priests, workers, orphans, widows
- **Feasts**: Public festivals, royal events
- **Relief**: Famine, flood, war relief
- **Ritual**: Offerings, sacrifices, processions

## Ideological Functions

### Legitimacy
- **Divine sanction**: Ruler chosen by gods
- **Cosmic order**: Ruler maintains maat/rita/dharma
- **Intermediary**: Only ruler accesses high gods

### Integration
- **Shared cults**: Pan-polity festivals
- **Pilgrimage**: Central shrines, oracle centers
- **Symbolism**: Royal iconography, cosmic symbolism

### Control
- **Surveillance**: Confession, oracle, informant
- **Ideology**: Royal propaganda, monumental art
- **Coercion**: Temple police, judicial ordeal
