# Descent Systems

Descent systems are the cultural rules that determine how individuals affiliate with kin groups through parentage. They structure inheritance, residence, marriage, and political allegiance across all human societies.

## Overview

Descent is not biological but cultural—a system for recognizing socially significant kin ties. All societies recognize both parents biologically, but descent systems select which line determines group membership.

## Major Descent Types

### Unilineal Descent
**Principle**: Affiliation through one sex only.

#### Patrilineal (Agnatic)
- **Affiliation**: Father's line only
- **Inheritance**: Sons inherit father's group, property
- **Residence**: Patrilocal (wife moves to husband's village)
- **Prevalence**: ~60% of documented societies
- **Examples**: China (traditional), Near East, pastoral Africa, Highlands PNG

#### Matrilineal (Uterine)
- **Affiliation**: Mother's line only
- **Inheritance**: Sister's sons (nephews) inherit uncle's property
- **Residence**: Matrilocal/avunculocal (husband moves to wife's village, or to maternal uncle)
- **Prevalence**: ~15% of societies
- **Examples**: Minangkabau, Navajo, Trobriand, Ashanti, Khasi

### Double (Duolineal) Descent
- **Principle**: Different properties through each line
- **Patrilineal**: Land, political office, surname
- **Matrilineal**: Movable wealth, ritual roles, spiritual identity
- **Examples**: Yako (Nigeria), Herero (Namibia), some Australian groups

### Cognatic (Bilateral) Descent
- **Affiliation**: Both parents' lines equally
- **Kindred**: Ego-centered network of relatives
- **No corporate groups**: No permanent descent groups
- **Prevalence**: ~20% (foragers, industrial societies)
- **Examples**: Inuit, !Kung, modern Western societies

### Ambilineal Descent
- **Choice**: Individual chooses father's or mother's group
- **Flexibility**: Optimizes resource access
- **Groups**: Ramages (Samoa), houses (Polynesia)
- **Examples**: Samoa, Maori, Tlingit, Kwaio

## Corporate Descent Groups

### Lineage
- **Depth**: 5-10 generations
- **Members**: Trace exact genealogical links
- **Corporate**: Collective land, ritual, defense
- **Segmentation**: Major/minor segments (segmentary lineage)

### Clan
- **Depth**: >10 generations (mythical ancestor)
- **Members**: Cannot trace all links
- **Totemic**: Often animal/plant emblem
- **Exogamous**: No marriage within clan

### Phratry / Moiety
- **Phratry**: Group of clans (3+)
- **Moiety**: Two complementary halves of society
- **Function**: Marriage regulation, ritual opposition

## Functions of Descent

| Function | Mechanism |
|----------|-----------|
| **Inheritance** | Land, titles, property pass along line |
| **Marriage** | Exogamy rules (no same clan/lineage) |
| **Residence** | Virilocal/uxorilocal/avunculocal |
| **Political** | Segmentary opposition, alliance |
| **Ritual** | Ancestor worship, totemic rites |
| **Economic** | Collective labor, resource rights |

## Archaeological Inference

| Evidence | Descent Inference |
|----------|-------------------|
| **Burial clustering** | Lineage/clan cemeteries |
| **Grave goods by sex** | Patrilineal vs matrilineal wealth |
| **Residence patterns** | Virilocal vs uxorilocal |
| **Settlement layout** | Segmentary vs centralized |
| **Iconography** | Totemic symbols, ancestor figures |
