# Early State

The early state represents a qualitative leap in social organization: a centralized political authority with a monopoly on force, territorial administration, social stratification, and bureaucratic institutions. States emerge independently in six primary regions during the Bronze Age.

## Overview

States differ from chiefdoms in having: (1) a professional bureaucracy, (2) territorial rather than kinship-based administration, (3) legal codes and courts, (4) standing military/police, (5) taxation in staple/wealth, (6) urban centers with monumental architecture, and (7) writing/administrative technology.

## Defining Characteristics (Service 1975; Claessen & Skalník 1978)

| Feature | Early State | Chiefdom |
|---------|-------------|----------|
| **Administration** | Bureaucracy, officials | Kin-based, personal |
| **Territory** | Defined, administered | Personal loyalty zones |
| **Law** | Codified, courts | Custom, chief's decree |
| **Force** | Standing army, police | Warrior retinue |
| **Taxation** | Regular, institutionalized | Tribute, irregular |
| **Stratification** | Classes (rulers, officials, commoners, slaves) | Rank (chief, nobles, commoners) |
| **Urbanism** | Cities >5,000, specialization | Centers <5,000 |
| **Writing** | Administrative, literary | Tokens, seals, mnemonics |
| **Ideology** | State religion, divine kingship | Chiefly ritual, ancestor cult |

## Primary State Formation (Six Cradles)

### 1. Mesopotamia (Sumer) - Earliest (~3,500 BCE)
- **Uruk Period** (4,000-3,100 BCE): First cities, temple economies, proto-cuneiform
- **Early Dynastic** (2,900-2,350 BCE): City-states, kingship, warfare, law codes
- **Akkadian Empire** (2,334-2,154 BCE): First multi-ethnic empire
- **Key innovations**: Cuneiform, wheel, plow, bronze, law codes (Ur-Nammu)

### 2. Egypt - Unified ~3,100 BCE
- **Predynastic** (4,000-3,100 BCE): Competing chiefdoms → unification
- **Old Kingdom** (2,686-2,181 BCE): Divine kingship, pyramid bureaucracy
- **Middle Kingdom** (2,055-1,650 BCE): Reunification, literature, forts
- **Key innovations**: Hieroglyphs, papyrus, stone architecture, nilometer

### 3. Indus Valley - Mature ~2,600 BCE
- **Early Harappan** (3,300-2,600 BCE): Regional centers, early script
- **Mature Harappan** (2,600-1,900 BCE): Planned cities, standardization, trade
- **Key innovations**: Grid cities, sanitation, weights/measures, Indus script

### 4. China (Erlitou → Shang) - Erlitou ~1,900 BCE
- **Longshan** (3,000-1,900 BCE): Walled towns, social hierarchy
- **Erlitou** (1,900-1,500 BCE): Palace complex, bronze ritual vessels
- **Shang** (1,600-1,046 BCE): Oracle bones, bronze industry, royal tombs
- **Key innovations**: Bronze casting, oracle bones, writing, chariots

### 5. Mesoamerica (Olmec → Maya) - Olmec ~1,400 BCE
- **Early Formative** (2,000-1,000 BCE): Villages, pottery, maize
- **Olmec** (1,400-400 BCE): Colossal heads, centers, jade, writing precursors
- **Maya** (1,000 BCE-900 CE): City-states, calendar, mathematics
- **Key innovations**: Maize nixtamalization, rubber, zero, Long Count

### 6. Andes (Norte Chico → Chavín) - Norte Chico ~3,000 BCE
- **Preceramic** (3,500-1,800 BCE): Monumental architecture without pottery
- **Chavín** (900-200 BCE): Temple complex, interaction sphere, iconography
- **Moche** (100-800 CE): Urban centers, irrigation, metallurgy
- **Key innovations**: Cotton fishing nets, camelid domestication, terrace farming

## State Institutions

### Bureaucracy
- **Officials**: Scribes, tax collectors, granary keepers, judges, generals
- **Hierarchy**: Central → provincial → local
- **Records**: Tax rolls, census, land surveys, legal cases, royal correspondence
- **Seals/Sealings**: Authorization, ownership, security

### Law & Justice
- **Law codes**: Ur-Nammu (Sumer), Hammurabi (Babylon), Tang (China)
- **Principles**: Lex talionis, class-based penalties, state as prosecutor
- **Courts**: Local elders → royal judges → king's court
- **Enforcement**: Police, bailiffs, punishments (fines, mutilation, death)

### Military
- **Standing core**: Professional soldiers, charioteers, royal guard
- **Levy**: Conscripted commoners (corvée labor)
- **Logistics**: Supply depots, roads, fortifications
- **Technology**: Bronze weapons, chariots, composite bow, siege engines

### Taxation & Redistribution
- **Staple finance**: Grain, labor (corvée), textiles collected → stored → redistributed
- **Wealth finance**: Metals, luxury goods controlled by elite
- **Tax units**: Household, land area, production (cadastral surveys)
- **Public works**: Irrigation, walls, temples, roads, granaries

## Urbanism & Administration

### Capital Cities
| Civilization | Capital | Population | Features |
|--------------|---------|------------|----------|
| **Sumer** | Uruk, Ur, Lagash | 20,000-50,000 | Temples, walls, canals |
| **Egypt** | Memphis, Thebes | 30,000+ | Palaces, temples, necropolis |
| **Indus** | Mohenjo-daro, Harappa | 30,000-50,000 | Grid, citadel, Great Bath |
| **China** | Erlitou, Anyang | 10,000-30,000 | Palace, workshops, tombs |
| **Olmec** | San Lorenzo, La Venta | 5,000-15,000 | Mounds, plazas, sculptures |
| **Norte Chico** | Caral | 3,000+ | Platform mounds, sunken plazas |

### Settlement Hierarchy
```
Capital (10,000-50,000+)
    ↓
Provincial Centers (5,000-10,000) — administration, garrison, storage
    ↓
Towns (1,000-5,000) — markets, crafts, local admin
    ↓
Villages (100-1,000) — primary production
    ↓
Hamlets/Farmsteads (<100) — subsistence
```

## Ideology & Legitimation

### Divine Kingship
- **Mesopotamia**: King as steward of city-god (not divine)
- **Egypt**: Pharaoh as living god (Horus/Osiris)
- **China**: Mandate of Heaven (moral legitimacy, revocable)
- **Mesoamerica**: King as shaman-ruler, portal to ancestors/gods
- **Andes**: King as descendant of sun, intermediary

### State Religion
- **Temples**: Economic centers, landholders, employers
- **Priesthoods**: Professional, hierarchical, literate
- **Calendar**: Ritual schedule, agricultural coordination
- **Sacrifice**: Animal, human (retainer, captive), auto-sacrifice

## Collapse & Transformation

### Common Stressors
- **Environmental**: Drought, soil salinization, flood shifts
- **Military**: Overextension, invasion, internal revolt
- **Economic**: Trade disruption, resource depletion, inflation
- **Political**: Succession crises, factionalism, loss of legitimacy

### Outcomes
- **Fragmentation**: Empire → competing states (Akkad → Ur III, Shang → Zhou)
- **Transformation**: State reorganization (Old Kingdom → Middle Kingdom)
- **Collapse**: Systemic failure, population decline (Indus, Late Bronze Age)
- **Continuity**: Cultural persistence despite political change
