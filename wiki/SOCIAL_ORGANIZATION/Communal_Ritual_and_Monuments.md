# Communal Ritual and Monuments

Communal ritual and monument building are among the most visible archaeological signatures of social integration. They mobilize labor, express shared identity, and materialize collective beliefs.

## Overview

Monument construction requires labor mobilization beyond the household, implying social organization capable of coordinating large groups. The monuments themselves become enduring symbols of collective identity and cosmic order.

## Labor Mobilization

### Scale
- **Göbekli Tepe**: Hundreds of workers, decades
- **Stonehenge**: Thousands of person-years
- **Pyramids**: Tens of thousands, years
- **Cahokia**: Millions of basket-loads

### Organization
- **Corvée**: Obligatory labor service
- **Feasting**: Compensation, social bonding
- **Ritual**: Labor as worship, pilgrimage
- **Identity**: Collective effort builds cohesion

## Ritual Activities

### Feasting
- **Scale**: Hundreds to thousands
- **Evidence**: Massive faunal assemblages, oversized vessels
- **Function**: Labor payment, alliance, status display

### Processions
- **Routes**: Causeways, avenues, riverine
- **Participants**: Elite, priests, populace
- **Function**: Territorial claim, cosmic reenactment

### Sacrifice
- **Animal**: Cattle, pigs, llamas
- **Human**: Retainer, captive, child (rare)
- **Votive**: Objects, weapons, precious goods

### Astronomical Alignment
- **Solstices**: Stonehenge, Newgrange, Chankillo
- **Equinoxes**: Chichen Itza, Mnajdra
- **Lunar**: Stonehenge, Callanish
- **Stellar**: Pyramids, Nazca lines
