# Independent Craftspeople

Independent craftspeople are artisans who produce goods for market or exchange on their own account, controlling their labor, materials, schedule, and distribution. They represent the earliest and most persistent form of craft specialization.

## Overview

Independent production is the oldest form of specialization, emerging in the Neolithic when households produced surplus for exchange. Unlike attached specialists, independents control their means of production and bear market risk.

## Characteristics

| Feature | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Autonomy** | Controls labor, materials, schedule |
| **Risk** | Bears market/production risk |
| **Scale** | Household or small workshop |
| **Distribution** | Direct (market, peddling) or merchant |
| **Materials** | Self-procured (local or traded) |
| **Schedule** | Flexible, seasonal |

## Historical Trajectory

### Neolithic (6,000–3,000 BCE)
- **Context**: Village households
- **Crafts**: Pottery, flintknapping, weaving, beadmaking
- **Exchange**: Local, reciprocal, periodic markets
- **Evidence**: Household workshops, diverse toolkits

### Chalcolithic/Bronze Age (4,500–1,200 BCE)
- **Coexistence**: With attached specialists
- **Niche**: Utilitarian goods, local markets
- **Competition**: Attached specialists dominate prestige goods
- **Evidence**: Village workshops, diverse toolkits, local distribution

### Iron Age/Classical (1,200 BCE–500 CE)
- **Urban growth**: Craft quarters in cities
- **Guilds**: Emerging (Greece, Rome, China)
- **Market integration**: Monetized, long-distance

## Craft Categories

### Pottery
- **Neolithic**: Household, coiled, pit-fired
- **Wheel-thrown**: Specialist potters, village workshops
- **Kilns**: Communal or household
- **Distribution**: Local/regional markets

### Lithics (Flint/Obsidian)
- **Neolithic**: Part-time, mining communities (Spiennes, Grimes Graves)
- **Bronze Age**: Decline (metal replaces stone)
- **Persistence**: Gunflints, strike-a-lights (historic)

### Textiles
- **Neolithic/Bronze Age**: Household surplus sold locally
- **Spinning**: Universal female activity, surplus sold
- **Weaving**: More specialized, loom ownership

### Metalworking (Early)
- **Chalcolithic**: Native copper, cold-hammering
- **Bronze Age**: Independent smiths for tools, repair
- **Competition**: Palace smiths for prestige, independents for tools

### Other Crafts
- **Beadmaking**: Shell, stone, glass (part-time)
- **Basketry/Matting**: Universal, seasonal
- **Leatherworking**: Household to specialist
- **Carpentry/Joinery**: Building, furniture, tools

## Organization

### Household Production
- **Unit**: Nuclear/extended family
- **Division**: Gender, age
- **Seasonality**: Agricultural off-season
- **Apprenticeship**: Kin-based, 5–10 years

### Small Workshops
- **Master + apprentices/journeymen**
- **Urban**: Craft streets/quarters (later)
- **Rural**: Village smith, potter

### Market Integration
- **Direct**: Producer → consumer (periodic markets)
- **Indirect**: Merchant intermediaries
- **Barter → Money**: Gradual monetization

## Social Position

| Aspect | Independent | Attached |
|--------|-------------|----------|
| **Autonomy** | High | Low |
| **Risk** | High (market) | Low (rationed) |
| **Wealth** | Variable | Stable, modest |
| **Status** | Respected, autonomous | Dependent, provisioned |
| **Mobility** | High | Low |
