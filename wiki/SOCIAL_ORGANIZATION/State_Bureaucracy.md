# State Bureaucracy

State bureaucracy is the professional administrative apparatus that manages the resources, population, and territory of early states. It replaces kin-based governance with impersonal, rule-based administration.

## Overview

Bureaucracy emerges when state scale exceeds the capacity of personal rule. It standardizes administration through offices, records, laws, and specialized personnel, enabling the state to extract resources, enforce decisions, and maintain order across large territories.

## Bureaucratic Institutions

### Offices & Hierarchies
| Level | Near East | Egypt | China | Inka |
|-------|-----------|-------|-------|------|
| **Supreme** | King/Ensi | Pharaoh | Emperor | Sapa Inka |
| **Central** | Sukkalmah, Viziers | Vizier, Overseers | Three Excellencies | Apus (4) |
| **Provincial** | Ensi (governors) | Nomarchs | Commanderies | Tucuyricoc |
| **Local** | Rabiānu (mayors) | Village heads | County magistrates | Kuraka |

### Specialized Departments
- **Treasury/Taxation**: Grain, livestock, labor, wealth
- **Judiciary**: Courts, judges, law codes
- **Military**: Standing army, logistics, conscription
- **Public Works**: Irrigation, roads, temples, walls
- **Granaries/Storage**: Collection, redistribution, relief
- **Census/Cadastre**: Population, land, livestock registers
- **Diplomacy**: Envoys, treaties, hostages
- **Ritual/Calendar**: State religion, astronomy

## Administrative Technologies

### Writing & Record-Keeping
| Technology | Culture | Function |
|------------|---------|----------|
| **Cuneiform tablets** | Sumer, Akkad, Hittite | Administration, law, literature |
| **Hieroglyphic/Hieratic** | Egypt | Monuments, administration |
| **Linear B** | Mycenaean | Palace inventories |
| **Quipu** | Inka | Census, tribute, history |
| **Seals/Sealings** | Near East, Indus, Aegean | Authorization, ownership |

### Standardization
- **Weights & measures**: Royal standards (shekel, deben, qian)
- **Legal codes**: Hammurabi, Tang, Twelve Tables
- **Calendars**: Agricultural, ritual, administrative
- **Currency**: Silver, copper, cowries, cloth units

## Bureaucratic Personnel

### Scribes
- **Training**: Years (edubba, House of Life)
- **Status**: Elite, literate, tax-exempt
- **Roles**: Tax collector, judge, envoy, priest, steward
- **Tools**: Tablets, papyrus, stylus, palette

### Officials
- **Appointment**: Merit, heredity, patronage
- **Accountability**: Audits, inspections, seals
- **Rotation**: Prevent local entrenchment
- **Corruption**: Audits, harsh penalties

## Resource Extraction

### Taxation
| Type | Basis | Collection |
|------|-------|------------|
| **Land tax** | Cadastral survey | Grain/share |
| **Poll tax** | Census | Labor/grain |
| **Trade tax** | Ports, markets | Silver/goods |
| **Corvée** | Labor obligation | Public works |

### Redistribution
- **Rations**: State workers, soldiers, priests
- **Public works**: Irrigation, roads, temples
- **Relief**: Famine, flood, war relief
- **Feasts**: Ritual, political integration

## Control Mechanisms

### Surveillance
- **Inspectors**: Royal envoys, "eyes of the king"
- **Spies**: Informants, secret police
- **Hostages**: Elite children at court
- **Garrisons**: Military presence in provinces

### Legal Enforcement
- **Law codes**: Public, posted, standardized
- **Courts**: Local → regional → royal
- **Punishment**: Fines, mutilation, death, exile
- **Royal pardon**: Prerogative of mercy
