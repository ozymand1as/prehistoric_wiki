# Household and Family

The household is the fundamental residential, economic, and reproductive unit in human societies. Its form varies widely but universally serves as the nexus of production, consumption, reproduction, and socialization. [1]

## Overview

The household is not synonymous with family. It is a co-residential group sharing resources, labor, and risk. Its composition, size, and internal organization vary with subsistence, economy, and cultural norms.

## Household Types

### Nuclear Family
- **Composition**: Parents + dependent children
- **Prevalence**: Industrial societies, some foragers [2]
- **Economy**: Independent production/consumption
- **Residence**: Neolocal (new household at marriage)

### Extended Family
- **Composition**: Nuclear + grandparents, aunts/uncles, cousins [3]
- **Prevalence**: Agricultural, pastoral societies
- **Economy**: Corporate labor, shared resources
- **Residence**: Patrilocal, matrilocal, or avunculocal

### Compound / Joint Family
- **Composition**: Multiple nuclear families (brothers' families)
- **Authority**: Senior male/female
- **Economy**: Joint production, common treasury
- **Division**: Fission at senior death or conflict

### Composite / Polygynous Household
- **Composition**: Husband + multiple wives + children
- **Structure**: Each wife + children = sub-household
- **Authority**: Husband (or senior wife)
- **Prevalence**: Polygynous societies (Africa, Islam, historical)

### Stem Family
- **Rule**: One child (usually eldest son) stays, inherits
- **Others**: Leave (marry out, migrate, clergy)
- **Continuity**: Household persists across generations
- **Examples**: Traditional Japan, Ireland, Pyrenees

### Communal / Corporate Household
- **Composition**: Multiple families, non-kin
- **Basis**: Religious (monastery), economic (commune), military (barracks)
- **Governance**: Rules, elected/appointed leaders

## Domestic Cycle (Goody)

### Phases
1. **Formation**: Marriage, new household
2. **Expansion**: Children born, grow
3. **Consolidation**: Children work, contribute
4. **Dispersal**: Children leave (marriage, migration)
5. **Contraction**: Parents age, die
6. **Dissolution/Reformation**: Inheritance, new cycle

### Duration
- **Forager**: Short (high mobility)
- **Peasant**: Long (land tenure)
- **Urban**: Variable

## Economic Functions

### Production
- **Subsistence**: Food, craft, fuel
- **Market**: Surplus, specialized craft
- **Labor**: Household members + hired

### Consumption
- **Pooling**: Income/resources pooled
- **Allocation**: Head decides or collective
- **Distribution**: By age, gender, status

### Reproduction
- **Biological**: Childbearing, childrearing
- **Social**: Socialization, education
- **Care**: Elderly, sick, disabled

## Archaeological Identification

| Feature | Household Inference |
|---------|---------------------|

| **Storage capacity** | Surplus, household size |

| **Burials** | Intramural = household members |
| **Artifact distribution** | Activity areas, gender |
