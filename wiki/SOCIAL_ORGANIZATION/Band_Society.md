# Band Society

The band is the simplest and most ancient form of human social organization. Characterized by small size, egalitarian ethos, kinship-based membership, and face-to-face decision-making, bands were the universal human social form throughout the Paleolithic and persist among hunter-gatherers today. [1]

## Overview

Bands are small (20-50 people), mobile or semi-mobile foraging groups bound by kinship, marriage, and friendship. They lack formal leadership, permanent institutions, and social stratification. Decision-making is by consensus, and authority is situational—based on age, experience, skill, or personality—not institutionalized.

## Defining Characteristics

| Feature | Band Organization |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Size** | 20-50 persons (10-30 adults + children) |
| **Subsistence** | Foraging (hunting, gathering, fishing) |
| **Mobility** | High (daily to seasonal moves) |
| **Leadership** | Situational, informal, non-coercive |
| **Decision-making** | Consensus, discussion, no voting |
| **Social stratification** | None (egalitarian) |
| **Property** | Personal possessions only; land open access |
| **Kinship** | Bilateral/cognatic; flexible residence |
| **Law** | Custom, gossip, ridicule, avoidance |
| **Warfare** | Feuding, raiding; no standing warriors |

## Social Structure

### Membership & Composition
- **Core**: Nuclear families linked by parent-child, sibling ties [2]
- **Fluid**: Individuals/families move between bands freely
- **Fission-fusion**: Groups split/merge seasonally or socially
- **No fixed boundaries**: Band identity is relational, not territorial

### Kinship & Marriage
- **Descent**: Bilateral (both sides recognized)
- **Residence**: Multilocal (uxorilocal, virilocal, neolocal flexible) [3]
- **Marriage**: Exogamous (outside band), often arranged but consensual
- **Incest taboo**: Extends to all band members (classificatory kin)
- **Alliance**: Marriage creates ties between bands

### Age & Gender Organization
- **Elders**: Respect for experience; advisors, not rulers [4]
- **Gender complementarity**: Distinct but valued roles (hunt/gather)
- **Initiation**: Rites of passage (variable, often minimal)
- **Childrearing**: Communal, alloparenting common

## Decision-Making & Leadership

### Consensus Process
1. **Discussion**: Open, prolonged, all adults participate [5]
2. **Influential voices**: Skilled hunters, elders, shamans, persuasive speakers
3. **No voting**: Continue until agreement or dissenters withdraw
4. **Implementation**: Voluntary compliance; no enforcement mechanism

### Leadership Types
| Type | Basis | Authority | Example |
|------|-------|-----------|---------|
| **Task leader** | Skill (hunt, heal, make) | Situational, specific | Best tracker leads hunt |
| **Elder** | Age, experience, memory | Advisory, moral | Mediates disputes |
| **Shaman** | Spiritual access | Ritual, healing | Intermediary with spirits |
| **Big man (incipient)** | Generosity, charisma | Influence, not authority | Feast sponsor |

### Conflict Resolution
- **Gossip/ridicule**: Primary social control
- **Avoidance**: Physical separation (band fission) [6]
- **Mediation**: Elders, kin intervene
- **Compensation**: Gift-giving restores relations
- **Execution/expulsion**: Extreme, rare (sorcerers, repeat killers)

## Economy: Immediate Return Egalitarianism

### Sharing Norms
- **Demand sharing**: "Give me" → compliance expected [7]
- **Generalized reciprocity**: Give without immediate return expectation
- **Leveling mechanisms**: Prevent accumulation (mockery, theft, destruction)
- **Food distribution**: Hunter's share → family → band (proportional)

### Property Concepts
- **Personal**: Tools, ornaments, clothing (small, portable) [8]
- **Communal**: Territory, water, fire, knowledge
- **No land ownership**: Use rights only, no exclusion
- **Inheritance**: Minimal (personal items destroyed/buried)

## Mobility & Territory

### Movement Patterns
- **Residential moves**: Entire camp relocates (days to weeks)
- **Logistical moves**: Task groups out from base (hours to days)
- **Seasonal rounds**: Predictable resource tracking
- **Home range**: 500-5,000 km² depending on productivity

### Territoriality
- **Not exclusive**: Overlapping ranges, shared resources
- **Core areas**: Recognized but not defended
- **Boundary negotiation**: Social, not military
- **Strangers**: Cautious interaction, gift exchange

## Band Types (Ethnographic Variation)

| Type | Environment | Mobility | Size | Example |
|------|-------------|----------|------|---------|
| **Simple foragers** | Tropical forest | High, frequent | 15-30 | Hadza, Mbuti, Agta |
| **Complex foragers** | Rich aquatic/coastal | Lower, semi-sedentary | 30-100+ | NW Coast, Calusa |
| **Equestrian foragers** | Plains/steppe | High, wide range | 30-80 | Comanche, Mongol (pre-state) |
| **Arctic foragers** | Tundra/ice | Seasonal, specialized | 20-50 | Inuit, Netsilik |

## Archaeological Visibility

### Site Patterns
- **Small campsites**: <500 m², thin deposits
- **Hearth-centered**: Activity areas around fire
- **Short occupation**: Days to weeks per episode
- **Repeated reoccupation**: Palimpsests over millennia
- **Portable toolkits**: Light, versatile, curated

### Material Correlates
- **No status goods**: Uniform grave goods (if any)
- **No monuments**: Labor not mobilized beyond band
- **No storage facilities**: Immediate consumption
- **Egalitarian toolkits**: Similar kits across individuals
- **Raw material sourcing**: Direct procurement, not trade

## Evolutionary Context

### Paleolithic Universality
- **Lower Paleolithic**: Bands of H. erectus, H. heidelbergensis
- **Middle Paleolithic**: Neanderthal bands, early H. sapiens bands
- **Upper Paleolithic**: Modern human bands, symbolic complexity
- **Mesolithic**: Band diversification, intensification

### Transition to Tribes
- **Triggers**: Population pressure, resource intensification, storage
- **Process**: Bands cluster → shared identity → tribal institutions
- **Archaeological**: Larger sites, cemeteries, storage, rituals

## Modern Survivals & Ethnographic Analogy

### Living Band Societies
- **Hadza** (Tanzania): ~1,000 foragers, extensive study
- **Mbuti/Efe** (Congo): Forest foragers, net hunters
- **San** (Kalahari): !Kung, G/wi, extensive documentation
- **Aboriginal Australians**: Pre-contact band organization
- **Inuit**: Arctic adaptation, seasonal aggregation
- **Paliyan, Nayaka** (S. India): Forest foragers

### Critiques of Analogy
- **Marginalization**: Modern foragers in worst habitats
- **Contact effects**: State pressure, missionization, markets
- **Variability**: No single "band model"
- **Projection**: Using present to interpret deep past
