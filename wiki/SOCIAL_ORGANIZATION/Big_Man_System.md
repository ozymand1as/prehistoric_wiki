# Big Man System

The big man system is a form of leadership based on achieved status rather than heredity. Big men compete for influence through competitive generosity, creating a dynamic, unstable political landscape characteristic of many tribal societies. [1]

## Overview

The big man is not a chief. He has no coercive authority, no office to inherit, and no guaranteed succession. His power rests entirely on personal charisma, economic success, and the ability to mobilize others through reciprocal obligation.

## Characteristics

| Feature | Big Man | Chief |
|---------|---------|-------|
| **Status** | Achieved | Ascribed |
| **Authority** | Persuasive | Coercive |
| **Succession** | Open competition | Hereditary |
| **Wealth** | To give away | To display/hoard |
| **Office** | None (personal) | Institutional |

## The Moka Cycle (Highlands PNG)

### Competitive Generosity
1. **Receive**: Pigs, shells, labor from supporters [2]
2. **Invest**: In pigs, gardens, trade
3. **Host feast**: Distribute massively to rivals/allies
4. **Obligate**: Recipients must reciprocate larger
5. **Escalate**: Each moka larger than last

### Functions
- **Redistribution**: Surplus → supporters → allies [3]
- **Alliance**: Creates cross-group ties
- **Status**: Validates big man's rank
- **Integration**: Binds segments together

### Instability
- **No succession**: Death = vacuum
- **Debt trap**: Escalating obligations
- **Fission**: Group splits if no successor
- **Warfare**: Failed moka → conflict

## Big Man vs. Chief Economies

| Aspect | Big Man | Chief |
|--------|---------|-------|
| **Surplus** | Mobilized for feasts | Collected as tribute |
| **Redistribution** | Personal, feast-based | Institutional, regular |
| **Labor** | Voluntary, feast-motivated | Obligatory (corvée) |
| **Prestige goods** | Circulated, given away | Hoarded, displayed |

## Regional Variations

### Melanesia (Highlands PNG)
- **Moka**: Pig-shell exchange cycle
- **Big men**: Multiple per tribe
- **Warfare**: Failed moka → raids

### Polynesia
- **Big men**: Pre-state precursors
- **Chiefs**: Hereditary, sacred rank
- **Transition**: Big man → chief with population growth [4]

### Africa (East African pastoralists)
- **Age-sets**: Complement big men
- **Cattle**: Wealth basis
- **Raiding**: Wealth accumulation

### Amazonia
- **Headmen**: Weak big men
- **Fission**: Frequent village splitting
- **Shamans**: Complement political power
