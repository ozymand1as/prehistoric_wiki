# Redistribution and Tribute

Redistribution and tribute are centralized resource flows that distinguish chiefdoms and states from egalitarian societies. They concentrate surplus at political centers for reallocation, elite consumption, and public works.

## Overview

Unlike reciprocity (dyadic, voluntary), redistribution is centralized (center → periphery) and tribute is obligatory (periphery → center). Both require storage, accounting, and coercive authority.

## Redistribution (Center → Periphery)

### Staple Finance
- **Resources**: Grain, livestock, labor
- **Collection**: Tax in kind (grain, corvée labor)
- **Storage**: Central granaries, silos, warehouses
- **Reallocation**: Rations to workers, feasts, emergency relief
- **Examples**: Ur III mesopotamia, Inka qollqa, Egyptian Old Kingdom

### Wealth Finance
- **Resources**: Prestige goods, metals, luxury items
- **Control**: Elite monopoly on sources, production
- **Distribution**: Retainers, diplomats, ritual deposits
- **Examples**: Bronze Age palace gifts, Mississippian copper, Mycenaean gold

## Tribute (Periphery → Center)

### Forms of Tribute
| Type | Source | Period |
|------|--------|--------|
| **Staple** | Grain, livestock, textiles | Neolithic+ |
| **Labor** | Corvée (construction, transport) | Bronze Age+ |
| **Wealth** | Metals, luxury goods, slaves | Bronze Age+ |
| **Military** | Troops, provisions | State period |

### Tribute Systems
| System | Center | Periphery | Mechanism |
|--------|--------|-----------|-----------|
| **Segmentary** | Chief | Villages | Lineage-based quotas |
| **Territorial** | State | Provinces | Cadastral assessment |
| **Vassal** | Empire | Client states | Treaties, hostages |
| **Trade** | Port polity | Hinterland | Port fees, monopolies |

## Political Economy

### Chiefdom Redistribution
- **Mechanism**: Chief collects → feasts → redistributes
- **Legitimacy**: Generosity validates rank
- **Instability**: Depends on personal charisma

### State Taxation
- **Institutionalized**: Offices, laws, schedules
- **Coercion**: Legal obligation, enforcement
- **Bureaucracy**: Scribes, assessors, collectors

### Inka Mit'a (Labor Tax)
- **Mit'a**: Rotational labor duty
- **Projects**: Roads, terraces, temples, mines
- **Reciprocity**: State provides food, cloth, feasts
- **Accounting**: Quipu records

## Archaeological Evidence

### Concentration
- **Storage facilities**: Disproportionate at centers
- **Sealings**: On stored goods, documents
- **Standardization**: Weights, measures, containers

### Distribution
- **Ration vessels**: Bevel-rim bowls (Uruk), standardized jars
- **Feasting deposits**: Massive faunal assemblages
- **Prestige goods**: Concentrated at elite residences
