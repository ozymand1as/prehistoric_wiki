# Craft Specialization

Craft specialization is the dedication of individuals or groups to specific productive activities beyond subsistence. It is a hallmark of social complexity, creating interdependence, generating surplus, and often serving elite interests. [1]

## Overview

Specialization ranges from part-time household production to full-time attached specialists working for elites. It transforms social relations by creating new dependencies, statuses, and economic flows.

## Types of Specialization

### Independent (Household) Specialization
- **Context**: Household produces for market/exchange [2]
- **Control**: Producer controls process, schedule, distribution
- **Scale**: Small, flexible
- **Examples**: Pottery, flintknapping, weaving in villages
- **Archaeology**: Workshops in residential areas, diverse tools

### Attached Specialization
- **Context**: Producer works for elite/state institution [3]
- **Control**: Patron controls materials, schedule, output
- **Scale**: Large, standardized
- **Examples**: Palace workshops (Mycenaean, Ur III, Shang), temple crafts
- **Archaeology**: Concentrated near elite architecture, standardized tools/products

## Dimensions of Specialization (Costin 1991)

| Dimension | Independent | Attached |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| **Context** | Household | Institution |
| **Concentration** | Dispersed | Nucleated |
| **Scale** | Small | Large |
| **Intensity** | Part-time | Full-time |
| **Skill** | Moderate | High |
| **Control** | Producer | Patron |

## Craft Categories & Specialization Trajectories

### Pottery
- **Neolithic**: Household, coiled, pit-fired
- **Chalcolithic**: Wheel-thrown, kiln-fired, specialized [4]
- **Bronze Age**: Mass production, standardization, factory-like (Uruk, Mycenaean)

### Metallurgy
- **Early**: Part-time, household (native copper)
- **Chalcolithic**: Smelting specialists, casting
- **Bronze Age**: Full-time, attached (palace smiths, bronze foundries)

### Textiles
- **Neolithic**: Household, spindle/loom
- **Bronze Age**: Palace workshops, rationed workers (Ur III, Linear B)

### Lithics
- **Paleolithic**: Universal skill
- **Neolithic**: Specialized miners/knappers (flint mines)
- **Bronze Age**: Decline (metal replaces stone)

### Other Crafts
- **Beadmaking**: Specialized (shell, stone, faience)
- **Seal carving**: Specialized (administrative)
- **Glass/glaze**: Late Bronze Age, attached

## Social Implications

### Status
- **Independent**: Respected, autonomous, variable wealth
- **Attached**: Dependent, but provisioned, elite access
- **Skill**: Respected, sometimes ritualized (smiths as liminal)

### Gender
- **Pottery**: Often female (Neolithic), male (wheel)
- **Weaving**: Predominantly female (spinning universal)
- **Metallurgy**: Predominantly male (ritual danger)
- **Lithics**: Variable, often male

### Knowledge Transmission
- **Apprenticeship**: 5-10 years, kin or institutional
- **Secrecy**: Trade secrets, ritual knowledge
- **Innovation**: Attached specialists → state-directed

## Archaeological Identification

### Workshop Evidence
| Indicator | Independent | Attached |
|-----------|-------------|----------|
| **Location** | Residential | Near palace/temple |
| **Tools** | Diverse, personal | Standardized, institutional |
| **Waste** | Dispersed | Concentrated dumps |
| **Products** | Variable | Standardized |
| **Raw material** | Local, diverse | Imported, controlled |

### Spatial Analysis
- **Cluster analysis**: Workshop districts vs household scatter
- **Raw material sourcing**: Controlled vs open access
- **Product distribution**: Market vs palace/temple/elite
