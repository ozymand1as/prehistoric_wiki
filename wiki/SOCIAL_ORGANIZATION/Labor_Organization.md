# Labor Organization

Labor organization refers to the social mechanisms by which work is coordinated, allocated, and compensated in prehistoric societies. It ranges from kinship-based cooperation to state-managed corvée and slavery.

## Overview

Labor organization evolves with social complexity. Egalitarian bands use voluntary cooperation; tribes add big-man mobilization; chiefdoms develop corvée and tribute labor; states institutionalize taxation, slavery, and standing workforces.

## Forms of Labor Organization

### 1. Kinship-Based Cooperation
- **Unit**: Household, extended family, clan
- **Motivation**: Mutual obligation, survival
- **Forms**: 
  - **Work parties**: Reciprocal labor (harvest, construction)
  - **Childcare**: Alloparenting, communal
  - **Defense**: Collective, kin-based
- **Compensation**: Reciprocal, delayed, social

### 2. Big Man Mobilization (Tribal)
- **Leader**: Big man (achieved status)
- **Mechanism**: Persuasion, feast sponsorship
- **Projects**: Gardens, men's houses, defense, ceremonies
- **Compensation**: Feast, prestige, future reciprocity
- **Voluntary**: No coercion, based on influence

### 3. Corvée Labor (Chiefdom/State)
- **Obligation**: Mandated labor service
- **Basis**: Territorial, household, or personal
- **Duration**: Fixed periods (days/months per year)
- **Projects**: Irrigation, walls, temples, roads, palaces
- **Compensation**: Rations, tax relief, protection
- **Examples**: Egyptian pyramids, Inka mit'a, Mesopotamian ziggurats

### 4. Slavery
- **Status**: Chattel, hereditary, war captives, debt
- **Roles**: Domestic, agricultural, mining, craft, military
- **Scale**: Household to state (Inka yanakuna, Roman latifundia)
- **Sources**: War, trade, birth, debt, crime
- **Archaeology**: Shackles, distinct burials, textual records

### 5. Standing Workforces (State)
- **Soldiers**: Professional, full-time, state-paid
- **Artisans**: Attached specialists (palace workshops)
- **Scribes/Bureaucrats**: Administrative labor
- **Priests**: Ritual labor, temple maintenance
- **Compensation**: Rations, land, status, tax exemption

## Labor Mobilization Mechanisms

| Mechanism | Society | Compulsion | Scale |
|-----------|---------|------------|-------|
| **Reciprocal work party** | Band/Tribe | Social | 10-50 |
| **Big man feast** | Tribe | Persuasion | 50-200 |
| **Chiefly corvée** | Chiefdom | Rank obligation | 100-1,000 |
| **State corvée** | State | Law | 1,000-100,000+ |
| **Slavery** | State/Chiefdom | Coercion | Variable |
| **Standing army** | State | Contract | 1,000-100,000+ |
| **Attached specialists** | State/Chiefdom | Patronage | 10-1,000 |

## Seasonal Scheduling

### Agricultural Calendar
- **Slack season**: Construction, craft, war (post-harvest)
- **Peak season**: All hands in fields (planting, harvest)
- **Ritual calendar**: Feasts, ceremonies structure labor

### Inka Mit'a (Model System)
- **Rotation**: Annual, rotational labor draft
- **Projects**: Roads, terraces, temples, mines, armies
- **Reciprocity**: State provides food, cloth, feasts
- **Accounting**: Quipu records, decimal organization

| **Construction** | Men (heavy), women (plaster, carry) |
| **Mining** | Men, slaves |

## Archaeological Evidence

| Evidence | Indicates |
|----------|-----------|
| **Massive architecture** | Corvée/state labor |
| **Worker housing clusters** | Institutional labor |
| **Ration vessels/tablets** | Rationed labor |
| **Quipu/tablets** | Labor accounting |
| **Shackles/constraints** | Slavery |
| **Mass graves** | Warfare/sacrifice |
| **Skeletal stress markers** | Heavy labor patterns |
