# Prestige Goods Economy

Prestige goods economies are systems where elites control the acquisition, production, and distribution of high-value, non-utilitarian items to legitimize power, create alliances, and distinguish themselves from commoners.

## Overview

Prestige goods are non-subsistence items whose value derives from rarity, exotic origin, skilled labor, or symbolic meaning. Control over their flow is a primary mechanism of elite power in chiefdoms and early states.

## Characteristics of Prestige Goods

| Attribute | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Non-utilitarian** | Not for subsistence production |
| **Exotic origin** | Distant sources, difficult access |
| **High labor** | Specialized craftsmanship |
| **Symbolic** | Encode status, identity, cosmology |
| **Restricted** | Elite access, sumptuary norms |
| **Durable** | Curated, heirloom, display |

### Metals
| Metal | Period | Significance |

## Control Mechanisms

### Source Control
- **Direct**: Elite expeditions, royal mines (Sinai turquoise, Nubian gold)
- **Indirect**: Tribute from source regions, trade monopolies

### Production Control
- **Attached specialists**: Palace workshops (Mycenaean, Ur III, Shang)
- **Sumptuary laws**: Restricted designs, materials, wearers

### Distribution Control
- **Gift giving**: Diplomatic, ritual, reward
- **Restricted circulation**: Sumptuary, sacred contexts
- **Burial deposition**: Removed from circulation (elite tombs)

## Economic Impact

### Elite Strategies
1. **Monopolize sources**: Direct or tributary control
2. **Sponsor specialists**: Attached craft production
3. **Manage exchange**: Gift diplomacy, marriage alliance
4. **Ritualize display**: Feasts, processions, burials

### Commoner Access
- **Limited**: Small items, heirlooms, ritual use
- **Labor**: Produce surplus for elite acquisition
- **Aspiration**: Emulation drives production
