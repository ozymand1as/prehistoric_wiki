# Settlement Patterns

Settlement patterns are the spatial distribution of human communities across a landscape. They reflect subsistence strategy, social organization, political structure, and environmental adaptation.

## Overview

Settlement pattern analysis (Willey, 1953) shifted archaeology from site-based to regional perspectives. The distribution, size, and hierarchy of settlements reveal the social, economic, and political organization of past societies.

## Settlement Types by Subsistence

### Foragers (Mobile)
- **Base camps**: Semi-permanent, substantial
- **Logistical camps**: Temporary, task-specific
- **Caches**: Resource storage locations
- **Pattern**: Low density, dispersed, seasonal

### Horticulturalists (Semi-Sedentary)
- **Villages**: 50–300 people, shifting (swidden) or fixed
- **Gardens**: Near village, fallow rotation
- **Pattern**: Clustered along rivers, forest edges

### Agriculturalists (Sedentary)
- **Villages**: Permanent, 100–500 people
- **Fields**: Permanent, often irrigated/terraced
- **Pattern**: Regular spacing, near water/soil

### Pastoralists (Mobile)
- **Base camps**: Winter/summer, substantial
- **Transhumance routes**: Defined corridors
- **Pattern**: Seasonal, along ecological gradients

### Urban (State)
- **Cities**: >5,000, specialized functions
- **Towns**: 1,000–5,000, administrative/craft
- **Villages**: Primary production
- **Pattern**: Hierarchical, central place

## Settlement Hierarchy

### Central Place Theory (Christaller)
- **Threshold**: Minimum market for a service
- **Range**: Maximum travel distance
- **Hexagonal hierarchy**: Efficient spatial coverage

### Archaeological Hierarchies
| Level | Population | Functions | Example |
|-------|------------|-----------|---------|
| **Primary center** | 10,000–100,000+ | Admin, religion, craft, trade | Uruk, Mohenjo-daro |
| **Secondary center** | 5,000–10,000 | Regional admin, storage | Provincial capital |
| **Tertiary center** | 1,000–5,000 | Local market, craft | Market town |
| **Village** | 100–1,000 | Primary production | Agricultural village |
| **Hamlet** | <100 | Subsistence | Farmstead cluster |

### Rank-Size Rule
- **Primacy**: Largest >> 2nd (state)
- **Log-normal**: Gradual decline (chiefdom)
- **Convex**: Even (egalitarian)

## Spatial Patterns

### Dispersed
- **Pattern**: Isolated farmsteads/homesteads
- **Cause**: Land tenure, defense, environment
- **Example**: Early medieval Europe, Jōmon

### Nucleated
- **Pattern**: Villages/towns clustered
- **Cause**: Defense, water, social, irrigation
- **Example**: Neolithic Near East, medieval Europe

### Linear
- **Pattern**: Along river, coast, ridge, road
- **Cause**: Resource corridor, transport
- **Example**: Nile Valley, LBK, Pacific coast

### Clustered
- **Pattern**: Villages in groups with center
- **Cause**: Social, economic, defensive
- **Example**: Mississippian, Maya lowlands

## Environmental Determinants

| Factor | Effect on Settlement |
|--------|---------------------|
| **Water** | Rivers, springs, wells = nucleation |
| **Soil** | Fertile = dense, permanent |
| **Defense** | Hilltops, islands, tells |
| **Resources** | Near flint, salt, metal, timber |
| **Transport** | Rivers, coasts, passes = linear |
| **Microclimate** | Aspect, elevation, frost-free |
