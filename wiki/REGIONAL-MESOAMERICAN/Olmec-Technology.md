# REGIONAL-MESOAMERICAN/Olmec-Technology

---
**Period:** Mesoamerican (Formative)
**Region(s):** Gulf Coast of Mexico (Veracruz, Tabasco)
**Culture(s):** Olmec
**Time Range:** 1500–400 BCE
**Confidence:** High
**Evidence Type:** Material

## Overview
The Olmec civilization was the earliest complex society in Mesoamerica, pioneering stone carving, rubber production, and long-distance trade networks that established foundational technological patterns for all subsequent Mesoamerican cultures. The Olmec are known for their colossal stone heads, obsidian trade, and cacao cultivation.

## History & Development
- **1500–400 BCE:** Flourishing of Olmec civilization along the Gulf Coast of Mexico
- **Rubber production:** Latex from Castilla elastica tree; the Aztec term "Olmeca" means "rubber people," reflecting this defining activity
- **Stone carving:** Development of monumental stone sculpture including colossal heads; techniques of pecking, grinding, and carving
- **Trade networks:** Long-distance exchange of obsidian, jade, cacao, and ceramics; established trade routes that connected Mesoamerican regions
- **Early pottery:** Among the first developments in ceramic production in the region

## Function & Use
- **Primary Use:** Monumental stone carving, rubber production, long-distance trade
- **Secondary Uses:** Ritual object creation, elite adornment (jade, obsidian), cacao cultivation for ritual drink and currency
- **Cultural Significance:** Colossal heads served as rulers' portraits and symbols of political authority; rubber balls used in ritual ballgame; cacao held ceremonial and economic value; jade symbolized elite status and cosmological power

## Construction & Technique
- **Stone carving:** Pecking, grinding, and carving techniques for figurines, jewelry, and colossal heads; basalt and other volcanic stone as primary medium
- **Rubber production:** Processing latex from Castilla elastica tree; mixing with other plant saps to create rubber balls
- **Lithic tools:** Obsidian tools via bifacial and unifacial flake production; prismatic blade production from polyhedral cores
- **Ceramics:** Early ceramic production techniques; utilitarian and ritual vessels

## Regional Variations
- **Gulf Coast lowlands (Veracruz/Tabasco):** Core Olmec heartland; colossal head production, rubber processing, major trade hub
- **Highland connections:** Obsidian sourced from Sierra Madre volcanic regions (Pachuca, Otumba, Zaragoza); jade from Motagua Valley (Guatemala)
