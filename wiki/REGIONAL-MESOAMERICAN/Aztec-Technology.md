# Aztec Technology

The Aztec civilization built the largest Mesoamerican city at Tenochtitlan and developed innovative agricultural, architectural, and military technologies. Their chinampas (floating gardens), monumental temple construction, and tribute empire represented the culmination of Mesoamerican technological development before European contact.

## History & Development

- **1325–1521 AD:** Aztec (Mexica) civilization centered on Tenochtitlan in the Valley of Mexico
- **Urban development:** Tenochtitlan (~125,000 people); built on island in Lake Texcoco with canals, aqueducts, causeways
- **Architecture:** Templo Mayor (100×80 m base, 60 m tall); talud-tablero style inherited from Teotihuacan
- **Agriculture:** Chinampas — floating gardens for intensive lake-based agriculture; milpa cycle in surrounding areas
- **Military technology:** Macuahuitl — wooden sword with obsidian prismatic blades; cotton-pad armor; ritual warfare
- **Metallurgy:** Recent evidence suggests Aztec developed unique metallurgy; copper, gold, silver work
- **Trade:** Tribute empire with extensive networks; traveling merchants (pochteca) acting as spies

## Function & Use

- **Primary Use:** Urban construction, agriculture (chinampas), tribute collection, ritual warfare, long-distance trade
- **Secondary Uses:** Personal adornment, metallurgy (utilitarian and ceremonial), writing (using bark paper/amate), water management
- **Cultural Significance:** Templo Mayor as religious and political center; chinampas triumph of agricultural engineering; macuahuitl symbol of warrior elite; metals associated with solar/lunar deities

## Construction & Technique

- **Urban engineering:** Island city construction on Lake Texcoco; canals, aqueducts, causeways; chinampas from layered mud and vegetation
- **Architecture:** Talud-tablero style; cantera stone veneer over tezontle; sandstone for ball court stones; limestone as primary building material
- **Stone tools:** Obsidian prismatic blades; macuahuitl; bifacial and unifacial flake tools
- **Metallurgy:** Lost-wax casting for bells; cold-working for ornaments; alloying for color properties; copper-tin bronze production
- **Agriculture:** Chinampas — raised beds in shallow lake water; milpa cycle; nixtamalization of maize

## Regional Variations

- **Tenochtitlan (Valley of Mexico):** Island city with canals; Templo Mayor; chinampas in Lake Texcoco
- **Central Mexico (broader):** Talud-tablero influence; obsidian workshop networks; Teotihuacan legacy
- **West Mexico:** Metallurgical traditions; Phase 2 copper-tin bronze sophistication
- **Michoacán (Purépecha):** Copper-tin bronze production hub; resisted Aztec domination
