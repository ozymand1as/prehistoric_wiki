# Maya Technology

The Maya developed the most sophisticated writing system and astronomical knowledge in Mesoamerica, alongside monumental architecture featuring stepped pyramids and corbelled arches. Their technological achievements in agriculture, construction, and record-keeping were sustained over more than three millennia.

## History & Development

- **2600 BCE–1697 AD:** Sustained Maya civilization across Yucatán and Central American highlands
- **Writing system:** Developed the most sophisticated logosyllabic script in Mesoamerica; used for record-keeping and ritual texts
- **Astronomy and calendar:** Advanced astronomical observation; Long Count calendar for tracking long time periods
- **Monumental architecture:** Stepped pyramids, corbelled arches, temple complexes; limestone construction
- **Agricultural systems:** Pet kot — Maya forest gardens; milpa cycle (slash-and-burn); terracing in highlands
- **Water management:** Irrigation systems; limestone cisterns (chultunes) for water storage

## Function & Use

- **Primary Use:** Monumental construction, agriculture, writing and record-keeping, astronomical observation
- **Secondary Uses:** Ritual and ceremonial practice, trade, warfare and defense
- **Cultural Significance:** Writing and calendar central to Maya cosmology and political legitimacy; stepped pyramids served as temples and symbols; forest gardens represented sustainable land management

## Construction & Technique

- **Architecture:** Limestone as primary building material; corbelled arch construction; stepped pyramid design with temple superstructures
- **Stone tools:** Obsidian tools (knives, lance points, prismatic blades, scrapers, awls); bifacial flake tools
- **Agriculture:** Milpa cycle; Pet kot forest gardens with low stone walls; terracing in highlands; irrigation
- **Processing:** Grinding stones (metates and manos); nixtamalization of maize

## Regional Variations

- **Yucatán lowlands:** Stepped pyramids at Chichén Itzá and Uxmal; corbelled arches; limestone construction; Toltec influence at Chichén Itzá
- **Guatemala highlands (Tikal, El Mirador):** Tallest pyramids; elaborate temple complexes; observatory structures (E-Group)
- **Belize (Caracol):** Extensive road networks and water management systems
- **Honduras (Copán):** Hieroglyphic stairway; sculptural traditions; metallurgical finds
- **Southern Maya area:** Earliest Maya developments; connections to Olmec trade networks

- Exact mechanisms of Maya astronomical knowledge development and transmission

- Full extent of Maya trade network reach and role of traveling merchants
- Dating precision of earliest Maya writing and relationship to Olmec script
- Sustainability and productivity of Pet kot forest gardens vs. milpa agriculture
