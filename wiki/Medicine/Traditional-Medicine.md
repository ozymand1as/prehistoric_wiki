## Overview
Traditional medicine encompasses the knowledge, skills, and practices based on indigenous theories and beliefs. It predates written history and was the primary form of medicine for most of human history, forming the foundation of healthcare across all cultures.

## History & Development
- Predates written history and represents the earliest form of medical practice
- Knowledge was passed down orally through generations
- Practiced by medicine men, shamans, and healers within communities
- Often blended physical treatments with spiritual practices
- Many modern medicines derive from traditional herbal remedies
- Traditional medicine is still practiced worldwide today

## Function & Use
- **Primary Use:** Healing illness, injury, and disease using herbal remedies, spiritual healing, and physical therapies
- **Secondary Uses:** Preventive care, health maintenance, community ritual, psychological well-being
- **Cultural Significance:** Central role in community life; healers held respected positions; practices intertwined with religious and spiritual beliefs

## Construction & Technique
- Herbal remedies prepared from locally available plants, roots, bark, and herbs
- Spiritual healing techniques including prayer, ritual, and ceremony
- Physical therapies such as massage, bone setting, and herbal compresses
- Knowledge transmitted orally through apprenticeship and generational teaching
- No standardized construction; techniques varied by practitioner and tradition

## Regional Variations
- **Africa:** Healers (sangomas, witch doctors) combining herbalism with spiritual divination
- **Asia:** Ayurvedic, Traditional Chinese Medicine, and other systems with structured herbal pharmacopeias
- **Americas:** Indigenous healers using plant-based remedies and ceremonial healing
- **Oceania:** Aboriginal healers blending herbal knowledge with Dreamtime spiritual practices
