# Traditional Medicine / Research Findings (Full)

---
**Period:** Prehistoric to Historic
**Region(s):** Global
**Culture(s):** Sumerian, Egyptian, Indian, Chinese, Greek, Roman, Indigenous
**Time Range:** 60,000 BCE - 1,000 CE
**Confidence:** Medium-High
**Evidence Type:** Material/Written/Ethnographic

## Overview
Traditional medicine encompasses the knowledge, practices, and treatments developed by various cultures throughout history, from prehistoric herbal remedies to the formalized medical systems of ancient civilizations. Research findings span paleopharmacological evidence of medicinal plant use dating to ~60,000 years ago, through the first written records of herbal prescriptions by the Sumerians (c. 3000 BCE), to the comprehensive pharmacological texts of Greece and Rome. Modern drugs continue to be derived from prehistoric herbal knowledge, including artemisinin, digitalis, quinine, and taxanes.

## History & Development
- **Paleolithic (~60,000 years ago):** Earliest evidence of medicinal plant use; paleopharmacological studies confirm herbal remedies; herbs found in Shanidar Cave; areca nut remains in Spirit Cave, Thailand
- **Prehistoric (~6,000 BCE):** Psychoactive plants (psilocybin mushrooms) used in the Sahara; plant-based treatments passed down orally
- **Sumerians (c. 3000 BCE):** First written records of herbal prescriptions on cuneiform tablets; medicinal herb use spans over 5,000 years beginning with Sumerians, Egyptians, Indians, and Chinese
- **Egyptians (c. 1600–1550 BCE):** Edwin Smith Papyrus (c. 1600 BCE) — earliest surgical text, nearly devoid of magic; Ebers Papyrus (c. 1550 BC) records 700+ compounds, mainly plant-based
- **India (1st millennium BC):** Ayurveda — Charaka and Sushruta described herbs and minerals; Sushruta credited as physician of 6th century BC; diet-based treatment, herbal remedies, spiritual elements
- **China (Han dynasty):** Shennong Bencaojing (Divine Farmer's Herb-Root Classic) — earliest Chinese materia medica; Mawangdui manuscript (168 BC) lists 52 ailment recipes; TCM roots in prehistoric era
- **Greece/Rome (c. 40–90 CE):** Dioscorides' De Materia Medica — 600+ plants, coined term "materia medica"; became basis for medieval texts

## Function & Use
- **Primary Use:** Treatment of diseases, ailments, and injuries using herbal, surgical, and spiritual methods
- **Secondary Uses:** Prevention of disease; spiritual healing and ritual practices; pain management; dental care; wound treatment
- **Cultural Significance:** Traditional medicine served as the primary healthcare system for millennia; healing practices blended physical and spiritual elements; medicine men and shamans maintained tribal health; women played a central role in gathering and dispensing plant-based remedies; ethnomedicinal knowledge was compiled by chroniclers (e.g., Spanish chroniclers of Aztec medicine)

## Construction & Technique
- **Herbal medicine:** Plant materials were the primary treatment; herbs prepared as decoctions, poultices, or ingested directly; paleopharmacological studies confirm medicinal plant use
- **Surgical techniques:** Trepanning — oldest surgical procedure (7,000–10,000 years ago); over 1,500 trephined skulls from Neolithic period; techniques evolved from crude drilling to sophisticated abrasive grinding (Maya) and cranioplasty (Andean cultures)
- **Dental care:** Earliest drilled and filled tooth (13,000 years ago, Italy, filled with bitumen, hair, plant fiber); Mehrgarh (Pakistan, c. 7000 BCE) shows dental drilling evidence
- **Bone setting:** Clay applied to broken bones; ant pincers used in the Americas to close wounds
- **Wound care:** Geophagy — eating earth/clays both internally and externally for wound treatment; learned by observing animal behavior
- **Pharmacological preparation:** Sumerians first to record herbal prescriptions on cuneiform tablets; Egyptians documented 700+ compounds

## Regional Variations
- **Sumeria:** First written herbal prescriptions on cuneiform tablets (c. 3000 BCE)
- **Egypt:** Ebers Papyrus (700+ compounds, plant-based); Edwin Smith Papyrus (earliest surgical text, minimal magic)
- **India:** Ayurveda — Charaka and Sushruta (1st millennium BC); diet-based treatment, herbal remedies, spiritual elements
- **China:** Shennong Bencaojing (earliest Chinese materia medica, Han dynasty); Mawangdui manuscript (52 ailment recipes, 168 BC); TCM roots in prehistoric shamanic practices
- **Greece/Rome:** Dioscorides' De Materia Medica (600+ plants); basis for medieval European texts
- **Northern Europe:** Birch polypore fungus used as laxative; found in Ötzi the Iceman's possessions (c. 3230 BC)
- **Peru/Andes:** Highest concentration of trepanning; sophisticated techniques avoiding the midline; cranioplasty existed
- **Mesoamerica:** Maya abrasive grinding for trepanation; Aztec ethnomedicinal knowledge compiled by Sahagún and de la Cruz
- **Africa:** Medicine men central to tribal systems; trepanning in East Africa (Bunyoro-Kitara, Kisii people)
- **Australia:** Bush medicine; Aboriginal plant use documented
- **Americas (Native American):** Navajo, Chumash, Mapuche medicine; herbal remedies passed down orally

## Uncertainties & Research Questions
- The exact nature of many prehistoric medicines remains unknown due to lack of preservation
- Cross-regional comparisons require more detailed analysis
- Ethnographic parallels need verification
- The extent of shamanic influence on early pharmacological development remains unclear
- Connections between prehistoric herbal knowledge and modern drug derivation require further study
