# Traditional Medicine in Prehistoric Times

---
**Period:** Prehistoric
**Region(s):** Global
**Culture(s):** Indigenous
**Time Range:** 300,000 BCE - 1,000 CE
**Confidence:** Medium
**Evidence Type:** Inferred

## Overview
Medicine in the time before the invention of writing. Prehistoric medicine is any use of medicine from before the invention of writing and the documented history of medicine. Because the timing of the invention of writing varies per culture and region, the term "prehistoric medicine" encompasses a wide range of time periods and dates.

## History & Development
The study of prehistoric medicine relies heavily on artifacts and human remains, and on anthropology. Previously uncontacted peoples and certain indigenous peoples who live in a traditional way have been the subject of anthropological studies in order to gain insight into both contemporary and ancient practices.

### Disease and Mortality
Some diseases and ailments were more common in prehistory than they are today; there is evidence that many people suffered from osteoarthritis, probably caused by the lifting of heavy objects which would have been a daily and necessary task in their societies. The life expectancy in prehistoric times was low, 25–40 years.

### Treatments for Diseases
**Plant materials**: Herbs such as rosemary may have been used for medical purposes by prehistoric people. Plant materials (herbs and substances derived from natural sources) were among the treatments for diseases in prehistoric cultures.

**Earth and clays**: Earths and clays may have provided prehistoric peoples with some of their first medicines. This is related to geophagy, which is extremely widespread among animals in the wild as well as among domesticated animals.

**Surgery**: Trepanning (sometimes Trephining) was a basic surgical operation in which a hole was drilled or scraped into the skull. It was carried out in prehistoric societies across the world. The first known trepanning operation was carried out c. 5000 BCE in Ensisheim, France.

**Bone setting**: An injured area was covered in clay, which then set hard so that the bone could heal properly without interference. Primarily in the Americas, the pincers of certain ant species were used to close up wounds from infection.

**Magic and medicine men**: Medicine men (also witch-doctors, shamans) maintained the health of their tribe by gathering and distributing herbs, performing minor surgical procedures, and supernatural treatments such as charms, spells, and amulets to ward off evil spirits.

### Dentistry
The earliest example of a drilled and filled in tooth dates back to 13,000 years ago in Italy where a tooth was filled with a mix of bitumen, hair and plant fiber.

## Construction & Technique
- Plant materials were gathered and used for medicinal purposes
- Earths and clays were used for wound treatment and bone setting
- Trepanning was performed using stone tools
- Knowledge was passed down orally through generations

## Regional Variations
- **Northern Europe**: Birch polypore fungus used as a laxative
- **Peru**: High concentration of trepanning practice
- **Americas**: Ant pincers used for wound closure
- **Africa**: Medicine men central figures in tribal system
