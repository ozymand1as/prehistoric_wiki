# Medicine/Herbal-Medicine

---
**Period:** Prehistoric–Present
**Region(s):** Global
**Culture(s):** All known cultures
**Time Range:** ~60,000 BCE–Present
**Confidence:** High
**Evidence Type:** Ethnographic/Written/Inferred

## Overview
Herbal medicine is the use of plants for medicinal purposes and represents the oldest form of medicine, predating written history. Plants—including herbs, roots, bark, leaves, and flowers—have been used across all cultures to treat illness and maintain health. Many modern drugs derive from plant compounds originally identified through traditional herbal knowledge.

## History & Development
- Paleolithic peoples likely used plants for medicinal purposes based on observational knowledge of animal behavior
- Traditional herbal knowledge was passed down through generations via oral tradition
- Different cultures developed distinct herbal traditions independently
- Herbal medicine was central to Traditional Chinese Medicine, Ayurveda, and indigenous healing systems
- The study of paleopharmacology examines ancient herbal medicine through archaeological evidence
- Written records of herbal remedies appear in Sumerian, Egyptian, Chinese, and Indian texts

## Function & Use
- **Primary Use:** Treatment of illness and disease using plant-derived remedies
- **Secondary Uses:** Pain relief, fever reduction, digestive aid, wound treatment, spiritual healing
- **Cultural Significance:** Herbal knowledge was often sacred, held by shamans, healers, or specialized practitioners; plants served as both medicine and ritual objects

## Construction & Technique
- Collection of plant parts (roots, bark, leaves, flowers, seeds) from wild or cultivated sources
- Preparation methods include decoction (boiling), infusion (steeping), poultice (crushed plant applied topically), and drying for storage
- Knowledge of dosage, preparation, and plant identification required extensive empirical observation and training
- Techniques varied by culture but shared core principles of extracting active compounds from plant material

## Regional Variations
- **Traditional Chinese Medicine:** Uses hundreds of plant species in structured formulas; ginseng, astragalus, and licorice root are foundational herbs
- **Ayurveda:** Systematized herbal pharmacology with categorization by taste, potency, and effect on bodily humors; turmeric, neem, and ashwagandha are key plants
- **Indigenous Healing:** Diverse traditions worldwide using locally available flora; knowledge often embedded in spiritual and ceremonial practice
- **European Folk Medicine:** Herbal remedies passed through household traditions; chamomile, garlic, and ginger were widely used
