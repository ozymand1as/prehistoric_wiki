# Rice Agriculture

Rice (Oryza sativa) is one of the world's most important staple crops, domesticated independently in China (O. sativa japonica) and India (O. sativa indica). Its cultivation shaped the demography, economy, and culture of monsoon Asia for millennia. [1]

## Overview

Rice agriculture represents one of humanity's most sophisticated agricultural achievements. From its origins in the Yangtze and Ganges valleys, rice cultivation spread across Asia, transforming landscapes, societies, and diets. Its intensive cultivation requirements drove technological innovation, social organization, and state formation.

## Domestication

### Independent Centers

#### China (Yangtze Valley) — Oryza sativa japonica
- **Wild ancestor**: Oryza rufipogon
- **Earliest evidence**: Shangshan culture (~9,000 BCE) — wild rice management [2]
- **Domesticated rice**: Hemudu culture (~7,000–5,500 BCE) — morphological changes (non-shattering, larger grains)
- **Key sites**: Shangshan, Hemudu, Tianluoshan, Liangzhu

#### India (Ganges Valley) — Oryza sativa indica
- **Wild ancestor**: Oryza rufipogon / Oryza nivara [3]
- **Earliest evidence**: Lahuradewa (~7,000 BCE) — early cultivation
- **Domesticated rice**: Mahagara, Koldihwa (~5,000–4,000 BCE)
- **Key sites**: Lahuradewa, Mahagara, Koldihwa, Senuwar

#### Southeast Asia — Possible third center
- **Possibility**: Independent domestication or early introgression [4]
- **Sites**: Khok Phanom Di (Thailand), An Son (Vietnam) — ~4,000–3,000 BCE

### Domestication Syndrome (Morphological Changes)
| Trait | Wild | Domesticated |
|-------|------|--------------|
| **Shattering** | Shatters at maturity | Non-shattering (tough rachis) |
| **Grain size** | Small, slender | Larger, plumper |
| **Dormancy** | Strong dormancy | Reduced/no dormancy |
| **Plant architecture** | Prostrate, spreading | Erect, compact |
| **Tillering** | Many, asynchronous | Fewer, synchronous |
| **Awns** | Long, barbed | Short or absent |

## Cultivation Systems

### Wet Rice (Paddy / Irrigated)
- **Water management**: Bunded fields, controlled flooding
- **Labor**: High (transplanting, weeding, water control)
- **Yield**: High (4–8 t/ha traditional, 8–12+ modern)
- **Regions**: River deltas, alluvial plains, terraced hills
- **History**: Hemudu (early), Liangzhu (elaborate), historic China/Japan/Korea

### Dry Rice (Upland / Rainfed)
- **Water**: Rainfall only, no standing water
- **Labor**: Lower (direct seeding)
- **Yield**: Lower (1–3 t/ha)
- **Regions**: Hill slopes, uplands, marginal areas
- **Varieties**: Aus (aus ecotype), drought-tolerant

### Deepwater / Floating Rice
- **Water**: 1–5+ meters depth
- **Adaptation**: Rapid elongation, floating leaves
- **Regions**: Bangladesh, Mekong Delta, South Sulawesi

### Terraced Rice
- **Engineering**: Stone/mud walls, irrigation channels
- **Regions**: Himalayan foothills, Yunnan, Philippines (Ifugao), Japan
- **Labor**: Extreme initial investment, sustainable long-term

## Processing Technology

### Threshing
- **Traditional**: Beating on rocks, trampling by animals
- **Tools**: Flail, pedal thresher (later)

### Winnowing
- **Method**: Tossing in wind, winnowing baskets
- **Separation**: Grain from chaff/straw

### Milling
| Stage | Traditional | Modern |
|-------|-------------|--------|
| **Dehusking** | Mortar & pestle, hand huller | Rubber roll huller |
| **Whitening** | Hand pounding | Abrasive/ friction whitener |
| **Polishing** | Not typical | Cone polisher, mist polisher |
| **Byproducts** | Bran, germ, broken rice | Bran oil, rice flour |

### Parboiling (India, Sri Lanka, parts of SE Asia)
- **Process**: Soak → steam → dry → mill
- **Benefits**: Nutrient retention, harder grain, less breakage

## Agricultural Calendar (Monsoon Asia)

| Season | Activity | Timing |
|--------|----------|--------|
| **Pre-monsoon** | Land prep, nursery sowing | Apr–May |
| **Monsoon (Kharif)** | Transplanting, weeding | Jun–Sep |
| **Post-monsoon** | Harvest, threshing | Oct–Nov |
| **Dry season (Rabi)** | Second crop (irrigated) | Dec–Mar |

## Social & Economic Impact

### Labor Organization
- **Reciprocal labor**: Work parties (gotong royong, bayanihan)
- **Tenancy**: Sharecropping (50/50 or fixed rent)
- **Gender**: Women dominate transplanting, weeding, processing

### State & Taxation
- **Land surveys**: Cadastral registers (China, Japan, Vietnam, Java)
- **Tax in kind**: Rice as revenue (tang, koku, cavan)
- **Granaries**: State reserves for famine relief, army supply

### Ritual & Cosmology
- **Rice deities**: Dewi Sri (Java), Inari (Japan), Mae Phosop (Thailand)
- **Rituals**: Planting ceremonies, harvest festivals, first fruits
- **Ancestral fields**: Royal ploughing ceremonies

## Archaeobotanical Evidence

### Macro-remains
- **Grains**: Charred, waterlogged, impressions
- **Spikelet bases**: Shattering vs non-shattering
- **Husk phytoliths**: Double-peaked (rice), bulliform cells

### Micro-remains
- **Phytoliths**: Bulliform, double-peaked, keystone
- **Starch grains**: Cooking residues on pottery
- **Pollen**: Paddy field sediments

### Biomolecular
- **Ancient DNA**: Domestication genes (sh4, PROG1)
- **Lipid biomarkers**: Rice lipids in pottery
- **Isotopes**: δ¹³C (C3 pathway), δ¹⁵N (manuring)
