**Culture(s):** Ancient

**Confidence:** Medium

## Overview
Agriculture is the practice of cultivating plants and livestock. In early societies, the transition from foraging to agriculture marked a fundamental shift in human society, enabling population growth and complex social structures.

## History & Development
The Neolithic Revolution (~12,000–6,000 BCE) triggered a paradigm shift from foraging to agriculture. Key technological innovations included pottery firing, domestic architecture, and irrigation systems. Archaeological records indicate that these technologies did not emerge simultaneously but diffused regionally.

## Function & Use
- Food production and storage
- Population growth support
- Social and economic organization
- Cultural and religious significance

## Construction & Technique
- **Crop selection**: Wild plant varieties selected for desirable traits
- **Domestication**: Plants bred over generations for larger yield and better quality
- **Tools**: Stone tools for planting, harvesting, and processing
- **Irrigation**: Water management systems for crop cultivation
- **Storage**: Granaries and storage pits for preservation

## Regional Variations
- **Fertile Crescent**: Wheat, barley, and legumes
- **Yangtze River Valley**: Rice cultivation
- **Mesoamerica**: Maize, beans, and squash
- **Andes**: Quinoa and tubers
