# Crop Domestication

---
**Period:** Neolithic Revolution (12,000 BCE – 6,000 BCE)
**Region(s):** Near East, China, New World Tropics, Southwest Asia
**Culture(s:** Neolithic cultures worldwide)
**Time Range:** 12,000 BCE – 6,000 BCE
**Confidence:** High
**Evidence Type:** Material, Archaeological, Written

## Overview
The Neolithic Revolution (or First Agricultural Revolution) marked a global transition from nomadic hunter-gatherer lifestyles to sedentary agricultural societies. This shift coincided with the end of the Pleistocene epoch (the last ice age) and the beginning of the Holocene. Archaeological data indicate that the food-producing domestication of some types of wild animals and plants happened independently in separate locations worldwide, starting in Mesopotamia after the end of the last Ice Age, around 11,700 years ago.

## History & Development

### Timeline and Origins
**Near East (Fertile Crescent):**
- Process generally estimated to have begun between 12,000 and 10,000 years ago
- Some evidence suggests transitions as early as 14,500–12,000 BP
- Climate became warmer, and vast areas were flooded due to relatively sudden rise in sea levels
- Between 12,000 and 6,000 BC, coastline was thrust inland by up to 1,000 km

**Independent Origins:**
- Domestication occurred independently in several major world areas:
  - **China:** Early domestication of millet and rice
  - **New World tropics:** Independent domestication of maize, beans, squash
  - **Southwest Asia:** Founder crops formed basis of early agricultural economies

### The Process of Domestication
Early farmers initially selected plants with desirable characteristics to use as seeds for future generations. Over time, this evolved from simple selection to deliberate hybridization.

**Founder Crops in Southwest Asia:**
- **Cereals:** Emmer wheat, einkorn wheat, and barley
- **Pulses:** Lentils, peas, chickpeas, and bitter vetch
- **Other:** Flax

**Plant vs. Animal Timing:**
- Older research suggested a 1,500-year gap between domestication of plants and animals
- More recent evidence indicates both likely occurred roughly at the same time
- Management of wild ancestors dating back to at least 11,500 cal BP

### Societal Impact
The transition led to:
- Sedentary settlements
- Population growth
- Establishment of cross-group organizations
- Increased social differentiation

**Costs and Benefits:**
- **Hard labor:** Introduction of agriculture implies increase in hard labor
- **Food quality:** Significant loss of access to high-quality food compared to what was previously available through hunting and foraging
- **Calorie-rich crops:** Production of calorie-rich crops allowed humans to invest their efforts in other activities
- **Necessary for civilization:** Ultimately necessary to the rise of modern civilization

**Critical Perspectives:**
- Minority of scientists take critical stance toward optimistic view
- Consider that since dawn of agriculture, reciprocal relationship may have been initiated whereby more and more people need to be fed by ever larger areas of cultivated land
- Process must be stabilized at level that prevents collapse of global ecosystems

### Mesopotamian Development
**Earliest Written Records:** Dated to c. 6,500 BP, originate from Sumerian civilization, which reached the Bronze Age and emerged also in the Fertile Crescent.

**Early Writing:** Initially, records exclusively documented quantities of foodstuffs to be delivered, often signed with impressions of cylinder seals. Over millennia, these simple signs were developed into complex cuneiform script, enabling both recording of myths and beginning of more or less realistic approach to historiography.

**Sumerian Myths:** The beginning of Mesopotamia's task-specific transformation into fertile garden landscape described in Atra-Hasis epic, also the list of rulers from allegedly some millennia before to after the Flood appearing in this context.

### Cross-Group Organizations
Prehistoric hunter-gatherers had different subsistence requirements and lifestyles from agriculturalists. They lived in relatively small groups that were mostly very mobile (migratory), built only temporary shelters, and had limited contact with foreign communities.

**Political Alliances:**
- Self-sufficient economy of such groups explained their mutual competition for available resources
- Territorial conflicts were not uncommon in human prehistory
- Aristotle already assumed that humans naturally possessed ability to form political alliances
- Highly evolved reasoning allowed Neolithic hunter-gatherer groups to cooperate with foreign communities based on understanding of advantages of such measures
- This cooperation occurred "much earlier than science had previously believed"

**Cross-Group Organization:**
- At least two to three teams of hunter-gatherers must come together to form cross-group organization
- Based on contract in which terms for peaceful sharing of territory (its resources) are agreed
- K. Schmidt assumes this cultural adaptation (politics) was already introduced at outset of Neolithic period, laying foundations for later first city-states

### Neolithic Package
The Neolithic Revolution encompassed much more than just introduction of various food production techniques:
- Cultivating large areas of land
- Erecting monumental works of art such as those at Göbekli Tepe
- Required level of labour that small groups of nomadic hunter-gatherers who had hitherto dominated human prehistory could hardly have achieved on their own

**Neolithic Package Elements:**
- Earliest political alliances
- Division of labour
- Emergence of centralised administrations and specialised crafts
- Hierarchical ideologies
- Expansion of trade and military operations
- Development of depersonalised systems of knowledge (e.g., writing)
- Aggregation of property and architecture in densely populated settlements
- Monumental art primarily proclaiming power of founders, depicting them as gods

### Neolithisation
According to current research, first Neolithic Revolution began in Mesopotamia about 11,600 years ago. From there, it expanded via migration into immediately adjacent regions, displacing and/or assimilating local hunter-gatherer cultures.

**European Neolithisation:**
- Reaching northern Europe around 5500 BCE
- Cross-group organisations founded by egalitarian communities may have existed there even before introduction of agriculture
- Archaeologists like K. Schmidt and C. Renfrew have drawn conclusions from their cognitive-archaeological calculations of man-hours required for construction of desert kites (kilometre-long traps for catching entire wild animal herds) or megalithic buildings above specified scale
- Example: Stonehenge in northern England

### Shift from Egalitarian to Hierarchical Relationships
Need to plan and coordinate agricultural communities' food production, manpower, and resource allocation encouraged division of labour, gradually leading to emergence of specialised professions within increasingly complex societies.

**Migration and Contact:**
- Migration, military conquests, diplomacy, and trade in surplus goods brought agrarian cultures into contact with outsiders
- Cultures that were in some cases very strange encountered each other
- Separately developed traditions, languages, and narratives about world's creation became mixed
- Knowledge was exchanged
- Thinkers attempted to create uniform cosmogonies or metaphysical systems contributing to rise of civilisations, philosophy, and technological advancements

**Hierarchies:**
- Traditionally entrenched hierarchies between superior and inferior groups are difficult to assume among egalitarian social associations of hunter-gatherers who founded first politically organisations
- Proto-states, primordial polis emerged with distinct pantheons
- Sumerian myths describe gods' attempt to destroy humanity, their wayward creation
- Epic Atra-Hasis tells of introduction of slavery in Eden – humans reduced to 'working cattle' as ultimate expression of hierarchical relationship between mentally powerless creatures and intellectually superior groups of gods

**Power Imbalance:**
- Increasingly specialized division into governing "thinkers" and executing "workers" over time can initiate ever more power imbalance
- Phenomenon of documented shifts in social relations (including supportive ideologies) linked to emergence and growth of initially simple cross-group organisations into modern nations

## Function & Use

### Agricultural Technologies
**Pottery:**
- Introduced in Neolithic period
- Used for cooking, food storage, and processing
- Ceramic vessels used for cooking since antiquity due to excellent thermal retention and even heat distribution
- Porous earthenware absorbs and releases moisture as steam to tenderise ingredients while preserving flavours
- Vitrified stoneware provides dense, non-reactive surface that resists acidic interaction

**Polished Stone Tools:**
- Change from round to rectangular dwellings
- Polished stone tools developed alongside agriculture
- Used for various agricultural tasks including land clearing and processing

**Irrigation:**
- Cultivation of certain crops required irrigation systems
- Natural environment radically altered through animal breeding, deforestation, cultivation of certain crops, and irrigation
- Irrigation enabled agricultural intensification and surplus production

### Monumental Architecture
**Göbekli Tepe:**
- Among oldest known large-scale art in human history
- Erected between approximately 9,500 BP and at least 8,000 BP in northern Mesopotamia
- Numerous circular formations consisting of group of about eleven megalithic pillars
- Each monument consists of artificial hill, so-called tell
- Erected over course of at least 1,500 years (approx. 50 generations) layer by layer like tower towards heaven
- Oldest representations form its fundament
- Pillars interpreted as symbolic male figures

**Stonehenge:**
- Monument underwent no further constructive changes since about 1400 BC
- Traces of deliberate destruction, as archaeology often records when foreign cultures displaces previous ones
- Around same time, custom of communal burial came to an end
- Individual tombs for single rulers began to appear (chieftains, priest-kings, such as pharaohs in their pyramids)
- Witness to distinctly non-egalitarian power hierarchy
- Megalith culture in south of island reached Bronze Age around 3000 BC
- Evidence: tin mine in Cornwall and proven trade of its metal as far as Aegean

### Social Organization
**Division of Labour:**
- Need to plan and coordinate agricultural communities' food production, manpower, and resource allocation encouraged division of labour
- Gradually led to emergence of specialised professions within increasingly complex societies
- Migration, military conquests, diplomacy, and trade in surplus goods brought agrarian cultures into contact with outsiders

**Centralised Administrations:**
- Emergence of centralised administrations and specialised crafts
- Hierarchical ideologies developed
- Expansion of trade and military operations
- Development of depersonalised systems of knowledge (e.g., writing)
- Aggregation of property and architecture in densely populated settlements

**City-States:**
- Most successful among initially simple communities grew into city-states
- Examples: Shuruppak mentioned in humanity's oldest written documents
- These societies radically altered their natural environment

### Trade and Military Operations
**Trade:**
- Expansion of trade brought diverse cultures into contact
- Knowledge exchange facilitated technological and cultural diffusion
- Trade networks developed for agricultural products, tools, and raw materials

**Military Operations:**
- Military conquests brought agrarian cultures into contact with outsiders
- Territorial conflicts documented by prehistoric battle scenes
- Armed conflicts between parties of hunters illustrated behavior when sources of life were severely scarce

## Regional Variations

### Mesopotamia (Near East)
**Timeline:** First Neolithic Revolution began about 11,600 years ago
**Founder Crops:** Emmer wheat, einkorn wheat, barley, lentils, peas, chickpeas, bitter vetch, flax
**Technology:** Pottery, polished stone tools, irrigation systems
**Architecture:** Göbekli Tepe, monumental circular formations
**Writing:** Cuneiform script developed from food quantity records
**Organization:** City-states emerged, centralized administrations

### China
**Timeline:** Early domestication during Neolithic period
**Crops:** Millet, rice
**Technology:** Agricultural tools, irrigation
**Architecture:** Megalithic structures, early state formation
**Organization:** Complex societies, hierarchical structures

### New World (Americas)
**Timeline:** Neolithizations happened in last 300 years in connection with discovery and colonization of Australia, Americas, and polar regions
**Crops:** Maize, beans, squash, tubers
**Organization:** Hunter-gatherers often parallel created simplest of gardens
**Impact:** Communities wiped out or introduced to achievements of modern civilization within few decades

### Europe
**Timeline:** Reaching northern Europe around 5500 BCE
**Architecture:** Stonehenge, megalithic monuments
**Organization:** Cross-group organisations founded by egalitarian communities may have existed before introduction of agriculture
**Development:** Neolithisation reached northern Europe around 5500 BCE

### Australia
**Timeline:** Neolithizations in last 300 years
**Organization:** Communities living as Stone Age hunter-gatherers
**Gardens:** Women often parallel created simplest of gardens
**Impact:** Wiped out or introduced to achievements of modern civilization within few decades

## Uncertainties & Research Questions
- Reasons why human populations adopted agriculture roughly 12,000 years ago remain "imperfectly understood"
- Exact timing of plant vs. animal domestication in different regions is uncertain
- How early experimental gardening was used and its functional significance unclear
- Causality: Whether agriculture preceded politics or vice versa remains debated
- Whether introduction of agriculture by already existing political organisation was necessary or not
