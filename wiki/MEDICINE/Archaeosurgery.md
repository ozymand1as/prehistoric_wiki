# Archaeosurgery

Archaeosurgery is the study of surgical practices in archaeological contexts, providing insight into prehistoric medical knowledge and beliefs. Trepanning (trepanation) is the oldest known surgical procedure, with evidence dating to 7,000–10,000 years ago and over 1,500 trephined skulls found worldwide.


## Overview

Archaeosurgery is the study of surgical practices in archaeological contexts, providing insight into prehistoric medical knowledge and beliefs. Trepanning (trepanation) is the oldest known surgical procedure, with evidence dating to 7,000–10,000 years ago and over 1,500 trephined skulls found worldwide.
## History & Development

- Earliest evidence of trepanning dates to 7,000–10,000 years ago during the Neolithic period
- Over 1,500 trephined skulls have been recovered from sites across Europe, Asia, Africa, and the Americas
- The practice persisted through the Bronze Age, Iron Age, and into historical periods in some regions
- Surgical techniques evolved over time, with improved tools and methods increasing survival rates in later eras

## Function & Use

- **Primary Use:** Treatment of headaches, epilepsy, skull fractures, and intracranial pressure
- **Secondary Uses:** Release of evil spirits and other spiritual purposes
- **Cultural Significance:** Trepanning served both medical and ritualistic functions, reflecting the intersection of early medicine and spiritual belief systems

## Construction & Technique

- Trepanning involved drilling or scraping a hole into the skull using stone, metal, or bone tools
- Techniques included circular grooving, scraping, and punching to remove a section of the cranium
- Bone setting, wound suturing, and amputation were also practiced as other surgical procedures
- Survival rates varied by era and region, with evidence of healed trepanation holes indicating successful procedures

## Regional Variations

- **Europe:** Extensive trepanning evidence from Neolithic and Bronze Age sites; varied techniques across regions
- **Mesoamerica:** Pre-Columbian cultures practiced trepanning with high survival rates, often for ritualistic purposes
- **Africa:** Archaeological finds show diverse surgical practices including trepanning and wound care across different cultures and periods
