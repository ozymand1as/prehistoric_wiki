## Overview

Herbal medicine is the use of plants for medicinal purposes and represents the oldest form of medicine, predating written history. Plants—including herbs, roots, bark, leaves, and flowers—have been used across all cultures to treat illness and maintain health. Many modern drugs derive from plant compounds originally identified through traditional herbal knowledge.
## History & Development

- **Sumerians (c. 3000 BCE):** First written records of herbal prescriptions on cuneiform tablets [2]
- **Egyptians (c. 1600–1550 BCE):** Edwin Smith Papyrus (c. 1600 BCE) — earliest surgical text; Ebers Papyrus (c. 1550 BC) records 700+ compounds
- **India (1st millennium BC):** Ayurveda — Charaka and Sushruta described herbs and minerals
- **China (Han dynasty):** Shennong Bencaojing (Divine Farmer's Herb-Root Classic) — earliest Chinese materia medica
- **Greece/Rome (c. 40–90 CE):** Dioscorides' De Materia Medica — 600+ plants
- **Paleolithic (~60,000 years ago):** Earliest evidence of medicinal plant use confirmed by paleopharmacological studies [3]
- **Traditional Systems:** Ayurveda, Traditional Chinese Medicine, and indigenous healing systems

## Function & Use

- **Primary Use:** Treatment of illness and disease using plant-derived remedies
- **Secondary Uses:** Pain relief, fever reduction, digestive aid, wound treatment, spiritual healing
- **Cultural Significance:** Herbal knowledge was often sacred, held by shamans, healers, or specialized practitioners; plants served as both medicine and ritual objects

## Construction & Technique

- Collection of plant parts (roots, bark, leaves, flowers, seeds) from wild or cultivated sources
- Preparation methods include decoction (boiling), infusion (steeping), poultice (crushed plant applied topically), and drying for storage
- Knowledge of dosage, preparation, and plant identification required extensive empirical observation
- Techniques varied by culture but shared core principles of extracting active compounds

## Regional Variations

- **Traditional Chinese Medicine:** Uses hundreds of plant species in structured formulas; foundations include ginseng, astragalus, and licorice root
- **Ayurveda:** Systematized herbal pharmacology with categorization by taste, potency, and effect; key plants include turmeric, neem, and ashwagandha
- **Indigenous Healing:** Diverse traditions worldwide using locally available flora; knowledge embedded in spiritual and ceremonial practice
- **European Folk Medicine:** Herbal remedies passed through household traditions; common plants include chamomile, garlic, and ginger
- **Africa:** Rich tradition of herbal remedies; plant knowledge preserved through oral tradition and ritual practices
